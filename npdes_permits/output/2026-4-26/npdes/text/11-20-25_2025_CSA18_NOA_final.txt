2. WASTEWATER SYSTEM OPERATION SUMMARY
2.1. The San Luis Obispo County Service Area 18, Country Club Estates
Reclamation Facility ( CSA-18) produces disinfected secondary -23 recycled
water. The CSA -18 facility and collection system is owned and operated by the
County of San Luis Obispo. The County of San Luis Obispo delivers recycled
water to the San Luis Obispo Country Club (Country C lub) for use as irrigation
on the restricted golf course. The Country Club historically held a recycled
water user permit, Order R3- 2003- 0005, however, the County of San Luis
Obispo and Country Club have opted to combine permitting under one permit. The Country Club, along with any additional recycled water users, must enter
into a User Agreement with the County of San Luis Obispo. In addition, the
County of San Luis Obispo is required to update the title 22 Engineering Report
to incorporate all new users.
2.2. CSA-18 wastewater is primarily of domestic origin. Total dissolved solids (TDS),
chloride, and sodium are known problem constituents. In 1993, San Luis
Obispo County Ordinance 2597 banned the use of water softening systems or devices which cause high concentration of salts to be discharged into the public sewer at CSA -18. The ordinance establishes a supplemental water quality
service cha rge for any buildings and lots that do not comply with this measure.
2.3. The treatment plant process consists of Aeration Ponds 1 and 2 which have
aerators and can be operated individually in parallel or together in series with a detention time of five or more days in each aeration pond. The operator typically runs the aeration ponds in series. After aeration, wastewater flows to Settling
Ponds 1, 2, and 3 which are operated in parallel with a detention time of one or more days. One settling pond can alternatively be used for sludge storage when not being used for settling operations.
