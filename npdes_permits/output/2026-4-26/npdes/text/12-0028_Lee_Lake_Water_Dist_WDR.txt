FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment or Controls 
1. Service Area 
The Discharger's service area encompasses approximately 6,755 acres within 
Riverside County, much of which is undeveloped. The Discharger currently provides 
sewerage services to approximately 450 acres and a population of 9,500. The facility 
receives and treats domestic wastewater generated within the Discharger's service 
area. The majority of the wastewater is generated from residential areas. 
2. Lee Lake Water Reclamation Facility Design Characteristics 
Current plant design capacity is 1.58 million gallons per day average dry weather 
flow of tertiary treatment. 
The wastewater treatment process consists of screening, grit removal, seven 
sequencing batch reactors (activated sludge/secondary clarification), flow 
equalization, rapid mix/flocculation, filtration, chlorination and dechlorination. The 
Facility produces tertiary treated water that complies with requirements established

3. Biosolids Handling Practices 
Waste activated sludge is conveyed to three aerobic digesters and dewatered prior 
to disposal. Digested sludge is dewatered with a centrifuge and the sludge cake 
(Class B Biosolids) is delivered directly to a local composting facility. Filtrate from the 
dewatering process is returned to the headworks of the treatment facility. The 
Facility can also utilize on-site lined drying beds as a secondary method of 
dewatering. 
4. Recycled Water Use 
Tertiary treated recycled water is currently used for landscape irrigation within the 
16-acre treatment plant site, as well as for irrigation of a nearby golf course. 
5. Land Disposal 
Undisinfected secondary treated wastewater may be discharged to an unlined 
storage/percolation basin located in the northwest portion of the Facility. A hydro­
geological study was completed and submitted on November 9, 2010, that 
demonstrates that this discharge will not adversely affect the beneficial uses of the 
down gradient surface or ground waters. 
6. Discharge Priority 
Prior to the construction of the unlined pond, excess recycled water that could not be 
used for irrigation was discharged to Temescal Creek. With the pond, the new 
priority of effluent discharges is first to irrigation, second to percolation, and third to 
discharge to Temescal Creek. 
B. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters 
1. Discharge Point to Surface Water 
Tertiary treated wastewater is discharged via Discharge Point (DP) 001 to Reach 2 
ofTemescal Creek. Historically, the discharges to Temescal Creek were intermittent 
because about 50% of the recycled water is use for irrigation. Future discharges will 
be much more infrequent, as excess recycled water will normally be discharged to 
the unlined percolation/storage basin.
