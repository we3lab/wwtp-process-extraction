 1.2. The Salinas River is the nearest surface water to the Meadowbrook WWTF
disposal site. The river is approximately 200 feet from the northern edge of the
disposal area, 300 feet from the eastern edge of the disposal area, and 250 feet from the southern edge of the disposal area. Paso Robles Creek is

flood plain. First -encountered groundwater is approximately 11.5 - 15 feet
below ground surface.

2.2. The Wastewater System is comprised of an advanced integrated
wastewater pond system (pond system) and can treat 600,000 gallons per day
to a secondary treatment level. Pond systems are typically comprised of
advanced facultative ponds with an anaerobic pit, digester pit, and aerated
zone (primary treatment) , secondary facultative ponds with digester pits and
aerated zone (secondary treatment) , secondary ponds backflow to primary
ponds, and maturation /settling ponds (polishing) . In the advanced facultative
ponds (primary treatment), there is an anaerobic and aerobic zone. The
Meadowbrook WWTF uses two treatment trains (1 and 2) for primary and
secondary treatment. Flow is mixed following primary and secondary treatment
for final polishing in three maturation ponds in series. The Wastewater System
consists of the treatment technologies and treatment capabilities shown in

the General Permit and this notice of applicability. Templeton CSD must comply
with the wastewater specific limitations specified in Table 6 through Table 8.
Templeton CSD land applies treated wastewater ; therefore, the organic loading
limitations specified in Table 9 apply. As of the date of this Notice of
Applicability, Templeton CSD is preparing a Wastewater Treatment Master
Plan which will evaluat e the capacity of the existing treatment and disposal
systems and may inform future operational or infrastructure improvements.
