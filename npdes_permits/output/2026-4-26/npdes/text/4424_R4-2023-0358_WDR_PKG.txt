FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Description of Wastewater  and Biosolids Treatment and Controls
2.1.1. The Burbank WRP  serves an estimated population of 103,340  people  and is 
connected to  the Cit y of Los Angeles ’s integrated network of facilities, kn own as the 
Hyperion Treatment System , which includes the wastewater collection system , the 
Hyperion Water Reclamation Plant  and the three  upstream water reclamation  plants  
(including this POTW (Burbank WRP) , Los Angeles -Glendale WRP, and D.C. 
Tillman  WRP) . The Hyperion Treat ment  System collects, treats, and processes 
municipal wastewater from domestic, commercial, and industrial sources from the 
entire city (except the Terminal Island Service Area surrounding the Los Angeles 
Harbor area) and fr om a number of other cities and agencies under contractual 
agreements, including the City of San Fernando , the Las Virgenes Municipal Water 
District , and the Triunfo Canyon Sanitation District. This system  allows biosolids, 
solids, and excess flows to be d iverted from the upstream treatment plants to the 
Hyperion Water Reclamation  Plant  for treatment and disposa l. All solids removed 
from the Burbank WRP  treatment process are returned untreated to the North 
Outfall Sewer (NOS), for downstream treatment at th e Hyperion Water Reclamation  
Plant.  If the Burbank WRP  has operational problems or need s to shut down,

2.1.2. The Dis charger  occasionally evaluates its local limits for industrial and commercial 
sources that discharge to the Burbank WRP  to determine if local limits  are sufficient 
to prevent pass -through or interference at the Burbank WRP and Hyperion WRP, to 
comply with permit limitations, to comply with recycled water and biosolid reuse  
requirements , and to ensure worker safety and public health. The Discharger 
conduct ed a local limits evaluation after the 2017 NPDES permit renewal , as 
required , and modified its Sewer Use Ordinance in the Burbank Municipal Code on 
December 17, 2019  to include a statement specifying that the local limits are 
instantaneous limitations.  This change to the SUO was not considered a substantial 
change to the approved pretreatment program, so it did not need to un dergo a 
separate public review process. Accordingly, the SUO went into effect 45 days later.  
In accordance with 40 CFR § 122.44(j)(2)(ii), the Discharger is required to submit a 
written technical evaluation of the need to revise local limits following the effective 
date of this Order.
2.1.3. The treatment system at the Burbank WRP  consists of  bar screen removal of large 
solids , flow equalization, primary sedimentation, nitrification and denitrification 
(NDN) activated sludge biological treatment, secondary clarification, coagulation, 
single media and deep bed sand filtration , disinfection by chlorination with the 
addition of ammonium hydroxide, and dechlorination. Since the Burbank WRP does 
not process its own solids, s olids from the Facility are returned to  the collection 
system for processing at the Hyperion Water Reclamation  Plant. Solids returned to 
the sewer consist of screenings, primary and secondary sludge skimmings, and filter 
backwash.  Attachment C 1 includes a process flow diagram  of the Burbank WRP .
2.1.4  In order to achieve compliance with the ammonia water quality objectives (WQOs) 
specified in the Water Quality Control Plan for the Los Angeles Region (Basin Plan), 
the Discharger  installed a n NDN  process , the Enhanced Modified Ludzack -Ettinger 
(eMLE) Process, in June  2007.  Since the installation of t his additional treatment , the 
Discharger  has been able  to meet ammonia final effluent limitations in the existing 
Order.
2.2. Discharge P oints and Receiving  Waters
2.2.1.  The Burbank WRP  discharges tertiary -treated wastewater to the Burbank Western 
Channel , a water of the United States, tributary to the Los Angeles River, upstream 
of the Estuary. Treated effluent is discharged from the Facility to surface waters at 
Discharge Point 002.
Discharge Point 002 : Discharge to Burbank Western Channel near Burbank 
Boulevard (approximate coordinates: Latitude 34.182778, Longitude -118.316111).
Former Discharge Point 001 was abandoned on June 14, 2005 and will not be used 
in the future .

Angeles River downstream of the discharge point are the Burbank WRP  and oth er

2.2.3.  The Los Angeles County Flood Control District channelized portions of the Los 
Angeles River to convey and control floodwater, and to prevent damage to homes 
located adjacent to the river . Although not its main purpose, the Los Angeles River 
conveys treated w astewater along with floodwater, and urban runoff. The Los 
Angeles River is unlined further downstream of its confluence with the Burbank 
Western Channel, in what is known as the Glendale Narrows. Groundwater recharge 
occurs incidentally in these unlined a reas of the Los Angeles River. At times when 
the groundwater table is high, groundwater rises and contributes flow to the Los 
Angeles River. Natural springs feed the river and support willows, sycamores, and 
cottonwood trees. South of the Glendale Narrows,  the Los Angeles River is 
concrete -lined down to Willow Street, in Long Beach .
2.2.4.  The Los Angeles River Watershed is one of the largest in the Los Angeles Region. It 
also has some of the most diverse land use patterns. Approximately 324 square 
miles of  the watershed are covered by forest or open space land including the area 
near the headwaters which lie in the Santa Monica, Santa Susana, and San Gabriel 
Mountains. The rest of the watershed is highly developed. The river flows through 
the San Fernando V alley past heavily developed residential and commercial areas.  
From the Arroyo Seco, north of downtown Los Angeles, to the confluence with the 
Rio Hondo, the river flows through industrial and commercial areas and is bordered 
by railyards, freeways, and m ajor commercial and government buildings. From the 
Rio Hondo to the Pacific Ocean, the river flows through industrial, residential, and 
commercial areas, including major refineries and petroleum products storage 
facilities, major freeways, rail lines, and railyards serving the Ports of Los Angeles 
and Long Beach.

Total coliform : The Burbank WRP effluent exceeded the effluent limitation s for total 
coliform five times in March 2018  and once in April 2018 . Although t he root cause was 
not determined, operations and maintenance ( O&M ) staff planned, scheduled, and 
executed the cleaning of the B urbank WRP’s Chlorine Contact Tank No. 3 (CCT3), 
where both , recycled water is pumped to the City of Burbank and bacteria compliance 
samples are collected . An elevated chlorine residual set point was maintained following 
cleaning as a preventive measure . The total coliform exceedance in April 2018 was 
related to th e low influent flow at the headworks  caused by failure of  the Beachwood 
Pump Station. Consequently, low flows in the chlorine contact chamber caused sodium 
bisulfite (SBS) to backflow and reduce d chlorine levels in the chlorine contact chamber. 
A plan was developed and put in place to coordinate the recycled water pumping rate at 
the time of coliform testing, to allow increased flow in the  chlorine contact  chamber.

Chronic Toxicity.  In June  2018, the effluent exceeded the monthly median effluent 
limitation due to the sodium bisulfite issue that caused coliform exceedances. A 
contractor  was retained to upgrade the sodium bisulfite injection control system, which 
included a Compound Loop Control on the chemical feed system . Accelerated testing 
was condu cted from July 3 through August 20, 2018, and the discharge tested back into 
compliance in September 2018, without having to initiate a Toxicity Reduction Evaluation 
(TRE ). In January 2020, t he maximum daily effluent limitation for chronic toxicity and the 
monthly median effluent limitation were exceeded. Although additional samples were 
collected during the month, the tests were invalidated because the contract lab’s control 
sample failed to meet test acceptability criteria. Accelerated testing was initiated and the 
discharge tested back into compliance without having to initiate a TRE. In April 2021, the 
effluent exceeded the MMEL because the Discharger was unable to collect a second  
passing chronic toxicity test to comply with the monthly median, since the contract lab’s 
control sample was unable to meet the test acceptability criteria specified in the USEPA 
test method. Accelerated testing was initiated on  May 11, 2021,  however  the chronic 
toxicity test failed  with an 8 7% effect . The contract lab issued a corrective action report 
indicating that there was a problem with one of the chemicals it had used to prepare the 
very hard water control sample. Subsequently, on May 18, 2021, Burbank initi ated a 
TRE.  A comparison of effluent chemical data to WET test data did not indicate any 
strong associations  that could account for the observed toxicity. Comparison of effluent 
chemical concentrations to  literature -reported toxic concentrations for C. dub ia also did 
not indicate any likely causative  agent(s).  The contract lab concluded that its control test 
failures are very likely responsible for B urbank WRP exceeding its  chronic toxicity limits.  
From June to September 2021, the Burbank  WRP effluent compl ied with the  chronic 
toxicity MMEL and MDEL, therefore the TRE process was concluded.
