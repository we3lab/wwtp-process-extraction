FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment and Controls
2.1.1. The Avalon  WWT F is a secondary  wastewater treatment plant  with a dry weather 
design capacity of 1.2 MGD .  The Facility serves an estimated population of 4,000 
people and receives untreated domestic and commercial wastewater from the City 
of Avalon . The wastewater is a  mix of fresh and s eawater. The influent to the Avalon 
WWTF is approximately 40% to 50% s eawater  that is used for toilet flushing in the 
City of Avalon.
2.1.2. In addition, to protect water quality in Avalon Bay, the Facility also treats a portion 
of the dry weather surface runoff and the first flush of storm water (first half hour of 
rainfall), via low -flow diversions into the sewer collection system. This is an  
intermittent system and only activates during dry weather and the first half hour of 
rainfall events; it is not a combined sewer system. After the first half hour of rainfall, 
the system is designed to redirect runoff to the storm drains, which con vey 
stormwater to the ocean.
2.1.3. Wastewater treatment at the Facility consists of a rotating screen for removal of 
large particles, an aerated grit unit, a trickling filter and activated sludge reactors for 
removal of organics, clarifiers for separation of solids, and a chlorination syste m for 
disinfection. The trickling filter is typically only used during the summer. When the 
trickling filter is online, approximately twenty percent of the influent flow is diverted 
from the aerated grit unit to remove additional BOD and suspended solids f rom the

2.2. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters
2.2.1. The secondary -treated wastewater is discharged to Discharge Point 001 to the 
Pacific Ocean off Pebbly Beach, approximately halfway between the Avalon WWTF 
and Avalon Bay. The discharge pipe is one foot in diameter and extends 400 feet 
offshore and 1 30 feet below the ocean surface (Latitude: 33.338611 °N, Longitude: 
118.311111 °W).
2.2.2. The State Water Board has designated the area from Binnacle Rock to Jewfish 
Point on Santa Catalina Island as an Area of Special Biological Significance (ASBS). 
The discharge point is about 1.5 miles north, outside the ASBS .

2.5 Planned Change s
The Avalon WWTF’s chlorine contact basin deteriorated over the years and was in 
danger of collapsing, creating a safety hazard to staff working at the Avalon WWTF 
when doing routine cleaning and potentially causing water quality issues. For the 
duration of the rehabilitation project  for the chlorine contact basin  (rehabilitation project) , 
the Discharger move d the sodium hypochlorite injection point from the chlorine contact 
basin to the clarifier effluent common channel at the beginning of the bypass line a nd 
collect ed routine grab samples 0.5 miles downstream of the Avalon WWTF at a street 
manhole. The wastewater continue d receiving the required chlorine contact time 
because the chlorine contact basin provided approximately 25 minutes of contact time , 
and the time it took the wastewater to travel to the manhole where grab samples were 
collected  was approximately 45 minutes. Wastewater was not dechlorinated prior to 
discharge. The Discharger also collect ed composite samples for routine monitoring 
along the en try point to the bypass line instead of at the end of the chlorine contact 
basin. The bypass line was rated to convey up to 2.3 million gallons per day and the 
daily average flow at the Avalon WWTF was approximately 0.5 MGD, so overloading of 
the bypass line was not anticipated. 
The rehabilitation project began on November 27, 2023 , and was completed on January 
10, 2024.
