FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
The SLRWRF and the LSWTP provides municipal wa stewater treatment services to a population 
of approximately 180,000 wit hin the boundaries of the City of Oc eanside and a portion of the City 
of Vista. Additionally, the SLRWRF serves a population of approxima tely 10,000 within the 
Rainbow Municipal Water District. The Rainbow Municipal Water District owns 1.5 MGD of the 
Discharger 's treatment capacity and is responsible  for its sanitary sewer system up to the point 
where it connects with the Discharger’s sanitary  sewer system. To reduce pumping costs, the 
Discharger has an agreement with the City of Vista for the exc hange, treatment, and disposal of 
equal volumes of nonindustrial wastewater generated in the respective service areas.  
The MBGPF is a groundwater desalin ation plant that treats local br ackish groundwater extracted 
from the Mission Hydrologic Suba rea (HSA) 903.11 to produce up to 6.37 MGD of potable water, 
15 percent of the City of Oceanside’s water supply.  
A. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment and Controls 
1. San Luis Rey Water Reclamat ion Facility (SLRWRF) 
The SLRWRF is located at 3950 N. River Road in Oceanside, California, approximately 6.5 
miles from the OOO. The head works of the SLRWRF includes  mechanical bar screens and 
aerated grit removal. From the headworks, the flow is divert ed into the East Plant (also 
known as Plant 1) and the West Plant (also k nown as Plant 2). Plant 1 consists of flow 
equalization, primary sedi mentation, biological secondary treatment by means of activated 
sludge and secondary sedimentation/ clarification. Plant 2 consis ts of primary sedimentation 
and biological secondary treatment by means of activated sludge and secondary 
sedimentation/clarificat ion. The Discharger may send the Pl ant 1 effluent to the effluent 
pump station wet well or to its tertiary treatment facility for further treatment and reuse. The 
Discharger can only send the Plan t 2 effluent to the effluent pump station wet well. At the 
effluent pump station wet well, the Discharger disinfects the secondary-treated wastewater 
with sodium hypochlorite to reduce bacte riological concentrations and pumps the 
disinfected secondary-treated wast ewater to the OOO. The Disch arger can also temporarily

2. La Salina Wastewater Tr eatment Plant (LSWTP) 
The LSWTP is located at 1330 Tait Street in Oceanside, California, adjacent to the mouth 
of Loma Alta Creek. Secondary treatment pr ocess at the LSWTP is accomplished by 
mechanical bar screens, aer ated grit removal, flow  equalization, primary 
sedimentation/clarific ation, and biological secondary tr eatment by means of activated 
sludge and secondary sediment ation/clarification. 
The Discharger’s ROWD stated t hat the peak monthly design ca pacity of the LSWTP is 5.5 
MGD. This Order carries over  the permitted flow of 5.5 MGD from Order No. R9-2011-
0016. From December 2011 to June 2019, the av erage influent flow into the LSWTP was 
2.8 MGD and the average effluent flow from  the LSWTP to the OOO was 2.6 MGD. 
Screenings from the headworks and solids from  the grit removal are collected onsite and 
trucked out of state to Cooper  Mountain Landfill in Welton, Arizona. The LSWTP features 
dissolved air floatation thickening for secondar y sludge, anaerobic digest ion of primary and 
secondary sludge, and belt filter  press dewatering of digest ed sludge. The LSWTP utilizes 
Option 1 (minimum 38 percent reduction of volatile  solids) to reduce ve ctor attraction. The 
LSWTP utilizes Alternative 1 (mo nitoring) for pathogen reduction. 
3. Mission Basin Groundwater Puri fication Facility (MBGPF) 
The MBGPF, located northwest of the intersecti on of Fireside Street and Heritage Street in 
Oceanside, California, treats br ackish groundwater for munici pal potable water supply. The 
MBGPF extracts groundwater fr om the Mission (HSA 903.11) Ba sin Aquifer (part of the 
larger San Luis Rey Valley groundwater basin). 
Treatment at the MBGPF is comprised of c hemical pretreatment (hydrochloric acid and 
scale inhibitor), cartridge filtration, re verse osmosis (RO) membrane filtration 
(demineralization), granular ac tivated carbon (GAC) for remova l and reduction of volatile 
organics (i.e. 1,2,3-trichloropr opane, 1,2-dichloropr opane), degasification (for removal of 
hydrogen sulfide and carbon dioxide gases),  and post-treatment disinfection by 
chloramination (sodium hypochlor ite and ammonium hydroxide). 
The primary RO process is designed as a tw o-stage system with a 75%  overall recovery 
rate. The permitted combined capacity of the MB GPF is 6.37 MGD. There are two RO units 
that can produce up to a maximum of 2.2 MGD permeate each (combined total of 4.4 
MGD). The RO permeate passes through the GAC system and then is blended with 
groundwater that is treated th rough a manganese greensand filtrati on system (for iron and 
manganese removal) just upstream  of the degasifiers. The m anganese greensand filtration 
system can produce up to a maximum of 1.97 MGD. Disinfection of the blended product 
water occurs after the degasif iers. Backwash waste waters produced by the GAC and 
manganese greensand filtrati on systems are discharged to an underground concrete 
holding basin and subsequently pumped to the SLRWRF via a 42-inch sewer force main. The brine waste (reject) from the primary RO process is discharged into a 24-inch ocean 
outline line. At maximum design conditions this  results in approximately 1.47 MGD of brine 
(reject) waste. From December  2011 to February 2019, the aver age effluent flow from the 
MBGPF to the OOO was 0.78 MGD.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes 
The Discharger listed the following long-term wa stewater efforts directed toward achieving 
goals of (1) maximizing recycled water use, and (2) improving wastewater treatment and 
conveyance system reliability:  
1. Replacement of the LSWTP.  In November 2014, the Disc harger’s City Council directed 
the Discharger’s Water Utilities staff to move  forward with developing a facilities-planning 
approach that would involve decommissioning the LSWTP, replacing the LSWTP with a 
pump station, and conveying wastewater fr om the LSWTP site to the SLRWRF for 
treatment and reuse. Under this new wastew ater planning approach, the Discharger would 
be positioned to make beneficial use of wastewater flows cu rrently treated at the LSWTP 
and discharged to the ocean. In accordance with this new planning direction, the 
Discharger is developing an im plementation plan for supporting planning, environmental 
evaluation, design, permitting, and cons truction of required facilities.  
2. Expansion of Non-potable Water System.  The Discharger is expanding the non-potable 
water system that conveys tertiary-treated ef fluent from the SLRWRF to regional recycled 
water customers in accordance with San Diego  Water Board Order No. 93-07. The purpose 
of this expansion is to serve the El Corazon soccer fields and the Goat Hill Park Golf Course. This effort will include converting five  miles of brine pipeline to non-potable use, 
constructing 4400 feet of new non-potable pipel ine, and routing wa ste brine from the 
MBGPF and Genentech, Inc. to the outfall through alternative pipes. 
3. Indirect Potable Reuse (IPR) Planning Study.  Since 2014, the Discharger has been 
engaged in a comprehensive effort  to identify opportunities to increase recycled water use 
and minimize ocean discharge through the implem entation of IPR, focusing on identifying 
IPR opportunities consistent with recycled water groundwater recharge and extraction 
regulations established by t he State Water Resources Contro l Board (State Water Board), 
Division of Drinking Water. Preliminary IP R planning has identifi ed a preferred Phase 1
