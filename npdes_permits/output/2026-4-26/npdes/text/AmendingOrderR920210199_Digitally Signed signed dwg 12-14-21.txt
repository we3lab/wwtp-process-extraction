2. La Salina Wastewater Tr eatment Plant (LSWTP)
The LSWTP is located at 1330 Tait Street in Oceanside, California, adjacent to the mouth
of Loma Alta Creek. Secondary treatment pr ocess at the LSWTP is accomplished by
mechanical bar screens, aer ated grit removal, flow equalization, primary
sedimentation/clarific ation, and biological secondary tr eatment by means of activated
sludge and secondary sediment ation/clarification.
The Discharger’s ROWD stated t hat the peak monthly design ca pacity of the LSWTP is 5.5
MGD. This Order carries over the permitted flow of 5.5 MGD from Order No. R9-2011-
0016. From December 2011 to June 2019, the av erage influent flow into the LSWTP was
2.8 MGD and the average effluent flow from the LSWTP to the OOO was 2.6 MGD.
Screenings from the headworks and solids from the grit removal are collected onsite and
trucked out of state to Cooper Mountain Landfill in Welton, Arizona. The LSWTP features
dissolved air floatation thickening for secondar y sludge, anaerobic digestion of primary and

LSWTP utilizes Alternative 1 (mo nitoring) for pathogen reduction.
3. Mission Basin Groundwater Puri fication Facility (MBGPF)
The MBGPF, located northwest of the intersecti on of Fireside Street and Heritage Street in
Oceanside, California, treats br ackish groundwater for munici pal potable water supply. The
MBGPF extracts groundwater fr om the Mission (HSA 903.11) Ba sin Aquifer (part of the
larger San Luis Rey Valley groundwater basin).
Treatment at the MBGPF is co mprised of chemical pretreat ment (hydrochloric acid and
scale inhibitor), cartridge filtration, re verse osmosis (RO) membrane filtration
(demineralization), granular ac tivated carbon (GAC) for remova l and reduction of volatile
organics (i.e. 1,2,3-trichloropr opane, 1,2-dichloropr opane), degasification (for removal of
hydrogen sulfide and carbon dioxide gases), and post-treatment disinfection by
chloramination (sodium hypochlor ite and ammonium hydroxide).
The primary RO process is designed as a tw o-stage system with a 75% overall recovery
rate. The permitted combined capacity of the MB GPF is 6.37 MGD. There are two RO units
that can produce up to a maximum of 2.2 MGD permeate each (combined total of 4.4
MGD). The RO permeate passes through the GAC system and then is blended with
groundwater that is treated th rough a manganese greensand filtrati on system (for iron and
manganese removal) just upstream of the degasifiers. The m anganese greensand filtration
system can produce up to a maximum of 1.97 MGD. Disinfection of the blended product
water occurs after the degasif iers. Backwash waste waters produced by the GAC and
manganese greensand filtrati on systems are discharged to an underground concrete
holding basin and subsequently pumped to the SLRWRF via a 42-inch sewer force main.
The brine waste (reject) from the primary RO process is discharged into a 24-inch ocean
outline line. At maximum design conditions this results in approximately 1.47 MGD of brine
(reject) waste. From December 2011 to February 2019, the aver age effluent flow from the
MBGPF to the OOO was 0.78 MGD.
