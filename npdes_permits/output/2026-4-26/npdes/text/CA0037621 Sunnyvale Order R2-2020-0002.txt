FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment and Controls  
1. Location and Service Area.  The Water Pollution Control Plant is located at 1444 Borregas 
Avenue , Sunnyvale. The plant provides advanced secondary treatment of wastewater from 
domestic, commercial , and industrial sources from the City of Sunnyvale, Rancho 
Rinconada, and Moffett Field. The Facility serves a population of  more than 150,000 in a 
23-square -mile service area.  
2. Collection System.  The City of Sunnyvale’s collection system consists of  approximately 312 
miles of gravity sewer pipes ranging from 4 inches to 48 inches in diameter, 140 miles of 
lower laterals, 2 miles of force mains, 4 lift stations, and 1 pump station.  
3. Wastewater Treatment.  The plant provides preliminary treatment, primary treatment, 
biological treatment, filtration, and disinfection . Disinfected advanced -secondary -treated 
effluent is discharged to Moffett Channel , a tributary of  Guadalupe Slough and Lower South 
San Francisco Bay . The plant ’s average dry weather effluent flow design capacity is  29.5 
million gallons per day (MGD) , and its  peak wet weather flow capacity  is 40 MGD. Over the 
past 5 years (December 1, 2014, to present), the plant’s highest daily dry weather discharge was 18.9 MGD (May 24, 2015), and the highest wet weather discharge was 28.4 MGD

a. Preliminary Treatment. Preliminary treatment consists of barrel grinders  followed by 
aerated grit removal . 
b. Primary Treatment. Following preliminary treatment, wastewater is pumped into 
rectangular primary sedimentation basins  to remove floatable and settleable material.  
c. Biological Treatment. All wastewater receives biological treatment through a three -step 
process. P rimary effluent is gravity -fed to 440 acres of mechanically aerated Oxidation 
Ponds, where organic matter and ammonia are degraded by aerobic and anaerobic 
bacteria. Nitrification and denitrification in the ponds are linked to ambient weather 
conditions and experience seasonal varia tions. Treated pond effluent is pumped to Fixed 
Growth Reactors (FGRs)  (i.e., trickling filters ) that  provide additional nitrification of 
ammonia. FGR effluent is conveyed to the Dissolved Air Flotation Tanks (DAFTs), where compressed air and polymer are i njected to coagulate, flocculate, and remove 
biological solids (algae and bacteria) generated during treatment in the Oxidation Ponds and FGRs . 
d. Filtration. Following biological treatment, the wastewater undergoes filtration  through 
four parallel  dual media  filters, two  of which may be used  to produc e recycled  water. The 
settled solids from the filter backwash water are pumped back to the oxidation ponds .  
e. Disinfection. Sodium hypochlorite is  metered into the filter ed effluent at the beginning of 
four serpentine chlorine contact tanks  to generate a chlorine residual (generally 
chloramines) for disinfection . Two of these channels may be used to produce  recycled 
water.  As the effluent leaves the contact tanks , sodium bisulfite is added as needed t o 
neutralize the hypochlorite. S odium bisulfite is added separately to t he portion of the 
effluent diverted for recycled water use before it is pumped  into the recycled water 
distribution system . 
As described in Fact Sheet sections III.E.1 and III.E.2, tre atment facility upgrades  may be 
installed pursuant to Provision VI.C.5.d of the Order.  
4. Sludge and Biosolids Management.  The Discharger pumps sludge produced in the primary 
sedimentation  basins to primary anaerobic digesters. Generally, the Discharger operates 
secondary digester s in series to stabilize and thicken the biosolids prior to dewatering, and to 
generate biogas used to fuel the plant ’s main influent pump engines and Power Generation 
Facility. The Discharger pumps digested sludge  (biosolids) to an equalization tank, 
conditions  the biosolids  with polymer , and mechanically dewater s them  using a belt filter 
press to approximately 20 to 25 percent solids prior to land application or disposal at a landfill. The Discharger may dispos e of biosolids at an onsite  biosolids monofill regulated as 
a surface disposal site under Order No. R2–2004– 0030. Secondary treatment solids removed 
by dredging the oxidation ponds are chemically conditioned in an  equalization tank and 
dewatered using a centr ifuge or belt filter press to approximately 20 to 25 percent solids prior 
to land application or disposal at a landfill. The Discharger may divert a portion of the algae “float” produced in the dissolved air flotation tanks to the primary digesters .

6. Stormwater Management. All stormwater at the plant, including the biosolids monofill, is 
collected and directed to the headworks for treatment; therefore, no additional stormwater 
requirements are necessary.  
B. Discharge Point and Receiving Waters  
The Facility discharge s to Moffett Channel, tributary to South San Francisco Bay via Guadalupe 
Slough, all of which are waters of the United States.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes  
The City Council approved a Master Plan in August 2016 authorizing a 30- year reconstruction 
project known as the Sunnyvale Cleanwater Program. T he program will serve as a long- term 
guide for replacing the pla nt’s aging infrastructure. The following are descriptions of two  key 
projects  in the Cleanwater Program . Implementation of these projects  is not a requirement  of this 
Order , except to the extent that they pertain to increasing or ensuring treatment or wastewater 
collection system reliability. However, t he proposed upgrades described below may be 
authorized in accordance with Provision VI.C.5.d of the Order . Otherwise, t he Dis charger must 
seek necessary permits or permit modifications to implement any  changes.  
 1. Primary Treatment Facilities . This project includes the phased design and construction of 
new headworks, primary sedimentation tanks, an influent pump station, grit rem oval 
facilities, and associated electrical, mechanical, and control systems . These upgrades would 
be designed to enhance treatment and reliability. Along with a  modernized  sedimentation 
tank design for enhanced solids removal, the new facilities would bett er protect downstream 
processes and improve  biosolids quality through use of influent bar screens and high 
efficiency grit basins.  In addition, a  new 2 -megawatt  diesel generator would provide standby 
emergency power in the event of a power outage. The new facilities would not alter the 
current designed flow rates of 29.5 MGD average dry weather and 40 MGD  peak wet 
weather.  The Discharger expects to commission the se new facilities  by the end of 2020.  
2. Secondary Treatment Facilities . The Discharger proposes  to replace the  current biological 
treatment f acilities with a n activated s ludge system consisting of bioreactors and secondary 
clarifiers configured as a Modified Ludzak -Ettinger (ML E) process  to enhance nitrogen 
removal. Construction of the new s ystem is planned to occur in two stages. F irst, the 
Discharger would construct two aeration basins, four secondary clarifiers, and associated appurtenances. This activated sludge system would operate in parallel with the existing biological treatment syste m (Oxidation Ponds, FGRs, DAFTs) under a split -flow regime. 
Primary effluent would be apportioned using a weir structure, with approximately two -thirds  
going to the new activated sludge system and the remaining one -third  going to the existing 
biological tr eatment system. The Discharger would blend e ffluent from the existing 
biological  treatment system with effluent from the new activated sludge  system at a point 
upstream of the disinfection and dechlorination system. The Discharger expects to commission the se new facilities by the end of 2025. 
For the second stage , the Discharger proposes to complete the remaining portion of the 
activated sludge  system and phase out the existing biological treatment system; however, 
operation of the Oxidation Ponds under a  split- flow regime could continue, allow ing the 
Discharger to retain flexibility in managing its flow due to the large storage  capacity of the 
ponds. T he new activated sludge  system  would generate more sludge , which the Discharger 
proposes to treat with a  new t hickening and d ewatering f acility . This second stage of 
upgrades is not expected to be complete d within the term of this Order.
