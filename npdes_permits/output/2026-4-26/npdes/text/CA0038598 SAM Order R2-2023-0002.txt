FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment
2.1.1.  Service Area and Collection System. The Discharger owns and operates the 
wastewater treatment plant locate d at 1000 North Cabrillo Highway in Half 
Moon Bay. The plant provides secondary treatment of domestic wastewater to a 
population of approximately 2 6,000 through three satellite collection systems: 
the City of Half Moon Bay, the Granada Community Services D istrict, and the 
Montara Water and Sanitary District. Attachment B shows a map of the Facility 
area.
The collection system, also called the Intertie Pipeline System, is a separate 
sewer system comprised of approximately 8 miles of force mains and gravity 
interceptors, and three pump stations. The City of Half Moon Bay, the Montara 
Water and Sanitary District, and the Granada Community Services District act

2.1.2.  Wastewater Treatment.  The Facility provides  secondary treatment as 
described below and as shown  in the Process F low Diagram in Attachment  C. 
The treatment process consists of screening, grit removal , primary clarification,  
activated sludge aeration, secondary clarification, chlorination by sodium 
hypochlorite, and dechlorination by sodium bisulfite .
2.1.3. Sludge and Biosolids Management.  During the wastewater treatment 
process, the primary basins collect settleable solids that are pumped to a rotary 
drum thickener. The thickened solids are pumped to two anaerobic digesters. 
Supernatant  from the rotary drum thickener is returned  back to the headworks.  
Digested  biosolids are dewatered via a belt filter press prior to landfill dispos al.
2.1.4. Stormwater Management. The Discharger directs all onsite stormwater  to the 
plant  headworks for treatment ; therefore, coverage under the State Water 
Board’s General NPDES Permit for Stormwater Dischargers Associated with 
Industrial Activities (NPDES General Permit CAS000001) is unnecessary.
2.1.5. Planned Upgrade s. The Discharger is evaluating development of a recycled 
water treatment process , and this Order account s for th is potential project . (Use 
of the recycled water would be governed by the State Water Board’s Water 
Reclamation Requirements for Recycled Water Use , Order 2016 -0068 -DDW. ) 
The Discharger w ould use ultrafiltration and reverse osmosis to further treat 
secondary -treated effluent and provide ultraviolet disinfection. The Discharger 
would route about 50 percent of its secondary -treated effluent through 
ultrafiltration, and then about 50 percent of the ultrafiltration permeate through 
reverse osmosis. Reject  water  from ultrafiltration and reverse osmosis w ould be 
blended with the final effluent prior to discharge.  The proposed recycled water 
facility w ould require the following new facilities:
2.1.5.1. Secondary -treated effluent diversion pipeline to convey secondary -treated 
effluent from the secondary clarifiers to the recycled water treatment system.
2.1.5.2. Secondary -treated effluent equalization basin to store secondary -treated 
effluent and accommodate diurnal flow variations while providing a constant 
influent flow to the recycled water treatment processes. The existing primary 
clarifier No. 4 (currently unused) w ould be rehabilitated and have a total 
volume of 160,000 gallons.
2.1.5.3. Membrane filtration to meet recycled water treatment requirements.

2.2. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters.  Discharge of treated wastewater to th e 
Pacific Ocean is through a discharge pipe and submerged, multi -port diffuser 
located west of Pilarcitos Creek, approximately 1,900 feet offshore (Discharge 
Point 001). The discharge pipe lies on a ballast rock on the sea floor 
approximately 37 feet below  mean lower low water ( -37 MLLW) and is covered 
with sand much of the year due to seasonal sand drift. The final 238 feet of the 
outfall is a multi -port diffuser fitted with thirty -five 6 -inch rubber duck -bill valves, 
approximately 7 feet apart, that disch arge vertically approximately 2 feet above the 
ocean floor . The Discharger inspected the outfall on August 5, 2022 , and 
discovered the following defects: many duckbill check valves need replacing, and 
diffuser D -33 needs to be excavated.  To ensure  that the outfall is operating as 
designed, to comply with Standard Provisions (Attachment D) section 1.4, the 
Discharger plans to  complete repairs by October 2023.

Most of these violations stem from events that took place in October 2020  and

treatment at the plant , severely diminishing its treatment capability  and leading

twice seeding aeration basins with healthy bacteria, and reimplementing a 
source control program .

2.5. Climate Change . The Facility  is located outside of the FEMA 100 -year flood plain, 
and is less likely to be impacted by sea level rise and groundwater rise.  The 
Discharger indicates they will evaluate  the collection system for vulnerability to 
direct and indirect impacts of climate change in the Discharger’s Sanitary Sewer 
Management Plan .
