FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
The Discharger provides sewerage service for the City of Solana Beach, City of Encinitas, City of 
Del Mar, Cardiff Sanitation District, Encinitas Sanitation District, Solana Beach Sanitation District, 
and the Rancho Santa Fe Community Services District, and serves a population of approximately 
34,600, including resi dential and commercial users. There are no significant industrial users (SIUs) 
in the Discharger’s service area. The design flow capacity of the Facility is 5.25 MGD. The 
average annual influent flow to the Facility was 2.4 MGD in 2016.  
The Facility also t reats nuisance surface flows f rom storm drains. Between 2010 and 2015, the 
Discharger implemented surface water diversion projects at Seascape Sur and at the Facility in 
partnership with the City of Solana Beach and the City of Encinitas. The Seascape Sur diversion 
structure allows the Discharger to divert nuisance surface runoff from a beach storm drain to the 
sanitary sewer for treatment at the Facility. The diversion system at the Facility also conveys 
nuisance surface flow from a nearby storm channel to the Facility for treatment.  However, as the 
City of Encinitas and other agencies in the Carlsbad Watershed Management Area plan on 
reducing non- storm water discharges to municipal separate storm system sewers (MS4) (as 
discussed further in section III.E.4  of this Fact Sheet), the amount of surface water available for 
recycled water production may decrease in the future.  
A. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment and Controls  
Secondary treatment at the Facility is accomplished with screening and aerated grit removal, 
primary sedimentation using clarifiers, flow equalization, and activated sludge treatment 
followed by secondary clarification. Secondary -treated effluent is either discharged to the 
Pacific Ocean through the San Elijo Ocean Outfall  (SEOO) , or receives tertiary treatment for 
reuse applications in the San Dieguito Water District (SDWD), Santa Fe Irrigation District 
(SFID), Olivenhain Municipal Water District  (OMWD), and City of Del Mar.  
Screenings from the headworks and solids from grit rem oval are collected onsite and trucked 
to a local landfill. Sludge treatment consists of dissolved air flotation thickening, anaerobic

The 3.02 -MGD tertiary treatment portion of the Facility (the Advanced Water Purification 
Facility, AWPF) is designed to comply with the State Water Board Division of Drinking Water  
(DDW)  criteria for “disinfected tertiary recycled water”. The AWPF consists  of two parallel 
treatment trains. Train No. 1 provides coagulation, flocculation,  and granular media filtration.  
Train No. 2 provides both microfiltration (MF) and reverse osmosis (RO). The San Diego 
Water Board issued written notice to the Discharger on March 15, 2013 authorizing the 
initiation of discharge operations from the MF and RO facility. Spent washwater from the MF 
units is stored in a sump and directed to the Facility primary clarifiers for treatment. RO 
treatment is provided by units featuring thin film composite (TFC) membranes that achieve 
approximately a 75 percent recovery rate. In its ROWD, the Discharger indicated that one- half 
of the RO production capacity addressed within Order No. R 9-2000- 10 has been installed, 
and existing onsite RO facilities can handle an influent flow of approximately 0.7 MGD and 
produce approximately 0.5 MGD of product water. RO reject (brine) is commingled with 
Facility secondary -treated effluent as return flow and discharged to the SEOO. RO product 
water is combined with product water from Train No. 1 and the combined flow is disinfected 
using sodium hypochlorite. The system is also capable of providing MF water from Train No. 
2 to the disinfection system when Train No. 1 is offline for maintenance. Disinfected ter tiary 
recycled water from the AWPF is purveyed by the Discharger to the SDWD , SFID , OMWD , 
and City of Del Mar. The discharge of tertiary -treated effluent for reuse application is covered 
under the San Diego Water Board Order No. 2000- 10 and Addendum No. 1 to Order No. 
2000- 10. 
B. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters  
Secondary -treated wastewater and RO reject (brine) from the Facility is pumped through 
Discharge Point No. 001, beneath the San Elijo Lagoon to the SEOO . The 2015 San Elijo 
Ocean Outfall Capacity Study for the San Elijo Joint Powers Authority & City of Escondido 
(dated April 2015 and included with the ROWD) concluded that the hydraulic capacity of the 
SEOO is between 25.5 and 25.8 MGD, and recommended the more conservative values of 
25.5 MGD as the  hydraulic capacity. The SEOO  is co-owned by the Discharger and the City 
of Escondido, which own 21 percent (5.35 MGD) and 79 percent (20.15 MGD) of the outfall 
capacity, respectively. The SEOO  begins at a point approximately 2,200 feet south of the 
mouth of the San Elijo Lagoon, where treated wastewater and RO reject (brine) from the 
Facility merges with treated wastewater from the City of Escondido’s Hale Avenue Resource 
Recovery Facility and brine flows from the proposed Membrane Filtration/Reverse Osmos is 
Facility  (MFRO ) Facility (both regulated by NPDES No. CA0107981) , and brine flows from 
Stone Brewing Co.1 and Palomar Energy Center2. The SEOO  extends into the Pacific Ocean, 
where the inshore end of a diffuser is located approximately 6,800 feet offshore at a depth of 
approximately 110 feet. The diffuser, which is collinear with the outfall, is approximately 1,200 
feet in length and extends to a depth of approximately 148 feet. The terminus of the diffuser 
(i.e., Discharge Point No. 001) is located at Latitude 33° 00’ 21” North and Longitude 117° 18’ 
09” West.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes  
In the near future, recycled water demands may exceed Facility influent flows during peak 
demand months. To meet the projected recycled water demands, the Discharger has 
implemented two surface water diversion projects within the City of Solana Beach and the 
City of Encinitas, as discussed  in section II of this Fact Sheet. The Discharger will continue to 
coordinate with the cities of Solana Beach and Encinitas and other local stormwater agencies 
to explore and (if feasible) implement additional surface water diversion projects that are 
consistent with protec ting the environment, will maximize recycled water production from the 
Discharger’s existing treatment facilities, and will meet the Discharger’s commitment to 
increasing recycled water production.  However, as the City of Encinitas and other agencies in 
the Carlsbad watershed plan on reducing non- storm water discharges to MS4s (as discussed 
further in section III.E.4 of this Fact Sheet), the amount of surface water available for recycled 
water production may decrease in the future.  
The Discharger has also been working with the City of Del Mar to increase flows to the 
Facility headworks. The City of Del Mar is a participating agency within the San Diego 
Metropolitan Sewerage System (Metro System), and currently directs approximately 0.7 MGD 
of wastewater to t he Metro System for treatment at the E.W. Blom Point Loma Wastewater 
Treatment Plant. In 2014, the Discharger and City of Del Mar entered into a 30- year 
agreement to transfer approximately 0. 6 MGD of the City of Del Mar wastewater to the Facility 
for treat ment and reuse. The City of Del Mar would retain its Metro System connection to treat 
a portion of its wastewater flows. As part of the agreement, the City of Del Mar has 
construct ed a pipeline and conveyance facilities to connect with the City of Solana B each 
sanitary sewer system, which flows to the Facility. The schedule for implementing the 
agreement has not been set yet, but is projected to be completed with in the next five years.  
Another option the Discharger has to increase recycled water production is to divert City of 
Escondido secondary effluent from the Escondido Land Outfall, just prior to the SEOO, into 
the Facility for tertiary treatment and reuse.   
The Discharger is also working with the City of Encinitas to consider redirecting flow from the 
Encinitas Sanitation District that currently flows through Moonlight Beach Pump Station to the 
Encina Water Pollution Control Facility  (EWPCF)3. The City of Encinitas owns capacity at 
both the Facility and EWPCF. Redirecting this flow would require a new pipeline from 
Moonlight Beach Pump Station and this work may begin within the next five years.
