DESCRIPTION OF DISCHARGE
The WWTF features a headworks, dual facultative lagoon system operated in parallel ,
and two disposal ponds. Screened effluent from the headworks (influent flow meter, bar
screen, and grinder) is discharged to one of two parallel facultative lagoons. The
lagoons are bentonite -lined , each contain a 5-horsepower aerator and have a total
depth of 7.5 feet , including two feet of freeboard. The s urface area at 5.5 feet depth is
2.3 acres. The RWD indicates each lagoon holds a volume of 1.8 million gallons ( MG);
however, based on the provided dimensions and an operating depth of 5.5 feet, each
lagoon holds a volume of 2.06 MG. Treated effluent from the lagoons discharges to a
splitter box enabling the return of treated effluent to the lagoons, recirculation via the lift
station, or discharge to one of two disposal ponds. Each disposal pond has a total
bottom area of approximately 3.25 acres, and each pond is approximately 4.9 feet deep,
including two feet of freeboard. The resulting volume for one disposal pond at the
operating depth of 2. 9 feet is 9.43 acre-feet, which combined provides about
19 acre-feet of total storage . The facultative lagoons are typically operated in parallel,
but the splitter box design enables them to be operated in series. There are no
disinfection components present at the WWTF.
According to Table 2 -1 of the RWD , which summarizes 2021 to 2023 self -monitoring
report (SMR) data, influent flows to the WWTF average approximately 28,000 gpd ,
much less than the permitted flow of 57,20 0 gpd. The peak month daily average flow
observed in 2021 was 62,000 gpd ; however, outside of this extreme flow, the maximum
month average daily observed flow during 2022 and 2023 was 3 8,000 gpd , and the
annual average daily flow from 2021 through 2023 ranged from 25,000 gpd to
33,000 gpd
