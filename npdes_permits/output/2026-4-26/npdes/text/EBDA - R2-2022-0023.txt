2.1.4.  Sludge and Biosolids Management  
2.1.4.1. City of Hayward  Water Pollution Control Facility . Sludge at the City of 
Hayward Water  Pollution Control Facility is anaerobically digested, 
dewatered in dewatering beds, and air  dried for up to two years. The City 
hauls dried biosolids to an authorized dis posal site for use  as alternative daily 
cover.
2.1.4.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant . Sludge at the City of 
San Leandro Water  Pollution Control Plant is anaerobically digested, 
dewatered using a belt filter press, and air  dried. The Ci ty hauls dried 
biosolids to an authorized disposal site or applies the biosolids to  land as 
Class A Exceptional Quality biosolids or Class B biosolids (as defined in 
40 C.F.R. part 503 ) depending on available bed  space . 
2.1.4.3. Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control 
Plant . Sludge at the  Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water 
Pollution Control Plant is anaerobically  digested, dewatered using a belt filter 
press, and air dried in a solar drying facility for 3 to 18 months. The Districts 
haul b iosolids to an authorized land application site.
2.1.4.4. Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant . Sludge at the 
Raymond A.  Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant is thickened, 
anaerobically digested, and  dewatered using centrifuges. The Union Sanitary 
District hauls  biosolids off -site for land application, composting, or used as  
alternative daily cover for municipal landfill. Up to 30 percent of the biosolids 
are composted offsite to make Class A Exceptio nal Quality biosolids  (as 
defined in 40 C.F.R. part 503 ).

2.2. Discharge Point and Receiving Water . The Discharger discharges treated 
effluent to Lower San Francisco Bay through the EBDA Common Outfall 
(Discharge Point 001). Lower San Francisco Bay is located in the Lower San 
Francisco Bay Basin watershed between the San Francisco -Oakland Bay Bridge 
and the San  Mateo -Hayward Bridge. The outfall’s diffuser is located 37,000 feet
(about 7 miles) offshore and is submerged 23.5 feet under the water surface. The 
diffuser consists of 251 six -inch bell mouth riser ports that each splits into 2 three -
inch perpe ndicular discharge points. In August 2016, EBDA completed a two -week 
diver and remote operated vehicle inspection of the outfall and concluded that it is 
in good working order. The outfall meets the requirements to be considered a 
deepwater discharge as de fined in Basin Plan section 4.6.1.

2.5. Sea Level Rise . Below is a description of Discharger’s specific sea level rise 
vulnerability assessments and adaptation planning relative to each EBDA member 
agency :
2.5.1.  City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility. Sea level rise does not 
threaten the City of Hayward  Water Pollution Control Facility . The plant is 
located above the 100 -year flood plain. According to the Hayward Regional 
Shoreline Adaptation Master Plan  (February 2021), the plant and its critical 
infras tructure would likely not experience  direct  flood impacts until 
approximately 7 feet of sea level rise occurs, which is unlikely to occur by 2050. 
In the event of 4 feet of sea level rise, the City of Hayward’s outlying facilities, 
such as its oxidation po nds and effluent channel, would likely become regularly 
inundated. The City is planning to prepare  an adaptation plan with a timeline for 
implementation of adaptation measures in response to the master plan’s sea 
level rise impact assessments .
2.5.2.  City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant. Sea level rise does not 
threaten the City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant. According to the 
City of San Leandro’s Climate Action Plan, sea levels are projected to rise 
approximately 2  feet by 2050 along the San Leandro shoreline where the 
treatment plant is located, which is consistent with the “median – high” risk 
projections in the California Ocean Protection Council’s State of California Sea 
Level Rise Guidance (2018). However, the plant is protected on all sides by 
levees and surrounding high ground that can accommodate 3.5 feet of sea level 
rise. Additionally, most of the plant’s critical control systems are elevated

2.5.3.  Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control 
Plant. Sea level rise does not immediately threaten the Oro Loma and Castro 
Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control Plant . The plant is currently 
protected by a perimeter levee, and critical infrastructure is located on higher 
ground that can accommodate 3.5 feet of sea level rise, which is the California

2.5.4.  Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Treatment Pl ant. Sea level rise does not 
immediately threaten t he Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Treatment Plant. 
According to a preliminary study the Union Sanitary District conducted in 2013, 
the plant is located an average  of 9.5 feet above mean sea level . Its northern 
and western sides are surrounded  by levees above that height , and its southern 
and eastern sides are surrounded by  levees  below that height . The preliminary 
study did not identify any critical infrastructure at risk of sea level rise impacts 
by 2050. The District plans to update the preliminary  study to reflect  updated 
guidance , and plans to base future infrastructure decisions on the updated 
study.
2.5.5.  Marina Dechlorination Facility. Sea level rise does not threaten t he Marina 
Dechlorination Facility and its critical infrastructure. The facility is located above 
the 100 -year flood plain  and several feet above  the 2050  sea level rise 
projections made in the  National Academy of Sciences Report: Sea -Level Rise 
for the Coasts of California, Oregon, and Washington: Past, Present, and 
Future (2012). The facility also has two berms to protect against high tides and 
storm surges. Additionally, t he Discharger install ed new sump pumps at the 
facility .
