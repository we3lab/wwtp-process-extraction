FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and Treatment
2.1.1. Locations and Service Areas . EBDA is comprised of five member agencies:
the City of Hayward, Oro Loma Sanitary District, Castro Valley Sa nitary District,
City of San Leandro, and Union Sanitary District. The member agencies convey
treated wastewater to the Marina Dechlorination Facility, located at 14150
Monarch Bay Drive in San Leandro. LAVWMA and its member agencies, the
Dublin San Ramon Services District , the City of Livermore, and the City of
Pleasanton, also convey wastewater to the Marina Dechlorination Facility.
Attachment B shows a map of the EBDA and LAVWMA member agency
facilities.
2.1.1.1. City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility. The City of Hayward
Water Pollution Control Facility is located at 3700 Enterprise Avenue in
Hayward. It provides secondary treatment of domestic, commercial, and
industrial wastewater for the City of Haywa rd, serving a population of about
160,000.
2.1.1.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant . The City of San
Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant is located at 3000 Davis Street in San
Leandro. It provides secondary treatment of domestic, commer cial, and
industrial wastewater for the northern portion of the City of San Leandro,
serving a population of about 60,000.

2.1.3.1. City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility . The City of Hayward
Water Pollution Control Facility can provide secondary treatment for an
average daily dry weather design flow of 18.5 MGD. In 202 1, the aver age
daily dry weather influent flow was 10 .0 MGD. The treatment process
consists of comminution, grit removal, primary sedimentation, flow
equalization, trickling filtration, activated sludge aeration, secondary
clarification, and chlorine disinfection. Th e plant also has 240 acres of out -of-
service oxidation ponds that can be used fo r emergency storage (e.g., during
peak wet weather).
2.1.3.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant . The City of San
Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant can provi de secondary treatment for
an average daily dry weather design flow of 7.6 MGD. In 202 1, the average
daily dry weather influent flow was 4. 3 MGD. The treatment process consists
of screening, grit removal, primary sedimentation, flow equalization, trickling
filtration, activated sludge aeration, secondary clarification, and chlorine
disinfection. The plant also has five acres of out -of-service oxidation ponds
that can be used for emergency storage.
2.1.3.3. Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control
Plant . The Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution
Control Plant can provide secondary treatment for an average daily dry
weather design flow of 20.0 MGD. In 202 1, the average daily dry weather
influe nt flow was 1 1.2 MGD. The treatment process consists of screening,
grit removal, primary sedimentation, modified Ludzack -Ettigner nitrification
and denitrification, secondary clarification, and sodium hypochlorite
disinfection.

2.1.4. Sludge and Biosolids Management
2.1.4.1. City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility . Sludge at the City of
Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility is anaerobically digested,
dewatered in dewatering beds, and air dried for up to two years. The City
hauls dried biosolids to an authorized dis posal site for use as alternative daily
cover.
2.1.4.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant . Sludge at the City of
San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant is anaerobically digested,
dewatered using a belt filter press, and air dried. The Ci ty hauls dried
biosolids to an authorized disposal site or applies the biosolids to land as
Class A Exceptional Quality biosolids or Class B biosolids (as defined in
40 C.F.R. part 503 ) depending on available bed space .
2.1.4.3. Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control
Plant . Sludge at the Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water
Pollution Control Plant is anaerobically digested, dewatered using a belt filter
press, and air dried in a solar drying facility for 3 to 18 months. The Districts
haul b iosolids to an authorized land application site.
2.1.4.4. Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant . Sludge at the
Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant is thickened,
anaerobically digested, and dewatered using centrifuges. The Union Sanitary
District hauls biosolids off -site for land application, composting, or used as
alternative daily cover for municipal landfill. Up to 30 percent of the biosolids
are composted offsite to make Class A Exceptio nal Quality biosolids (as
defined in 40 C.F.R. part 503 ).
