FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids  Treatment and Controls
2.1.1. The LAGWRP, a tertiary wastewater treatment plant is located at 4600 Colorado 
Boulevard, Los Angeles, California.  Attachment  B shows the location of the Facility . 
The LAGWRP currently receives wastewater from the cities of Glendale, Burbank, 
Los Angeles, La Cañada Flintridge, and from Los Angeles Zoo. The wastewater is a 
mixture of domestic and industrial wastewater that is pre -treated pursuant to 40 CFR 
part 403. LAGWRP has a design capacity of 20 MGD  and serves an estimated 
population of 434,000 people .

Limits Evaluations for this facility are conducted concurrently with the Local Limits 
Evaluations for the Hyperion Water Reclamation Plant and the Donald C. Tillman 
Water Reclamation Plant.
2.1.3. Treatment at the LAGWRP consists of preliminary treatment (bar screening and 
grit removal) ; primary treatment (primary sedimentation  and scum removal ); 
secondary treatment (activated sludge biological treatment with nitrification and 
denitrification  (NDN) ); secondary sedimentation  with coagulation ; tertiary treatment 
(aqua cloth and deep bed sand filtration ); disinfection  with ammonium  hydroxide and 
sodium hypochlorite ; and dechlorination  with sodium bisulfite . Treated wastewater 
discharged to  the Los Angeles River is dechlorinated but the effluent delivered for 
reuse is not dechlorinated .
2.1.4. Ammoni um hydroxide and sodium hypochlori te are  added to the treated effluent 
after tertiary treatment and prior to entering the chlorine contact tanks  to destroy 
bacteria, pathogens and viruses, and to minimize algal growth in pipes . Prior to 
discharge, sodium bisulfite is added to the treated e ffluent to remove residual 
chlorine .
2.1.5.  In 2006, t he Discharger  constructed  a biological nutrient removal system with 
NDN in order to achieve compliance with the ammonia Basin Plan objectives.  The 
system was completed and has been in operation since June 2007 .
2.2. Discharge Points  and Receiving Waters

of the United States, upstream the Los Angeles River Estuary. Treated effluent is 
discharged from the Facility to surface waters at Discharge Point 001.

The Los Angeles River watershed is one of the largest in the Region. It is also one of 
the most div erse in terms of land use patterns. The Los Angeles River drains an 824-
square mile area. Approximately 324 square miles of the watershed are covered by 
forest or open space land including the area near the headwaters which originate in the 
Santa Monica, S anta Susana, and San Gabriel Mountains. The rest of the watershed is 
highly developed. The river flows through the San Fernando Valley past heavily 
developed residential and commercial areas. The LAGWRP discharges to a soft -
bottomed portion of the Los Ange les River in the eastern end of the San Fernando 
Valley  where the river bends around the Hollywood Hills and flows through Griffith and 
Elysian Parks, in an area known as Glendale Narrows.   Since the water table was too 
high to allow laying of concrete, th e river in this area has a rocky, unlined bottom with 
concrete -lined or rip -rap sides. This stretch of the river is fed by natural springs and 
supports stands of willows, sycamores, and cottonwoods. The many trails and paths 
along the river in this area ar e heavily used by the public for hiking, horseback riding, 
and bird watching.   Due to major flood events at the beginning of the century, by the 
1950s most of the river was lined with concrete  except for the Glendale Narrows area .  
From the Arroyo Seco, no rth of downtown Los Angeles, to the confluence with the Rio 
Hondo, the river flows through industrial and commercial areas and is bordered by 
railyards, freeways, and major commercial and government buildings. From the Rio 
Hondo to the Pacific Ocean, the r iver flows through industrial, residential, and 
commercial areas, including major refineries and petroleum products storage facilities, 
major freeways, rail lines, and railyards serving the Ports of Los Angeles and Long 
Beach .

2.4. Compliance Summary
According to the monitoring data  collected from May 1, 201 7 to January 31, 202 2, the 
Discharger was able to meet all effluent limitations except for the effluent limitation for 
dibenzo(a,h) anthracene. The concentration of dibenzo(a,h) anthracene was 0.25 µg/L 
on June 26, 2017 and 0.47 µg/L on September 5, 2017, exceeding the maximum  daily 
effluent limitation of 0.098 µg/L. This exceedance is being investigated for enforcement 
action . Table F -2 above summarizes the effluent limitation s in the previous permit and 
the associated monitoring results . In some instances , the maximum daily c oncentration 
for a pollutant exceeded the objective , however these were not considered 
exceedances as described below:
1. Temperature:  The maximum daily temperature exceeded the maximum daily 
effluent limitation for temperature;  however , the previous permit i ncluded an 
exception to the effluent limitation if the effluent temperature was elevated as a 
result of external ambient temperature. The Discharger reported that this 
temperature should not be considered a violation  due to the high  ambient 
temperature; however, this determination has not yet been made by the enforcement 
unit at the Los Angeles Water Board.  
2. Nitrate and nitrate plus nitrite : The Discharger ran weekly sampling for nitrate and 
nitrite. The maximum daily concentration e xceeded the objective, but since the 
Discharger collected multiple samples during the month, the average monthly 
effluent limitation of 7.3 mg/L was not exceeded. 
There were multiple monitoring and reporting requirement violations due to deficient 
monitoring of multiple pollutants  throughout the monitoring period . The Dischar ger 
conducted corrective actions to prevent deficient monitoring  violations including: (1) 
providing additional staff training ; (2) updat ing labels , sample checklists and the contract 
laboratory  chain -of-custody  form; and (3) updating laboratory  QA/QC  protocols .
