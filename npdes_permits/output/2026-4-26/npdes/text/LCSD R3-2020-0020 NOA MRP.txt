2. WASTEWATER SYSTEM OPERATION SUMMARY

Laguna County Sanitation District wastewater reclamation plant (Wastewater System) is
a Class IV wastewater system . Laguna County Sanitation District must staff a Grade IV
chief plant operator for proper operation of the Wastewater System. The current chief
plant operator is Jerry Nichols , Grade IV.

The Wastewater System can treat a maximum of 3,700,000 gallons per day .
Approximately, 3,200,000 gpd has a low salt concentration and is treated to disinfected
tertiary6 treatment levels. Another 500,000 gpd has a high salt concentration and is
treated through a reverse osmosis system. The reverse osmosis permeate is treated to
disinfected tertiary treatment levels. The Wastewater System consists of the treatment
technologies and treatment capabilities shown in Table 2 .

Influent Separation Wastewater flows into the primary clarifier splitter box after
degritting. The primary clarifier splitter box splits high salt
flow and low salt flow. High salts flow goes to the high
TDS pond, while low salts flow normally goes to the
aeration basin/mixed liquor splitter box . Separation is
controlled by monitoring electrical conductivity (EC). The
high salts set point is approximately 2,600 microsiemens
per centimeter (μS/cm). High salts flow is typically 0.35
million gallons per day (mgd) and as high as 0.5 mgd. This flow regularly occurs between 5:00 am and 10:00 am with
the low salts flow occurring the remainder of the time.
 The low salts pond receives flow from the primary clarifier splitter box, tertiary filtration reject, sludge beds return flow and captured stormwater. Typical flows are 650 gpm
during the high salts diversion five-hour period.

Low Salts Secondary
Treatment Secondary tr eatment systems for low salts wastewater
consists of an aeration basin/mixed liquor splitter box, three aeration basins , two secondary clarifiers, a return
activated sludge (RAS) and wasted activated sludge (WAS) pump station, and two equalization ponds .
Undisinfected secondary recycled water may be diverted by overflow from these ponds to Lower Storage Ponds.
Bypass of less than undisinfected secondary recycled water from these ponds is pumped to the Low TDS Pond where it is reintroduced to plant treatment at the aeration
basin/mixed liquor splitter box. The aeration basin/mixed
liquor splitter box equally distributes wastewater to t he
aeration basins. The aeration basins are sized for 3.2 mgd and a solids retention time (SRT ) of 12 days.

Each aeration basin has six zones and bypassing capabilities with various settings for anaerobic, anoxic, or
oxic functions. N ormal zone operation is anticipated to be
anoxic (can be anaerobic) , anoxic, oxic (can be
anaerobic) , anoxic (can be oxic) , oxic and oxic. Various
combinations of zone functions exist when planning
maintenance, startup, and other operating conditions.

High Salts Biological
and Filtered Treatment Biological and f iltered treatment for high salt concentration
wastewater is performed by a membrane bioreactor
(MBR). When high salt concentrations are detected by an
electroconductivity meter in the primary clarifier splitter
box, wastewater is redirected as high salt flow to 1 mm
rotary drum screens with flows directed to a shotcrete-lined high TDS pond. This flow is pu mped over the course
of the next 24- hour period to a MBR for biological
treatment and ultrafiltration (UF). The MBR is housed in
two above ground steel process tanks. Each tank holds 4
cassettes with each cassette housing 16 membrane
modules. Membrane pore sizes range from 0.001 microns to 0.05 microns with a nominal size of 0.035 microns and absolute pore size of less than 0.1 microns. Membrane
useful life is approximately 5 years.
High Salts biological and filtration treatment capacity
(mgd): 0.5
Low Salts Filtration Treatment Filtration of the low salt concentration wastewater stream
is performed by the UF membrane facility. The UF
membrane is used to separate or remove colloidal sized
contaminants and suspended solids but does not remove dissolved solids such as salts. M embrane pore sizes range
from 0.001 microns to 0.05 microns with a nominal size of 0.035 microns and absolute pore size of less than 0.1
microns.
Filtration treatment capacity (mgd): 3.2
High Salts RO Filtration Treatment –
Dissolved Salts
Reduction The h igh salts treatment train flows from the MBR to a
reverse osmosis (RO) system for advanced treatment . The
RO pore size is on the order of 0.0008 microns and employed to remove dissolved solids (salt) content from the MBR effluent. The RO facility consists of three RO
military surplus refurbished mobile water treatment plants. Three units are used in 2+1 fashion with daily rotation to
match the MBR effluent flow rates. Each of the three units houses a cartridge filter assembly and 40 spiral wound
medium pressure membranes contained in 8 pressure vessels in a 4:2:1:1 four stage array . MBR effluent is
dosed with ammonia to convert free chlorine to chloramines to prevent damage to the RO membranes. Sulfuric acid and antiscalant is also dosed to prevent calcium carbonate and sulfate formation on the RO membrane surface. RO permeate merges with the low
salts UF membrane filtration permeate prior to disinfection.

UV Disinfection The UV disinfection system disinfects the comingled flows
from the background UF permeate (l ow TDS treatment
train) and the RO permeate (high TDS treatment train).

Check valves upstream of the UV units and on both inflow
lines prevent backflow in the system and the new
upstream UV break tank provides steady state flow rates that improves UV system performance. The UV
disinfection system in use is manufactured by Aquionics
and consists of 4 inline Model 7500 medium pressur e lamp
chambers (a.k.a. banks) . Each chamber houses 12 lamps.
Standard operation is based on three in use with the one
in standby. With all four units in operation at power level 3,
the system can disinfect up to 4.7 mgd. Revalidation of UV
lamps performed after startup of new treatment train may change these settings.

Turbidity is monitored in the feed water, UV transmittance is monitored from the through- flow, and flow rate is
monitored at the effluent .
Disinfection Capacity ( mgd): 4.7
