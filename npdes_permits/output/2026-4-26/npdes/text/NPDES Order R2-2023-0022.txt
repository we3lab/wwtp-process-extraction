FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment
2.1.1. Service Area. The treatment plant provides secondary treatment of domestic
wastewater for the City of Sausalito, uni ncorporated areas of Marin County
including Marin City, part of the Tamalpais Community Service District, and the
Golden Gate National Recreation Area (National Park Service) , serving a
population of about 22,290 . Attachment B shows the area around the plant .
2.1.2. Wastewater Treatment. The average dry weather flow to the plant is about
1.1 MGD. The treatment process is described below and as shown in the
Process Flow Diagram in Attachment C. During the previous order term, the
Discharger upgraded its treatment plant , which increased its secondary
treatment capacity from 6.0 MGD to 9.0 MGD , and increased its hydraulic
capacity to 12 MGD . The Discharger concluded construction of this project in
March 202 0. The project included addition of a headworks with bar screening
and grit removal, construction of a supplemental primary clarifier, upgraded
media and new pumps to increase the treatment capacity of fixed -film reactors,
replacement of sand filt ers with rotating disk filters, and construction of 600,000
gallons of flow equalization storage . The updated treatment process includes
bar screening, grit removal, primary clarification, biofiltration using fixed film
reactors, secondary clarification, filtration using rotating disk filters , chlorine
disinfection, and sodium bisulfite dechlorination.
During wet weather periods, primary -treated wastewater flows above 9.0 MGD
are bypassed around biological treatment when necessary and blend ed with
biologically -treated wastewater prior to disinfection.
2.1.3. Collection System. The Discharger owns and operates seven pump stations
and about 11 miles of sanitary sewer lines in unincorporated areas of Marin
County , Marin City, and the City of Sausalito . Three satellite collection systems
owned and operated by other entities also contribute wastewater to the
treatment plant: the City of Sausalito (2 1 miles), Tamalpais Community Service
District (3 0 miles), and the National Park Service (1 7 miles). The Discharger
operates and maintains four pump stations owne d by the City of Sausalito. With
the exception of the National Park Service, all of these collection systems are
covered by the Statewide General Waste Discharge Requirements for Sanitary
Sewer Systems ( State Water Board Order WQ 2022 -0103 -DWQ) .
2.1.4. Sludge and Biosolids Management. The Discharger pumps secondary sludge
from the primary clarifiers to the primary digester and from the secondary
clarifiers to the headworks for capture in the primary clarifier . The primary
digester is heated, mixed, and dosed with ferric chloride. From the primary
digester, the Discharger pumps digested sludge to a secondary digester where
it is mixed but not heated. Sludge from the secondary digester passes through

2.1.6. Recycled Water. The Discharger does not recycle treated waste water due to
its small discharge volume and the distance between the treatment plant and
potential users .

8.1.3. Removal Efficiency. The ratio of pollutants removed by the treatment facilities
to pollutants entering the treatment facilities (expressed as a percentage). The
Discharger shall determine removal efficiencies using monthly averages (by
calendar month unless otherwise specifi ed) of pollutant concentration of influent
and effluent samples collected at about the same time and using the following
equation (or its equivalent):
Removal Efficiency (%) =
100 x [1 - (Effluent Concentration / Influent Concentration)]
8.2. Blending – the practice of bypassing biological treatment units and recombining
the bypass wastewater with biologically -treated wastewater.
8.3. Composite Sample – a sample composed of individual grab samples collected
manually or by an automatic sampling device on the basis of time or flow as
specified in the MRP. For flow -based composites, the proportion of each grab
sample included in the composite sample shall b e within plus or minus five percent
(+/-5%) of the representative flow of the waste stream being measured at the time
of grab sample collection. Alternatively, equal volume grab samples may be
individually analyzed with the flow -weighted average calculated by averaging flow -
weighted ratios of each grab sample analytical result. Grab samples comprising
time-based composite samples shall be collected at intervals not greater than
those specified in the MRP. The quantity of each grab sam ple comprising a time -
based composite sample shall be a set of flow proportional volumes as specified in
the MRP. If a particular time -based or flow -based composite sampling protocol is
not specified in the MRP, the Discharger shall determine and implement the most
representative protocol.
