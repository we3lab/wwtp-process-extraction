FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and  Treatment
2.1.1.  Service Area and Collection System . The plant provides advanced -
secondary treatment of domestic and industrial wastewater from within the City 
of America n Canyon , and  serves a population of about 20,000 people  and 
several industrial dischargers , including  a food processing facility, a winery, and 
a beverage bottling company.  
The Discharger owns and operates two separate  collection systems, one for 
domestic wastewater (85 to 90 percent of the influent) and one for industrial 
wastewater (10 to 15 percent of the influent). These collection systems include 
approximately 54 miles of gravity sewer, 4.9 miles of force main, and  five pump 
stations.
2.1.2.  Wastewater Treatment.  Treatment  consists of flow equalization and aeration , 
micro -screening  and grit removal, biological secondary treatment, membrane 
filtration, disinfection, and, for discharges at Discharge Point 001, post -
discharge aeration. The plant  has an average daily treatment capacity of up to 
2.5 MGD and a peak wet weather capacity of 5.0  MGD. From June  2017 
through December  2021, the plant treated a n average  daily flow of 1.5  MGD 
and a maximum daily flow of 3.3 MGD. A ttachment C provides the plant  flow 
schematic.
The secondary treatment system consists of four parallel biological treatment 
trains, each providing anoxic and aerobic biological treatment, and membrane 
filtration. The four treatment trains can be used to s egregate domestic 
wastewater (Main Aeration Basin) from industrial wastewater (North Aeration 
Basin). The Discharger typically operate s the plant to combine domestic and 
industrial wastewater before secondary treatment but may begin  to segregate 
domestic w astewater from industrial wastewater more frequently as recycled 
water production  increases . Treated wastewater discharges are disinfected by 
ultraviolet light (UV). Recycled water is disinfected using chlorine  (see § 2.1.4.) . 
2.1.3. Sludge and Biosolids Management . Biosolids generated during the treatment 
process are stored in two 5 -million -gallon earthen clay -lined sludge storage 
basins, one of which is lined with an impermeable polyethylene liner . The 
basins provide biosolids storage capacity sufficient for about  10 years. Biosolids

2.1.4. Recycling . The plant  produces recycled wastewater under the State  Water 
Board’s General Water Reuse Order  (WQ 2016 -0068 -DDW ) year -round for 
irrigation and non -potable indoor uses , such as toilet flushing. Domestic 
wastewater is typically lower in total dissolved solids than  the Facility’s  industrial 
wastewater, thus it is more suitable for recycling. The Discharger, Am erican 
Canyon High School, and local vineyards use the recycled water for irrigation. 
The plant’s recycling facilities include a separate chlorine contact basin used 
only to disinfect recycled water, a one -million -gallon storage tank , and a 
recently  constr ucted 1.5 -million -gallon storage tank; the Discharger also has at 
least ten miles of recycled water pipe lines. 
2.1.5  Stormwater Management . The Discharger directs all onsite stormwater to the 
plant headworks for treatment ; therefore , coverage under the State Water 
Board’s General NPDES Permit for Stormwater Discharges Associated With 
Industrial Activities  (NPDES General Permit CAS000001)  is unnecessary .
2.2. Discharge Point s and Receiving Water . The plant either distributes treated 
wastewater for recycling or discharges effluent  from either Discharge Point 001 
to the North Slough or Discharge Point 003 to two constructed wetland ponds 
(Pond 1 and Pond  2) tributary to the North Slough. Treated wastewater is 
discharged at Discharge Point  001 during the wet season (November 1 through 
April 30) , but discharge at Discharge Point 001 is prohibited during the dry 
season (May 1 through October  31). Treated wastewater is discharged  at 
Discharge Point  003 year -round. The North Slough and the cons tructed wetlands 
are water s of the United States within the San Pablo Bay watershed .

2.4. Compliance Summary
2.4.1. Treatment Plant. On May 7, 2020, t he Discharger  exceeded the previous 
order’s  maximum daily effluent limitation for nickel of 11 µg/L with a nickel 
concentration of 22 µg/L at Monitoring Location EFF -001. Accelerated 
monitoring commenced on May 15, 2020, as soon as the lab notified the 
Discharger of the result . The subsequent nickel results  ranged from non -detect 
to an estimated detectio n of 3.4 µg/L, so accelerated monitoring was terminated 
on May 19, 2020.  Regional Water Board enforcement is pending .
The Discharger reported deficient monitoring in July and November 2020. 
These events are described below:
·The Discharger found the weekly BOD results from July 31, 2020 , to be 
abnormal  and requested that the laboratory review them. Upon review, the 
laboratory  found it had made several errors  that rendered the results 
unreportable . By that time , it was too la te to collect replacement samples . 
The lab took steps to ensure the error will not be  repeated .
