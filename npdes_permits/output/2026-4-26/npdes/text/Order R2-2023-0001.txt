FACILITY DE SCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and Treatment
2.1.1.  Service Area and Collection System . The plant  provides  secondary  treatment  
of domestic and commercial wastewater from the City of Vallejo , the former 
Mare Island Naval Facility, and an adjacent unincorporated area . It serves a 
population of about 120,000  people . The wastewater collection  system consists 
of approximately 395 miles of sanitary sewer main lines, 3 8 pump stations, and 
about 78 miles of lower laterals . Attachment B shows a map of the area around 
the Facility . 
In 2005 and 2006, the Discharger completed significant capital improvement 
projects to the collection system to eliminate sanitary sewer overflows from two 
constructed wet  weather overflow structures, the Sears Point Pump Station 
Overflow and the Ryder Street Overflow. The improvements included a 
3.0 million gallon (MG) underground storage tank to eliminate sanitary sewer 
overflows from the Sears Point Pump Station and an 8.6 MG storage basin  
adjacent to the treatment p lant to eliminate sanitary sewer overflows from the 
Ryder Street Pump Station. When possible, t he Discharger operate s the 
storage basin  like an equalization basin to reduce discharges of blended 
effluent from the Facility; however, its primary purpose is to reduce and 
eliminate sanitary sewer overflows .
During the term of the previous order, the Disc harger  rehabilitated or replaced 
sewer mains, lower laterals, and manholes to reduce infiltration and inflow, 
including more than 24,000 feet of sewer mains  and 16,000 feet of lower 
laterals . The Discharger also  financially assist ed homeowners to replace 790 
upper laterals. The Discharger also intends to  complete a Collection System 
Master Plan documenting its collec tion system facilities and their condition, and 
recommending actions to further reduce inflow and infiltration , in March 2023 .
2.1.2.  Wastewater Treatment . The treatment plant’s average dry weather design 
capacity for secondary treatment is 15.5  million gallons per day (MGD), and its 
peak wet weather capacity for secondary treatment is 35  MGD. The plant has 
an additional 25 MGD primary treatment capacity. The  plant’s peak wet weather 
discharge capacity is 60 MGD (through Discharge Points  001 and 002 
combined). Average dry weather flow from 2018 through 20 20 was 7.9 MGD. 
The daily wet weather flow from November 2017 through April 2021 ranged 
from 3.9 to 60 MGD.
Treatment consists of screening, aerated grit removal, primary sedimentation by 
circular and rectangular clarifiers, biological treatment by trickling filters followed

Under normal conditions, the plant discharges secondary -treated effluent to 
Discharge Point s 001 and 002, deepwater outfalls located in Carquinez Strait 
and Mare Island Strait; Discharge Point 002 is the primary  outfall. During wet 
weather, the plant provides full secondary treatment of flows up to 35 MGD and 
primary treatment of flows from 35 MGD to 60 MGD. The plant blends primary -
treated effluent with secondary -treated effluent before discharging the blended 
effluent to Carquinez Strait via Discharge Point  001. By means of automated 
flow splitting, the plant discharges only secondary -treated effluent to Mare 
Island Strait via Discharge Point 002 to minimize potential receiving water 
impacts. Attachment C provid es plant flow schematics.
2.1.3. Biosolids  Management . The Discharger treats its b iosolids by gravity 
thickening or co -settling, lime stabilization, and dewatering by belt -filter presses. 
The Discharger’s s tabilized, dewatered biosolids are hauled offsite for land 
application at the Discharger’s Biosolids Utilization Project on Tubbs Island, 
Sonoma County. Biosolids are temporarily stockpiled at the Tubbs Island site 
and subsequently spread and incorporated into the soil as a soil amendment on 
land used for  agricultural crop production.  Beginning in November 2019, the 
Discharger began sending a small portion of its biosolids to the Lystek Organic 
Material Recovery Center located at the Fairfield Suisun Sewer District  
wastewater treatment plant in Fairfield, California , in Solano County  for 
incorporation into Lystek’s biosolids -derived liquid  fertilizer.
2.1.4.  Stormwater Management . The Discharger is not required to be covered under 
the statewide NPDES permit for stormwater discharges associated with 
industri al activities (NPDES General Permit CAS000001) because all 
stormwater at the plant and pump stations is collected and directed to the 
plant’s headworks for treatment.
2.2. Discharge Point s and Receiving Waters
2.2.1. Discharge Point 001 – Carquinez Strait.  Discharge Point 001 is an ancillary  
outfall. It is a submerged diffuser 400 feet from the north shore of Carquinez 
Strait near the north end of the Carquinez Bridge at a depth of  about 75 feet 
below the water surface. It consists of eight  16-inch diameter duckbill discharge 
ports elevated 10 feet from the Strait bottom (i.e., a depth of about 65 feet). The 
ports are spaced seven feet apart on center  on alternating sides of the diffuser. 
The ports are  at a vertical angle of 0 degrees (i.e.,  horizontal) and an horizontal 
angle of 20 degrees. The discharge receives an initial dilution of approximately 
41:1 (see Fact Sheet § 4.3.4. 2). 
The plant is allowed to discharge blended effluent to Discharge Point 001 when :
(1) peak wet -weather influent flows exceed the 35 -MGD capacity of the 
secondary treatment units,

2.2.2 Discharge Point 002 – Mare Island Strait.  Discharge Point 002 is the primary 
outfall . It is a submerged diffuser about 250 feet from the eastern shore of Mare 
Island Strait, submerged 10 to 11 feet below the water surface and parallel to 
the ambient current direction (i.e., approximately north -south). It consists of 
fourteen 8 -inch-diameter duckbill discharge ports elevated 3 feet from the Strait 
bottom (i.e., a  depth of about 7 to 8 feet). The ports are spaced 8 feet apart on 
center on the west side of the diffuser (seven on each side of a 20 -foot north -
south tee). Each port is at a vertical angle of 0  degrees (i.e., horizontal) and a 
horizontal angle of 90 degrees (perpendicular to the current direction). The 
discharge receives  an initial dilution of approximately  26:1 (see Fact Sheet 
§ 4.3.4. 2).
From November  2017 through April 2021, the plant discharged at an average 
rate of 8.9 MGD and a maximum rate of 30 MGD at Discharge Point 002.  
Discharge at this point occurred almost continuously.

2.4. Compliance Summary
2.4.1.  Treatment Plant. The Discharger violated its instantaneous maximum total 
residual chlorine effluent limitation of 0.0  mg/L once  during the previous order 
term. The violation occurred at Monitoring Location EFF -002 (Discharge 
Point  002) on May 18, 2022. The violation was ca used by an increase in 
discharge flow and consequent ly in sodium hypochlorite (i.e., chlorine) dosage 
while the sodium bisulfite analyzer controlling the sodium bisulfite  dosage had 
been  taken offline to clean its probe. The sodium bisulfite dosage therefo re 
remained constant instead of increasing  and was insufficient to neutralize the 
residual chlorine . When the chlorine residual was detected by the effluent 
chlorine analyzer , plant staff manually increased the sodium bisulfite dosage, 
which ended the chlorine residual discharge. The violation lasted about nine 
minutes and the maximum effluent residual chlorine concentration  was 
0.34 mg/L.
To prevent a recurrence of this violation, the  Discharger added a high flow 
alarm to alert plant staff when an increase d discharge flow necessitates an 
increased sodium bisu lfite dosage rate. The Discharger also established 
standard operating procedures directing plant staff to operate the sodium 
hypochlorite and sodium bisulfite pumps manual ly when their analyzers are 
offline and , as a precaution , to increase the sodium bisulfite dosage above the 
current level needed. There have been no further exceedances of the chlorine 
residual  effluent limit ation .

2.5. Sea Level Rise . The Facility (i.e., both the plant and collection system) is 
vulnerable to sea level rise. In 2018, t he Discharger completed  a 100 percent  
design plan for a flood wall around the perimeter of the plant  that would protect  it 
against two feet (24 inches) of sea level rise. The Discharger based this design on 
an analysis completed in 2016 that projected  a mid-to-high range of two feet  of 
sea le vel rise by 2050 .
The Discharger has not established plans to mitigat e sea level rise with regard to  
its collection system or its discharge. The Discharger states that its Wastewater  
Treatment Master Plan, scheduled for completion in 2024, will address sea level 
rise and plant hydraulics.  The Dis charger also states  it is coordinating with the City 
of Vallejo to develop a standardized  approach to sea level rise for new 
infrastructure associated with development.
2.6. Recycling . The Discharger uses  about 600,000 gallons  per year of its treated 
effluent onsite for Facility operations but does not current ly produce or distribute 
recycled water.  The Discharger  stated during the Regional Water Board’s 2022 
Compliance Evaluation Inspection of the Facility that there are few opportunities 
for recycling water off -site because the City of Vallejo has strong water rights from 
several sources and demand for recycled water is therefore relatively low. In 
addition, the Discharger would need to add filtration and additional disinfection to 
its treatment system to produce recycled water that would meet C alifornia Code of 
Regulations  Title 22 requirements for unrestricted non -potable reuse  (Vallejo 
Flood and Wastewater District Recycled Water Facilities Plan , HydroScience, 
March 2018) .
