FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment  
1. Location and Service Area . The wastewater treatment plant is located at 2500 
Pittsburg -Antioch Highway on the border of Pittsburg and Antioch (see 
Attachment  B, Figure B -1). It provides primary and secondary treatment of 
wastewater from  the cities of Pittsburg  and Antioch, and the  unincorporated 
community of Bay Point . The plant also provides tertiary -treated recycled water, 
primarily to two local power plants for reuse in cooling towers. The  Discharger ser ves 
a population of approximately 213,000.  
2. Collection System s. The Discharger ow ns and operates approximately 76 miles of 
gravity and pressure sewer lines , five  pump stations , and one flow equalization 
station . The c ities of Pittsburg and Antioch own and operate about 170 miles  and 
320 miles of sewer lines.  
3. Wastewater Treatment . The Discharger treats all  influent to secondary treatment 
standards  at its plant. T he treatment process  include s screening and grit removal, 
primary clarification, biological treatment using trickling towers and/or aeration 
basins  (parallel  treatme nt processes) , secondary clarification, disinfection using

Following secondary clarification, the Discharger sends approximately half the 
plant’s flow to its Recycled Water Facility  (further described in Section II.A.5 below)  
for tertiary treatment and beneficial reuse.  
Two power plants , the Delta Energy Center and the Los Medanos Energy Center,  use 
recycled wat er and  return about 1.9 MGD of cooling tower blowdown water to the 
plant . The plant also receives a small , high- dissolved- solids  waste stream of industrial  
brine ( approximately  15,000 gallons per day)  from  the Corteva Agriscience – 
Pittsburg Operations  plant. The Discharger typically combine s these flow s with 
secondary -treated water  prior to disinfection, al though it may route them to the plant 
headworks for treatment . 
The plant  has an average dry weather design flow of 19.5 MGD and a peak wet 
weather capacity of 31.1 MGD. From October 2014 through May 2019, the plant treated a daily average of about 13 MGD;  the highest  reported average daily flow was 
22.1 MGD. The average flow sent t o the Recycled Water Facility  for tertiary 
treatment and distribution to customers over the same period was 5.8 MGD , with a 
maximum of 12.4 MGD.  
4. Sludge and Biosolids Management . Primary solids are sent to the anaerobic 
digesters, while secondary solids are  first concentrated using gravity belt thickeners, 
then anaerobically digested. Digested biosolids are dewatered by centrifuge and either hauled by truck for land application, sent to a separate composting facility, or sent to a landfill by a contractor. G rit and screenings from the wastewater treatment process 
are also sent to a landfill.  
5. Recycl ing. The treatment plant produces recycled wastewater under the Regional 
Water Board’s General Water Reuse Requirements for Municipal Wastewater and Water Agencies  (Order No. 96- 011) and the State Water Board’s General Waste 
Discharge Requirements for Landscape Irrigation Uses of Municipal Recycled Water  
(Order No. 2009- 0006- DWQ). During this Order’s term, the Discharger’s coverage 
under these permits will be replace d by coverage under the State Water Board’s 
Water Reclamation Requirements for Recycled Water Use (Order No.  WQ 
2016- 0068- DDW). The Discharger will receive written notification regarding when 
this change will take effect.  
The Recycled Water Facility’s treatment process consists of flocculation and clarification, sand filtration, and chlorination. The Discharger routes waste flows generated by tertiary treatment to the plant headworks upstream of the influent sampler.  Recycled Water Facility effluent meets the tertiary treatment standards of 
California Code of Regulations, Title 22, Division 4, section 60301.230.

6. Stormwater Management. All stormwater from the plant property  is directed to the

B. Discharge Point s and Receiving Waters  
Plant e ffluent is discharged to New York Slough through a deep- water diffuser about 
500 feet offshore . The diffuser is a 42 -inch diameter iron pipe about 400 feet in length at a 
depth of about 26 feet  below the mean water level . The diffuser consists of 50 ports space d 
eight feet apart in alternating directions. The diffuser port openings are three inches in diameter. The Discharger inspects the diffuser annually.

average age of  collection systems  in the San Francisco Bay Region as a whole. 
The Discharger ’s sewer system management program is intended to ensur e adequate 
capacity to conv ey peak wastewater flows, reduc e and prevent  SSOs, and effectively 
mitigate  any SSOs that do occur. The program consists of regular inspection and 
rehabilitation of  pipes, collection system cleaning every three to five years, root 
intrusion control, and community outreach to minimize fats, oils, and greases. Historically, the Discharger  has had minimal reportable SSOs and very low inflow 
and infiltration in to its sys tem.
