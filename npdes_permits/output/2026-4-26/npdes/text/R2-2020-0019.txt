FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment  
1. Location and Service Area . The plant  is located at 500 Davidson Street in Novato, Marin 
County. It provides secondary treatment of wastewater from domestic  and non- domestic 
wastewater from the City of Novato and adjacent unincorporated Marin County. The Facility 
serves a population of a pproximately 60,000. 
2. Collection System . The collection system consists of approximately 212 miles of gravity 
sewer  mains, 23 miles of pressure sewer mains, and 39 pump stations.  
3. Wastewater Treatment . The plant can provide secondary treatment for an averag e daily dry 
weather design flow of 7.0 million gallons per day (MGD) and a sustained three -hour peak 
wet weather flow of 47 MGD. Between September 2015 and July 2019, the plant treated a daily average flow of 5.0  MGD, with the maximum daily flow peaking at  30.3 MGD.  
The treatment process consists of influent screening and grit removal, primary clarification, activated sludge secondary treatment  with nitrification and partial denitrification , secondary 
clarification, and ultraviolet (UV) disinfection.

5. Stormwater Management . All stormwater  at the plant is collected and directed to the 
headworks for treatment ; therefore, no additional stormwater requirements are necessary .  
6. Water Recycling and Reclamation . The Discharger operates a recycled water treatment 
system . Regional Water Bo ard Order No. 96- 011 regulates the production of up to 1.7 MGD 
of disinfected tertiary recycled water f or the North Marin Water District for subsequent 
distribution to landscape irrigation customers.  
The Discharger has two storage ponds with a combined st orage capacity of 180 million 
gallons and an irrigation pump station. The effluent from these ponds meets California Code of Regulations, Title 22, section 60304(d) standards . Preceding or during the dry season 
(typically between June  1 and August 31), the  Discharger diverts secondary- treated treated 
effluent to its storage ponds  for subsequent  pumping to approximately 800 acres of 
pasturelands. The pasturelands are comprised of Site 2 (161 acres), which the Discharger  
owns, and Sites 3 (234 acres) and 7 (409 acres), which Marin County lease s to the 
Discharger. This lease will expire in 2023. The pastureland irrigation is regulated under Regional Water Board Order No. 92 -065.  
During the dry season, the  Discharger also provides about 221,000 gallons per day of 
secondary treated effluent to the Bel Marin Keys Unit V restoration site (see Attachment  B). 
The effluent provides  soil moisture conditioning and compaction, dust control, and 
vegetation irrigation  as part of Phase 1 of the California State Coastal Conservancy’s  Bel 
Marin Keys Unit V wetland restoration project. This operation is scheduled to end after the 
2020 dry season per the Agreement for Reclaimed Water Use B etween Novato Sanitary 
District and  California State Coastal Conservancy (September 23, 2019).  
The Discharger continues to expand its recycled water program. Since 2013, the Discharger 
has produced disinfected tertiary recycled water for the  North Marin Water District. As of  
2019, the capacity of the Discharger’s recycled water treatment system doubled from 0.85 MGD to 1.7 MGD.  
B. Discharge Point and Receiving Waters  
Secondary -treated effluent is discharged to  San Pablo Bay through a multiport diffuser currently 
located 950 feet offshore at Discharge Point No. 001. The diffuser is submerged at the 1 foot above mean lower low water tidal elevation. At lower tidal elevations, the outfall is exposed, and the distance from the diffuser to the San Pablo Bay water line can range from 1,000 to 3,500 feet.

Planned Changes  
The Discharger plans to relocate its discharge location to create and support new brackish marsh 
habitat as part of the State Coastal Conservancy’s Bel Marin Keys Unit V wetland restoration project as described in Fact Sheet section II.C.  
The Marin County Flood Control and Water Conservation District  has long -term plans to 
implement tidal wetland restoration projects on Sites 3 and 7 of the Di scharger ’s pasture 
irrigation lands  (see Fact Sheet section II.A.6) . Implementing these projects would require the 
Discharger to c ease pasture irrigation , which could occur  during this Order term. T he Discharger 
relied on  pasture irrigation  to comply with the previous order’s discharge prohibition to San 
Pablo Bay during the dry season ( typically between June 1 and August 31) . The previous order 
authorized year -round discharge to San Pablo Bay upon the Discharger’s completion of its 
outfall relocation (from Discharge Point No. 001 to Discharge Point No. 002) to support the Bel Marin Keys Unit V wetland restoration project. To accommodate the wetland restoration projects 
on Sites 3 and 7, this Order authorizes the Discharger to discharge year -round to San Pablo Bay
