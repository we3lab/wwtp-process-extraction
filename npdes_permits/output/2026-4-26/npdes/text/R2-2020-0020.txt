FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
This section provides informatio n about the Facility and Planned Facility. Requirements related 
to the Planned Facility apply only if the Discharger satisfies Provision VI.C.5.c  of the Order .  
A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment  
1. Location and Service Area  
a. Facility.  The existing wastewater treatment plant is located at  1220 Avenue  M on 
Treasure Island in San Francisco . It provides secondary treatment o f domestic and 
commercial wastewater from  Treasure Island and Yerba Buena Island, located in 
San Francisco Bay . The Facility serves a population of approximately 2,500. A  
few commercial facilities exist within the Facility’s  service area, but  no 
manufacturing industries. 
b. Planned Facility.  The Treasure Island Water Resource Recovery Facility will be 
located adjacent to the existing pla nt and will provide tertiary treatment of 
wastewater from Treasure Island and Yerba Buena Island. The Planned Facility is 
anticipated to serve 8,000 homes, up to three hotels, and various restaurants, 
retail, and outdoor recreation and entertainment venues . 
2. Collection System s 
a. Facility . Treasure Island has separate stormwater and sanitary sewer system s. The 
Discharger transferred ownership of approximately 70 percent of the former 
Treasure Island naval base, including the underlying collection system, to TIDA 
between May 2015 and October 2019. The portions of collection system owned by the Discharger and TIDA are both subject to the statewide WDRs described in Provision VI.C.4.b. 
The entire wastewater collection system , including the separate portions owned 
by the U.S. Navy and TIDA, consists of  approximately 15 miles of gravity sewer 
mains and 4 miles of force mains, with pipes ranging in diameter from 4 to 36 
inches. Pipeline materials include cast iron, plastic, vitrified clay, and asbestos 
cement. In addition to gravity sewers, the system includes 25 sanitary sewer pump 
stations, two  of which are on Yer ba Buena Island. Wastewater from the southern 
portion of Yerba Buena Island flows to Treasure Island through a 6- inch 
submarine force main. Wastewater from the northern portion of Yerba Buena Island flows to Treasure Island through a 6 -inch gravity sewer.

3. Wastewater Treatment  
a. Facility.  The existing wastewater treatment plant was initially constructed in 
1961 to provide primary treatment and was later upgraded to provide secondary treatment and disinfection. The plant  is now  designed to provide secondary 
treatment for an average of 2.0 million gallons per day (MGD). A s indicated in 
the study “Improving Process Reliability and Performance Through Flow Equalization” (Nolte & Associates, April 1991), the plant can provide secondary treatment for up to 4.4  MGD during wet weather . From April 2015 through 
November 201 9, daily in fluent flows ranged between 0.10 and 1.6 MGD, with an 
average of 0.3 4 MGD.  
Existing plant  operations and processes include  preliminary treatment with bar 
screening and grit removal , primary treatment with primary sedimentation tanks , 
secondary treatment  with  trickling filters and secondary sedimentation , 
disinfection with sodium hypochlorite , and dechlorination with sodium bisulfite. 
Treated wastewater is routed to Discharge Point No. 001.  
b. Planned Facility.  The Planned Facility will  be designed to  treat all wastewater to  
tertiary treatment standards. It is being designed for  an average dry weather flow 
of 1.3 MGD and a peak wet weather flow of 3.9 MGD.  
Planned Facility operations and processes are anticipated to  include an 
equalizatio n basin for storage of instantaneous peak flows; preliminary treatment, 
including screening and grit removal; secondary treatment by means of a 
membrane bioreactor (MBR), consisting of activated sludge biological process basins and submerged membrane filtr ation trains; and ultraviolet disinfection.  
Effluent recycled for use onsite or  distributed to other users will be chlorinated. 
Effluent in excess of recycled water demands will not be chlorinated and will be 
pumped to a constructed wetland adjacent to the new treatment plant for polishing 
treatment. After wetland treatment, the treated effluent will be routed to 
Discharge Point No. 002. 
To adapt to rising sea levels, the Planned Facility will adher e to the City and 
County of San Francisco’s Guidance for Incorporating Sea Level Rise into 
Capital Planning in San Francisco. The Planned Facility will accommodate 39 
inches of sea level rise, plus 6 inches of freeboard.

Solids and sludge from the Facility are sent to  Oceanside Water Pollution Control 
Plant in San Francisco for treatment and disposal . Solids from the Planned Facility 
will also be sent offsite, although the location has not yet been determined. 
5. Recycl ing 
a. Facility. T he Facility does not produce recycled wastewater . 
b. Planned Facility.  The Planned F acility will be designed to achieve  the 
disinfected  tertiary treatment standards of California Code of Regulations, Title 
22, Division 4, section 60301.230. The average annual recycled water demand is 
anticipated to be 0.43 MGD, with a peak flow of 0.98 MGD. The recycled water 
system will serve indoor and outdoor non- potable water demands, including 
landscape irrigation, urban farming, and dual plumbing. Prior to recycl ing water 
from the  Planned F acility, the Discharger  must apply for coverage under the State 
Water Board’s Water Reclamation Requirements for Recycled Water Use ( Order 
No. WQ  2016- 0068- DDW).  
6. Stormwater Management  
a. Facility.  All drains within the existing plant boundary convey  stormwater flows 
to the treatment plant headworks; however, some stormwater from within the plant boundary may also flow  over land to storm drains outside of the treatment 
plant perimeter. The Discharger maintains coverage for its stormwater discharges 
through enrollment in the Phase II Small Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System 
(MS4) Program, NPDES No. CAS000004. 
b. Planned Facility.  All onsite stormwater at the Planned Facility will be captured 
in a catchment basin and conveyed to the plant headworks for treatment. Rainfall 
onto the treatment wetlands will be captured and discharged.  
B. Discharge Point s and Receiving Waters  
1. Facility.  Plant effluent is discharged to Central San Francisco Bay through a deep- water 
outfall about 460 feet offshore  at a depth of 30 feet below mean lower low water . The 
outfall is a single port “duckbill” valve, angled 45 degrees up from horizontal, which expands  with increasing flow. Its effective diameter ranges from about 6.2 inches to 
7.6 inches.  
2. Planned Facility. The Planned  Facility will discharge effluent from the plant’s 
treatment wetland through a shallow -water outfall to Central San Francisco Bay.  Due to  
the variability of receiving water surface elevation with tides, the 42 -inch outfall pipe 
may be fully submerged, partially submerged, or not submerged.

In response to the Discharger’s high SSO rate  from 2007 through 2015, and to 
monitoring and reporting violations noted during an August 2015 collection system 
inspection, a Notice of Violation  was issued  in October 2015. The Notice of  Violation 
require d the Discharger and TIDA to submit a technical report  outlining corrective 
actions to upgrade  the collection system s and address inspection findings . It also 
continues to require the Discharger to submit regular status reports documentin g 
progress toward completing corrective actions.  
The Discharger has made progress toward addressing the issues identified in the 
October 2015 Notice of Violation. It has improved its processes for monitoring and 
reporting collection system problems, has id entified high -priority areas of its 
collection system that require rehabilitation or replacement, and has budgeted 
substantial funding for these upgrades based on estimated costs. In coordination with 
TIDA and the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, the Discharger has developed a rehabilitation and replacement plan ; the parties mee t regularly to update 
priorities based on system changes and planned redevelopment. Additionally, t he 
Discharger submit s semi -annual status reports regarding implementation of 
prioritized projects .
