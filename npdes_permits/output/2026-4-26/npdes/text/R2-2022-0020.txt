FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and Treatment
2.1.1.  Location and Service Are a. The wastewater treatment plant is located at  5019 
Imhoff Place in Martinez. It provides secondary treatment of domestic,  
commercial, and industrial wastewater for Danville, Lafayette, Martinez,  
Moraga, Orinda, Pleasant Hill, San Ramon, Walnut Creek, Concord, Clayton,  
and adjacent unincorporated areas. The Discharger also regulates 19  
signific ant industrial users that discharge to the Facility through its  pretreatment 
program. The Facility serves a population of about 484,800.  Attachment B 
shows a map of the area around the Facility.
2.1.2  Collection System . The collection system is a separate sewer system  
comprised of approximately 1,500 miles of publicly -owned pipes that range  from 
6 to 102 inches in diameter. The Discharger owns and operates 15  pump 
stations and operates two privately -owned pump stations in Orinda.  This 
collection system is p art of the Facility covered by this Order. The City of  
Concord owns and maintains the collection system within the Concord city  limits 
and maintains the collection system serving the City of Clayton.  The portions of 
the collection system owned or operated by the City of  Concord are not covered 
by this Order.
2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment . The plant can provide secondary treatment for an 
average daily dry weather design flow of 53.8 million gallons per day (MGD). 
From May 2020 through April 2021, the average d aily influent flow was 
34.2 MGD. The wastewater treatment process consists of screening, grit 
removal, primary clarification, secondary biological treatment, secondary 
clarification, and ultraviolet (UV) disinfection. Attachment C shows a flow 
process diag ram for the Facility.
The Facility also has holding basins with a combined capacity of 168 million 
gallons. The Discharger uses these holding basins to temporarily store peak 
wet weather flows when primary -treated wastewater exceeds the secondary 
treatment  capacity. When flows subside, the Discharger routes stored 
wastewater back to the headworks for full primary and secondary treatment.
2.1.4. Recycled Water . From 2017 through 2020, the Discharger annually diverted, 
on average, approximately 5  percent  of its total effluent, or 533 million gallons, 
to its Recycling Plant. The Recycling Plant further treats the UV -disinfected 
effluent with sand/anthracite filtration and sodium hypochlorite disinfection and 
stores recycled water in a covered seven -million -gallon reservoir prior to 
distribution. The Discharger uses about two -thirds of the recycled water 
produced for washdown operations at the wastewater treatment plant and 
distributes the remaining recycled water offsite, primarily for landscape 
irrigation. Irri gation customers include schools, parks, corporation yards, 
landscape medians, and commercial businesses. Light industrial uses include a

2.1.5. Sludge and Biosolids Management . The Discharger thickens secondary 
sludge with a dissolved air flotation unit, combines secondary sludge with 
primary sludge that it treats with lime, and dewaters the combined sludge with 
centrifuges. The Discharger incinerates the final dewatered sludge o nsite, 
which produces ash. The Discharger hires a contractor to truck the ash to an 
offsite recycling facility for use as a soil amendment. If Facility incinerators are 
inoperable, the Discharger may haul biosolids to a landfill, an organic materials 
recov ery facility, or the East Bay Municipal Utility District wastewater treatment 
plant .
2.1.6. Stormwater Management . The Discharger is not required to be covered under 
the statewide NPDES permit for stormwater discharges associated with 
industrial activities , General Permit for Storm Water Discharges Associated with 
Industrial Activities, Order 2014 -0057 -DWQ (NPDES Permit 
CAS000001) ,because all stormwater at the plant is collected and directed to the 
plant’s headworks for treatment.
2.2. Discharge Point and R eceiving Waters . Discharge of treated effluent to Suisun 
Bay is through a submerged, multi -port diffuser,  located approximately 1,600 feet 
offshore (Discharge Point 001) at an average  depth of about 24 feet. The diffuser 
is 6 feet in diameter and embedded 4 feet into the  sediment. The diffuser is 
oriented nearly perpendicular to the shoreline. It consists of  11 upward -facing 
18-inch diameter circular ports, separated 11.5 feet on center, for a  total length of 
115 feet. Based on the outfall configuration, th e discharge is considered  a deep 
water discharge as defined in the Basin Plan.
About every 5 to 10 years, during the dry season, the Discharger drains and  
inspects its 3.5 -mile long, 72 -inch reinforced concrete outfall pipe to verify the  
alignment and asse ss the physical integrity of the pipe joint seals. During this  time, 
the Discharger  redirects  fully-treated effluent to a holding basin for discharge  to 
Walnut Creek via a concrete weir structure.  The Discharger conducted an  
inspection (and any necessary repairs identified as a result) in 2020. It took 
approximately 14 weeks to dewater the outfall,  inspect it, repair the damaged 
joints, and return it to service.

2.5. Sea Level Rise . The Discharger most recently completed a sea level rise impact 
evaluation in 2017 as part of its Comprehensive Wastewater Master Plan

system.  The Discha rger provided  the following  findings in response to a sea level 
rise questionnaire the Regional Water Board sent on April 14, 2021:
·Levees built along Walnut Creek and Grayson Creek protect the Discharger’s 
wastewater treatment plant. The existing levees d o not currently provide 3 feet 
of freeboard protection during a 100 -year flood recurrence interval. To address 
flood risk , the Discharger has been collaborating with the  Contra Costa Flood 
Protection and Control District to design and implement improvement  projects 
to provide 3 feet of freeboard protection for a 500 -year flood recurrence 
interval. Additionally, the Discharger has an existing levee along Pacheco 
Slough that does not provide adequate protection for flood risks. The 
Discharger is working to im prove this levee to provide a similar  level of 
protection as the improved Walnut Creek and Grayson Creek levees.  The 
improved levees around the Discharger’s treatment plant are expected to 
provide protection against sea level rise.
·In the Discharger’s 2017  Comprehensive Wastewater Master Plan , it identified 
four pump stations that may be susceptible to future sea level rise impacts 
within the cities of Martinez and Clyde. Projects to enhance protection from sea 
level rise at three of  the pump stations a re currently in the design phase.  The 
fourth pump station, located in Clyde, will be reevaluated in the Discharger’s 
next Collection System Master Plan Update.
The Discharger plans to re -evaluate sea level rise impacts  on its collection 
system within the next two years as part of its Collection System Master Plan 
Update. The Discharger is also continuing its planning efforts to evaluate sea 
level rise impacts on the treatment plant .
2.6.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes . The Discharger c ontinues to promote and support recycled 
water planning efforts in conjunction with local water agencies. This includes 
leading the continued evaluation of the Refinery Recycled Water Exchange Project 
– an advanced recycled water treatment project (includi ng nutrient removal) that 
would produce approximately 13.5 MGD of recycled water for the  Contra Costa 
Water District to serve to its two existing refinery customers . The freed -up water 
supply could then be conveyed to the Santa Clara Valley Water District.  In 
response to interest in recycled water from cities within the service area, the 
Discharger has also initiated an evaluation to determine the most cost -effective 
method to provide recycled water to portions of the cities of Walnut Creek, 
Lafayette , and Orinda , and the Town of Moraga. Moreover, t he Discharger 
continues to  collaborate with a local golf course to consider the viability of a 
satellite water recycling facility, which could divert wastewater from the collection 
system and treat it onsite to produce recycled water for golf course irrigation and 
return the recycling facility process  solids to the collection system.
