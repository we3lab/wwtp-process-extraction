FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection  and Treatment
2.1.1.  Location and Service Are a. The wastewater treatment plant is located at 
11 Tennent Avenue in Pinole. It provides secondary treatment of domestic , 
commercial , and industrial  wastewater for the cities of Pinole and Hercules in 
Contra Co sta County. The Facility serves a population of about 4 5,000 people . 
Attachment B shows a map of the area around the Facility .
2.1.2  Collection System . The wastewater treatment plant receives flows from two 
collection systems. The City of Pinole’s collection system is a separate sewer 
system comprised of approximately 51 miles of pipes and 2 pump stations. The 
City of Hercules’ collection system is a sepa rate sewer system comprised of 
approximately 58 miles of pipes and 6 pump stations , covered separately under 
State Water Board Order WQ 2022 0103 DWQ (Statewide Waste Discharge 
Requirements General Order for Sanitary Sewer Systems) . The cities own and 
operate the collection systems within their city limits.
2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment . The wastewater treatment process consists of influent 
screening, grit removal, primary clarification , activated sludge biological 
treatment (with Modified Ludzack -Ettinger pr ocess for 
nitrification/denitrification),  secondary clarification,  disinfection with sodium 
hypochlorite, and dechlorination using sodium bisulfite. Attachment C provides 
the treatment plant flow schematic.  The treated effluent (disinfected and 
dechlorinat ed) is pumped through an effluent force main to Rodeo, where it is 
combined with Rodeo Sanitary District effluent and discharged through a 
deepwater outfall/diffuser to San Pablo Bay. The treatment plant has an 
average daily dry weather treatment capacity of up to 4.06 million gallons per 
day (MGD) and a peak wet weather capacity of 20 MGD.  During wet weather, 
the Discharger routes  flows that exceed 12 MGD directly to its activated sludge  
biological treatment  system  (after screening and grit removal) . From November 
2021 through October 20 22, the plant treated a daily average of 2.7 MGD.
2.1.4. Sludge and Biosolids Management . The Discharger thickens primary and 
secondary sludge with a gravity thickener, treats the combined sludge with 
anaerobic digesti on to a Class B level , and dewaters the combined sludge with 
a centrifuge. Biosolids are currently hauled to  the Keller Canyon  Landfill for 
disposal . The Discharger is considering c omposting and land application of 
biosolids for future disposal practices.
2.1.5. Stormwater Management . All stormwater flows in contact with equipment or 
wastewater at the treatment plant are collected and directed to the plant 
headworks for treatment. Therefore, no additional stormwater requirements are 
necessary.

2.5. Sea Level Rise . The Discharger  has not yet developed a climate change action or 
resiliency plan for the Facility  and its collection system.  In its response to a sea 
level rise questionnaire the Regional Water Board sent on April 14, 2021, the 
Discharger noted that  the Facility  is located outside the 500-year flood zone and is 
protected by a levee from a 100-year flood. However, it is potentially subject to 
flooding from Pinole Creek when a tidal surge or heavy rain occurs. The Facility  is 
also susceptible to sea level rise  at its western boundary . When planning  future 
upgrades and capital improvement project s, the Discharger  indicates that it  will 
consider the impacts of  flooding from  Pinole Creek and sea level rise  on its 
western boundary . For example, grading will be designed to protect against the 
100-year flood elevation and 2050 sea -level rise projections .
2.6. Recycled Water.  The Discharger uses  treated wastewater onsite for Facility 
operations but does not currently produce or distribute r ecycled water.  The 
Discharger has allocated  funds for a recycled water feasibility study, which it 
expect s to complete by the end of 2024 .

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes . The Discharger  recently completed system upgrades to 
increase the Facility’s wet weather capacity up to 20 MGD and plans several 
improvements for the next permit term :
·Effluent Outfall Eductor Station : The Dischar ger is working with the Rodeo 
Sanitary District to coordin ate the replacement of a section of outfall piping at 
the Rodeo Eductor Station to increase the wet weather effluent pumping 
capacity . The Effluent Pump Station is currently capable of conveying 
11.5 MGD through the deepwater outfall. The pipe will be upsi zed to achieve a 
pumping capacity of 13.9 MGD .
·Replacement of Air Release Valves : Four air release valves found on the 
effluent pipeline between the Facility and the Effluent Outfall in Rodeo are in 
poor condition . The project includes removal and replacem ent of the pipe 
saddle, short pipe section, isolation valve, and air relief valve.
These changes are mentioned here for informational purposes only and are not 
requirements of this Order, except to the extent that they pertain to ensuring 
Facility reliabil ity. Their inclusion here does not imply Regional Water Board 
authorization. The Discharger may need to obtain necessary permits or permit 
modifications to implement the changes .
