FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and Treatment
2.1.1.  Location and Service Are a. The Discharger was formed under a Joint Powers 
Agreement in 1979 and is comprised of six member agencies: Almonte Sanitary 
District, Alto Sanitary District, Homestead Valley Sanitary District, City of Mill 
Valley, Richardson Bay Sanitary District, and Tamalpais Community Services 
District.  The Discharger owns and operates the treatment plant and its 
associated wastewater collection and conveyance system. The treatment plant, 
located within the City of Mill Valley  at 450 Sycamore Avenue , provides 
secondary treatment of domestic wastewater for its six member agencies. The 
service area population is approximately 29,000. Attachment B provides a map 
of the area aro und the Facility.
2.1.2  Collection System . The Discharger owns and operates sewer pipelines that 
collect and convey wastewater from the member agencies’ satellite collection 
systems to the treatment plant. The Discharger’s conveyance system consists 
of approximately 3.5 miles of force mains, 5.5 mi les of gravity lines, and six 
pump stations. Under agreement with the City of Mill Valley, the Discharger 
operates two lift stations within the Mill Valley service area (i.e., the Frontage 
Road Lift Station and Shelter Bay Lift Sta tion). Each satellite collection system 
is owned and operated independently from the Discharger and collects 
wastewater from its respective service area. These collection systems, and the 
Discharger’s wastewater collection and conveyance system, are covere d under 
the Statewide Waste Discharge Requirements General Order for Sanitary 
Sewer Systems ( Order WQ 2022 -0103 -DWQ ).
2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment . The treatment plant has a dry weather design capacity 
of 3.6 million gallons per day (MGD) and can treat up to 24.7 MGD during wet 
weather (based on equalized flow capacity of the treatment plant) when the 
influent concentration of organic matter is lower.  From May 1, 20 20, through 
April 30, 20 23, the average daily flow was 2. 5 MGD and the maximum daily flow 
was 30 MGD. The wastewater treatment process consists of screening, grit 
removal, flow equalization, primary sedimentation , biological treatment (trickling 
filters), secondary clarification, disinfection (chlorination), gravity thickening, and 
solids digestion and dewatering. Chlorine contact is accomplished in the 
effluent force main, which is approximately 6.5 miles long, a nd dechlorination is 
accomplished by sodium bisulfite addition at Sanitary District No. 5 of Marin 
County (Tiburon) prior to discharge. Attachment C of this Order contains a 
treatment process schematic diagram.
During wet weather, when primary -treated flows exceed the plant’s biological 
treatment capacity of 24.7 MGD and all onsite storage is used, the effluent from 
the primary clarifiers may bypass the trickling filters and secondary clarifiers 
and mix with biol ogically -treated effluent before disinfection and discharge. The

2.1.4. Sludge and Biosolids Management . Solids removed from the wastewater 
stream are treated by gravity thickening, primary and secondary digestion, and 
dewatering by belt filter press. Dewatered biosolids are sent to the Redwood 
Sanitary Landfill in Novato, where they are composted with yard w aste and 
used for daily cover. The Discharger currently generates and disposes of  about 
251 dry tons of biosolids per year.
2.1.5. Stormwater Management . The wastewater treatment plant headworks 
receives drainage from the area in front of the solids handling units (i.e., belt 
filter press, grit and screening, chemical feed room) and the area surrounding 
the dump station for septic haulers. The remaining sto rm drains at the treatment 
plant are not connected to the headworks. The Discharger is covered under the 
State Water Board’s statewide industrial stormwater NPDES permit (NPDES 
General Permit CAS000001) for stormwater runoff f rom areas that do not drain 
to the headworks.
2.1.6.  Recycled Water Activities.  The Discharger’s water recycling facility provides 
additional treatment of the wastewater treatment plant produced secondary 
effluent via coagulation, sand filtration, and chorine disinfection. The Discharger 
currently recycles wastewater under the Regiona l Water Board General Water 
Reuse Requirements for Municipal Wastewater and Water Agencies 
Order  96-011, although expects to transition its coverage to the State Water 
Board General Water Reclamation Requirements for Recyc led Water Use 
Order WQ 2016 -0068 -DDW during the next permit term. An average of 
0.03 MGD of d isinfected tertiary treated recycled water is typically  provided for 
landscape irrigation  and environmental enhancement  (about 7 MG/year 
between April and November ). The Discharger also distributes disinfected 
secondary -23 treated recycled water via their commercial truck fill station for 
sanitary sewer system cleaning uses.
2.2. Discharge Point and Receiving Waters . The Discharger shares an outfall with 
the Sanitary District No. 5 of Marin County Wastewater Treatment Plant in Tiburon 
(regulated under NPDES Permit No. CA0037753). Discharge of treated effluent to 
Raccoon Strait in Central San Francisco Bay is through a 36 -inch outfall and a 
submerged, multi -port diffuser (Discharge Point 001) located about 8 50 feet 
offshore. The diffuser spans 195 feet and consists of 15 risers, each with four 
3-inch di ameter ports submerged approximately 92 feet below mean sea level.

From 2019 to 2023, the Discharger exceeded its ammonia effluent limits a total 
of 22 times.  The Discharger’s trickling filters provide nitrification (ammonia 
removal), and i n 2018 , the Discharger  replaced the media in both trickling filters 
with improperly configured filter media based on recommendations from an 
engineering contractor. The Discharger believes this replacement diminished 
the trickling filters’ capability to nitrify ammonia . In a letter dated April 14, 2022, 
the Discharger  notified the Region al Water Board that they had filed a claim  
with the engineering contractor and would begin replacement of the filter media 
in the summer of 2022 . The letter also  outlined the  Discharger’s  interim 
measures to prevent or minimize violations during the media replacement ; 
however, the Discharger was still unable to meet the ammonia effluent 
limitations during the replacement project . The Discharger began the installation 
of properly configured filter media  on April 1 7, 2022, and  completed the 
replacement on September 22, 2022.  Since project completion , the Discharger 
has met its ammonia effluent limitations, excluding  two exc eedances  related to 
a wet weather event .

On October 24, 2021, a heavy rainstorm caus ed the Discharger to bypass on 
biological treatment on  October 24. The blended effluent sample had an 
enterococcus bacteria result that caused the Discharger to exceed the monthly 
geometric mean enterococcus effluent limit  in October 2021 . In response, t he 
Discharger increased the chlorine dosage, which resulted in compliant  
enterococci results. 
On October 19, 2022, the Discharger violated  the minimum effluent limit for pH 
during the adjustment of the Facility’s operation with the new trickling filter 
media. The Discharger was operating the trickling filters in series to achieve 
greater ammonia removal. However, ammonia nitrification  consumes  alkalinity 
(which buffers pH) and the increased nitrification subsequently dropped the pH 
in the treated effluent. Once discovered, the Discharger changed the trickling 
filters’ configuration from series to parallel and the effluent pH returned to 
compliance.
On December 30, 2022, a heavy rainstorm caused a significant disruption to 
Facility operations.  During this storm, a 30-inch plug valve broke in the closed 
position, which prevented the Facility from recirculating the trickling filter 
effluent.  This lack of process control led to fluctuations in secondary clarifier 
flow, which reduced solids removal.  As a result, t his led to four total coliform 
effluent limit exceedances in January 2023, BOD 5 and ammonia effluent limit 
exceedances in February 2023, and the ammonia effluent limit exceedance in 
April 2023. The Discharger repaired the valve on February 24, 2023,  but noted 
that optimizing the trickling filters ’ ammonia removal was a slow process , 
resulting in the April 2023 exceedance.
In March 2023, consecutive rain events  reduced influent BOD 5 and TSS 
concentrations . These decreased concentrations provided less BOD 5 and TSS 
for the Facility to remove, and the Discharger violated its average monthly 
minimum percent removal effluent limitations for BOD 5 and TSS.  The 
Discharger maintained compliance with the BOD 5 and TSS concentration -based 
effluent limits  (in mg/L)  during March 2023 and returned to compliance  for 
monthly percent removal  in April.

2.6. Sea Level Rise . In its response to a sea level rise questionnaire the Regional 
Water Board sent on April 14, 2021, the Discharge r noted that portions of the 
Facility are within the 100 -year flood zone. To protect the Facility against a 
100-year flood, the Discharger’s 2014 Master Plan recommended to build a 3-foot 
berm around the north and west parts of the facility which would provide  2 feet of 
protection above  the FEMA 100 -year flood ’s expected height . The Discharger is  
budgeted to complete the berm design during fiscal year 2023 -2024, which is 
within the current safety cycle (2021 -2025). The Discharger expects c onstruction 
of the berm to occur during the next safety cycle (2026 -2030).
