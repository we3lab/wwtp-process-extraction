FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Sanitary Sewer System  and Treatment
2.1.1.  Location and Service Are a. The plant is located at 400 East Millbrae Avenue  
in the City of Millbrae . It provides secondary treatment of domestic, commercial, 
and industrial wastewater for the City of Millbrae and serves a population of 
approximately 23,200 . No significant industrial users are located within the 
service area.
Attachment B provides a map of the area around the treatment plant .
2.1.2 . Sanitary Sewer System . The City of Millbrae ’s sanitary sewer system is 
comprised of approximately 59 miles of gravity sewer pipes, 22 miles of lower 
laterals, and 3 pump stations  and approximately 1 mile of associated force 
mains . 
2.1.3.  Wastewater Treatment . The treatment process consists of preliminary 
screening  (two fine bar screens and one coarse bar screen) , grit removal  (two 
vortex -type grit removal devices) , influent flow equalization  (two tanks with a 
maximum  combined  volume of 1.2 million gallons) , primary sedimentation  (two 
primary clarifiers) , biological activated sludge treatment (two aeration basins) , 
secondary clarification  (two secondary clarifiers) , and sodium hypochlorite 
disinfection. After disinfection, the plant routes effluent to the South San 
Francisco  / San Bruno Water Qua lity Control Plant through the NBSU force 
main for dechlorination. The plant can provide secondary treatment for an 
average dry weather design flow of 3.0 million gallons per day (MGD)  and a 
peak wet weather capacity of 9.0 MGD . From May 2019 to September  2023, 
the plant treated a daily average of 1.5 MGD, with the highest reported average 
daily flow being 8.1 MGD . 
Attachment C provides flow schematics  for the treatment plant .
2.1.4.  Sludge and Biosolids Management . The Discharger thickens primary sludge 
with gravity or mechanical thickeners, treats the thickened sludge with 
anaerobic digestion, and dewaters the digested sludge with a belt filter press .

2.1.5. Stormwater Management . The Discharger captures all stormwater at the plant 
and directs it to the plant headworks  for treatment.  Therefore, no additional 
stormwater requirements are necessary.
2.1.6.  Recycled Water Activities. The Discharger ’s recycled water use is limited to 
onsite applications at the plant. Recycled water is used to wash down and clean 
equipment (e.g., bar screens and clarifiers) and for dust control. The Discharger 
is evaluating the feasibility of implementing a recycled water program to provide 
disinfected tertiary recycled water to existing  sites and future development for 
landscape irrigation.
2.2. Discharge Point and Receiving Waters . Discharge of treated effluent is through 
a deepwater outfall in Lower San Francisco Bay. T he Discharger shares the outfall 
with the City of Burlingame Wastewater Treatment Facility in Burlingame  
(regulated under NPDES Permit CA0037788) , the Mel Leong Treatment Plant in 
San Francisco  (regulated under NPDES Permit CA0038318) , and the South San 
Francisco /San Bruno Water Quality Control Plant in San Bruno  (regulated under 
NPDES Permit CA0038130) . The outfall is  a submerged, multi -port diffuser 
(Discharge Point  002) located approximately 5,300 feet offshore at a depth of 
20 feet below mean lower low water . The diffuser  is 620 feet long and consists of 
66 ports (3 -inch diameter ) spaced 7 feet apart and 28 ports (2.8 -inch diameter) 
spaced 6 feet apart . The NBSU operates and maintains this outfall and inspects it 
on a five -year schedule. The outfall was last inspected on July 17, 2020, and was 
found to be in good condition with marine  growth affecting a few ports.  The outfall 
meets Basin Plan section 4.6.1 requirements for deepwater discharge.

2.4.1.2.  Unauthorized Discharges.  During the previous permit term, the Discharger 
reported an unauthorized discharge, violating  Discharge Prohibition III.A of 
the previous permit order.
The Discharger experienced intense  rain toward the end of December 2022 . 
On December 31, 2022, the plant experienced elevated in fluent  flows  
reach ing as high as  12.4 MGD, exceeding the plant’s 9.0 MGD peak wet 
weather flow design capacity.  Around 10:00 a .m., the Discharger de cided to 
pump waste water out of the equalization tank and into a nearby storm drain 
to minimize the risk of damage to the plant equipment.  The influent flows 
gradually decreased as the day went on , with the unauthorized discharge 
ceasing around  9:00 p .m.
The unauthorized discharge lasted around 1 1 hours, and the total volume  
was approximately 930, 600 gallons , none of which was recovered.  Along 
with significant stormwater flows, t he discharge  was conveyed into Highline 
Canal, a tidally influenced waterway that drains to San Francisco Bay. 
The Discharger is developing a private sewer lateral policy  that will allow the 
rehabilitation of privately -owned upper laterals and investigating the 
feasibility of providing additional wet weather storage capacity at the plant.

2.5.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes . In 2024, the Discharger plans to repair and reconstruct its 
nearshore outfall. The outfall was formerly the Discharger’s primary discharge 
point before the NBSU was formed and the deepwater outfall was constructed. 
The nearshore outfall will not replace the NBSU outfall described above and will 
only serve as an emergency discharge point for the plant. The nearshore outfall 
will prov ide an alternate means of discharge when the NBSU force main and/or 
outfall is out of service due to routine maintenance or other reasons. This Order 
does not authorize such discharges.
2.6. Sea Level Rise.  The Discharger projected that under a 100-year flood  plus 
3.3 feet of sea level rise  scenario , portions of the plant would be inundated . This 
amount  of sea level rise could be expected  between the years of 2070 and 2100. 
To protect  the plant, the shoreline will need to increase in height from one to five 
feet, which could include making improvements to the Highline Canal tide gate and 
constructing  a levee or seawall across Old Bayshore  Highway . The Discharger 
plans to work with regional partners of the Colma -San Bruno Operational Land 
Unit, which include San Francisco International Airport, San Mateo County, and 
the cities of South San Francisco, San Bruno, Burlingame, and San Mateo,  to 
develop  cohesive and regional shoreline solutions to sea level rise .
