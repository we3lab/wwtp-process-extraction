FACILITY DESCRIPTION  
The Discharger provides sewerage service for the community of the City  of Anderson . 
The City of Anderson has a population of approximately 11,323; however, the Facility 
provides service to a population of approximately 7,774 . The  design daily average flow 
capacity of the Facility is 2.0 million gallons per day (MGD ).

ATTACHMENT F – FACT SHEET  F-5 A. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment and Controls  
The treatment system at the Facility consists of a headworks with one mechanical 
bar screen, a backup manual screen, a backup comminutor, two activated sludge 
aeration basins, two secondary clarifiers, one ballast pond to equalize flow to the 
pressure filt ers, three pressurized multi -media filtration units, one chlorine contact 
basin, and sulfur dioxide dechlorination. Treated effluent is discharged to the 
Sacramento River through a multiport diffuser.  
Influent Pump Station & Headworks:  The influent pump station  is equipped with 
three dry pit submersible pumps with a pumping capacity of 2.5 MGD each. The 
headworks also has a 3.5 MGD natural gas emergency pump if the backup diesel 
power generator fails or flows exceed 7.5 MGD.  
Bar Screen:  The mechanical screen (Aqua Guard) removes large debris and rags. 
The headworks also includes a backup communator and a manual bar rake for 
screenings. In the event of a total screening failure, a high -level alarm will sound and 
notify the standby operator . The influent flow will eventually overflow into the 
emergency basin pipeline located at the headworks.  
Complete Mix Activated Sludge:  Flow from the headworks is distributed evenly 
between the two 420,000 -gallon aeration basins. Detention times run from 4 to  
8 hours, depending on flows.  
Secondary Clarifiers:  Flow from aeration basins enters a distribution box, dividing 
the flow to each clarifier. Surface loading rates range from 276 to 1,104 gallons per 
day per square foot. The return -activated sludge rate is 30% to 100% of the influent 
flow. Waste -activated sludge is pumped to the digester at 36,000 to 50,400 gallons 
daily. Scum and floatable are skimmed and pumped (approximately 3,000 gallons 
per week) to the aerobic digester.  
Pressure Filtration:  Clarifier effluent flows to an equalization ballast pond  
(290,000 gallons), where filter pumps draw from. Filters No.1  and No. 2  have a 
maximum flow rate of 1.7 MGD each, and filter No. 3  is 2 MGD. The f ilter’s 
maximum surface loading rate is 5 gallons per minute per square foot. Backwash 
rates are 18,000 gallons to 25,000 gallons per backwash. A typical backwash occurs 
every 24 to 72 hours. Backwash flow is typically sent to the sludge stabilization 
ponds or can be routed to either ae ration basins. A fourth pump (2 MGD) is available 
for bypass pumping to the chlorine contact chamber if emergency storage (5.3  MG) 
is at capacity and influent flow exceeds 6 MGD.  
Chlorination:  Two chlorinators supply a gas solution for disinfection with a 
maximum feed rate of 400 lbs/day. The dose is applied at the beginning of the 
134,000 -gallon chlorine contact chamber. Detention time during the average flow is 
around 2.5 hours. The chlorine gas solution can also be valved to the influent 
maintenance hole for odor control and is also used for pre -filtration, MLSS 
filamentous control, and algae control on clarifier launders.

ATTACHMENT F – FACT SHEET  F-6 De-chlorination:  The effluent discharged from the chlorine contact chamber is 
dosed with a sodium bisulfite solution. The sodium bisulfite neutralizes any 
remaining chlorine to have a zero -chlorine residual, leaving the Facility . Between the 
two peristaltic chemical feed pumps, there is a combined max feed rate of  
120 gallons/day.  
Digester:  The Facility  has two aerobic digesters. Digester No. 1  has a capacity of 
256,000 gallons, and Digester No. 2  has a capacity of 453,000 gallons. Currently, 
only Digester No. 2  is online. Approximately 36,000 to 46,000 gallons per day of 
waste -activated sludge is pumped to the digester daily. Every evening, an automatic 
decant valve opens to decant off the supernatant to one of the ponds. The digester 
is wasted twice weekly to one of the three sludge stabilzation  ponds  at 
approximately 120,000 gallons weekly. Solids retention time is 25 days.  
Sludge Stabilization Ponds and Drying Beds:  Ponds receive digester decant, 
wasting, and filter backwash flows. The ponds are decanted back to the headworks 
at a maximum flow of 0.04 MGD. Ponds are dewatered in the spring to an empty 
pond. The solids are dried in the pond or in conjunction with the drying beds if solid 
levels are too high. The drying beds have an underdrain system that flows back to 
the headwork s. Once the sludge has dried in ponds and beds, it is removed and 
trucked to the landfill.    
Sludge stabilization ponds No. 3 and No. 4 are identical in specifications: total 
surface area is 1 -acre, overall depth is 8.4 feet, design liquid depth is 6.4 feet and 
the total volume is 2 million gallons (each).  The invert elevation is 395.85 feet. 
Sludge stabilization pond No. 5 has a total surface area of 1.75 acres, the overall 
depth is 8.33 feet, the design liquid depth is 6.33 feet and the total volume is  
3.6 million gallons.  The invert elevation for sludge stabilization pond No. 5 is  
396.57 fee t. The sludge stabil ization ponds are typically operated in an annual 
rotation; sludge stabilization ponds No. 3 and No. 4 are operated concurrently when 
sludge stabil ization pond No. 5 is offline, and vice versa.  As a result, approximately 
every 18 months one or two of the ponds will be emptied of solids in the fall.  
Sludge drying beds: The  sludge drying beds have a total surface area of  
30,000 square feet.  Individual surface areas of the drying beds are as follows: 
Drying bed No. 1, No. 2, No. 3, and No. 4 are 7,000, 8,000, 8,000, and 7,000 square 
feet respectively .  The sludge drying beds’ overall depth is 26 inches and the design 
liquid depth is 16 inches.  
The Facility also has two historic sludge stabilzation ponds located northwest of the 
sludge drying beds.  These ponds do not receive or return any flows from/ to the 
treatment process  and only receive  precip itation .  
Emergency Storage Pond : The Facility’s emergency storage pond is only used in 
the event of catastrophic mechanical failures where sufficient treatment cannot be 
achieved prior to discharge or if there is a 100 -year flood and the treatment plant is 
pushed beyond operational capacity and needs ad ditional storage. In the event the

Side streams:  All other plant processes with minor flows or decants are piped back 
to headworks.  
Storm Drainage:  Plant stormwater is drained into the emergency storage pond  and 
removed by pumping to the headworks if needed . 
Unit Construction : The ballast pond was constructed in 1972 with concrete and 
rebar walls with an  asphalt bottom. The ballast pond  is inspected during cleaning , 
which typically occurs at least once per year . The Discharger has reported that no  
defects have been  observed. Sludge stabilization ponds No. 3 and No. 4 were 
constructed in  1989. Both have concrete with rebar  sides and asphalt bottoms. 
Sludge stabilization pond No. 5 was constructed in 2010 with concrete, rebar sides, 
and asphalt  bottom. The  sludge stabilization ponds  are inspected when they are 
emptied , approximately every 18 months, i n the fall.  The Discharger has reported 
that no  defects  have been  noted. The Discharger reports that they have  not had to 
make any repairs to the original construction of  these basins.
