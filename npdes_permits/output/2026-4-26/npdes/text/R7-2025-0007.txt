FACILITY DESCRIPTION
The Discharger owns and operates a wastewater collection, treatment, and disposal 
system (Facility) and provides service to  a population of approximately  38,249 
located in the City of Calexico . The permitted maximum daily flow limitation is equal 
to the design capacity of the wastewater treatment plant which is 4.3 million gallons 
per day (MGD) . The Facility is located in the NW ¼ of the SW ¼ of Section 14,  T17S, 
R14E,  SBB&M36. The discharge from the Facility consists of disinfected  secondary 
treated wastewater.
2.A. Discharge of Wastewater Treatment and Controls
The Facility design capacity is 4.3 MGD. The Facility provides secondary treatment 
through two treatment trains: an activated sludge treatment system with a design 
capacity of 2.5 MGD, referred to as Plant #1, and an aerated lagoon treatment 
system with a desi gn capacity of 1.8 MGD, referred to as Plant #2.
Wastewater received at the headworks is screened at influent pump stations #1 
and #2 and pumped to two circular primary clarifiers for removal of suspended 
solids and reduction of  biochemical oxygen demand. After clarification, flow is

Plant #1 was originally constructed in 1967 and was subsequently  upgraded in 
1974, 1991, and 1995. Plant #1 provides extended aeration activated sludge 
biological treatment through three concrete -lined aeration basins fitted with surface 
aerators operated in parallel. Three circular secondary clarifiers remove solids af ter 
aeration.  Return sludge from the clarifiers is directed to influent pump station #1 
and combines  with raw wastewater to maintain sufficient levels of biomass in the 
system.
Plant #2 was constructed in 1991 and later upgraded  in 1994. Plant #2 consists of 
a set of four aerated lagoons  operated in series. Each treatment lagoon has an 
approximate volume of 3 million gallons and is equipped with  floating aerators.
Secondary effluent from both treatment systems is combined for disinfection by an 
ultraviolet (UV) disinfection system prior to discharge to  the New River . The UV 
system was installed in 1995 and replaced a chlorine contract tank chamber that 
was constructed in 1967.
Sludge generated from the primary clarifiers is pumped to two 238,000 -gallon  
anaerobic digesters that are normally operated in series . Secondary sludge from 
Plant #1 is thickened by dissolved air flotation before being pumped to the 
digesters . Sludge may be removed periodically from the lagoons as needed in 
Plant #2. Digested sludge is dewatered using drying beds and is stored for a 
period of no more than two years before being hauled by contractor to Yuma, 
Arizona for land application of the Class B solids .
Wastewater is discharged from Discharge Point 001  or Discharge Point 002 to the 
New River, a water of the United States.
2.B. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters
Final  effluent is discharged through Discharge Point 001 at Latitude 32°, 40’, 17”  
North and  Longitude 115°, 30’, 45”  West , or Discharge Point 00 2 at Latitude 32°, 
39’, 56.37”  North and Longitude 115°, 30’, 9.01” West , to the New River, a water of 
the United States.
