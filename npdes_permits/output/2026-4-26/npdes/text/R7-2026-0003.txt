FACILITY DESCRIPTION
The Discharger owns and operates the Facility, a wastewater collection, treatment, and 
disposal system , and provides service to a population of approximately  94,216 
individuals  located in the  City of Indio . The Facility is located in SW ¼ of Section 19, 
T5S, R8E, SBB&M25. The permitted maximum daily flow limitation is equal to the 
design capacity of the wastewater treatment plant which is 12.5 MGD .
2.A. Discharge of Wastewater Treatment and Controls
The wastewater treatment plant consists of two separate treatment systems: 1) an 
activated sludge treatment system, and 2) an oxidation pond system.
Influent enters the main influent pumping station and is screened through 
mechanical bar screens. Screened influent is directed to the grit chamber and then 
to the primary clarifiers. Flow is then apportioned between the activated sludge

The design capacity of the activated sludge system is 10 MGD. The activated sludge 
treatment system consists of anoxic selectors, aeration basins, secondary clarifiers, 
and a chlorine contact chamber.
The design capacity of the oxidation pond system is 2.5 MGD. The oxidation pond 
system consists of two oxidation ponds and two cells.
Treated effluent from each treatment system (i.e., activated sludge and oxidation 
pond  system) is disinfected with sodium hypochlorite in separate chlorine contact 
tanks. The chlorinated effluent from each chlorine contact tank is then combined and 
dechlorinated with the addition of sodium bisulfite prior to discharge.
Screenings are removed from the influent wastewater using mechanical bar screens 
and are sent to a grinder for washing and compaction prior to collection in a portable 
dumpster. Grit removal occurs after the screening process and is placed in a 
portable dumpster  to be combined with the screening . The screening and grit 
dumpster are hauled to  a landfill weekly for disposal.
Excess solids from the activated sludge treatment system are pumped to  a pond 
system for stabilization. Primary sludge from the primary clarifiers is pumped to the 
anaerobic digester. Solids from the anaerobic digester and oxidation ponds are 
pumped to a belt press  for dewatering. Dewatered solids are place d on drying beds 
for further moisture reduction prior to disposal. Biosolids are continuously removed 
from the facility site  and are transported to Arizona for further treatment by 
composting to ensure Class A requirements are met prior to land application.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes
The Discharger indicated in the Report of Waste Discharge  that there are  planned 
improvements  at the Facility during the upcoming permit term  and the  subsequent  
permit term . During the course of the current permit term , the Discharger has 
initiated treatment plant upgrades identified in the Master Plan Update Project. It is 
expected that during the next permit term, Phase 2b  will be complete d. Phase 2b 
consists of the following:
·Rehabilitation of the Influent Pump Station.
·Addition of mechanical Bar Screen at headworks.
·Installation of a replacement grit chamber.
·Installation of waste activated sludge thickener.
·Addition of a second Digester and related systems (flare).
·Installation of a replacement electrical main switchboard.
·Addition of pump station to return sludge dewatering filtrate back to 
headworks.
During the next permit term, the Master Plan will be updated and Phase 2c will 
commence.  Phase 2c consists of the following:
·Pond 2 and Pond 3 free water will drain and treated prior to discharge to the 
Coachella Valley Stormwater Channel.
·Sludge from Pond 2 and Pond 3 will be dredged for solar drying.
·Construction of extension to sludge drying bed.
·Installation of digester gas holding facility (optional).
·Implementation of cogeneration with digester gas (optional).
The Master Plan update project will identify upgrades over the next 40 plus years.  
Phase 2b and Phase 2c, when completed, will not  increase the Facility's permitted 
maximum daily flow  capacity of 12.5 MGD , nor will the projects modify  the treatment 
process. Instead, the improvements will provide redundancy to current treatment 
process es and allow the Discharger  to begin the phasing out of the pond system in a 
future phase of construction work.
