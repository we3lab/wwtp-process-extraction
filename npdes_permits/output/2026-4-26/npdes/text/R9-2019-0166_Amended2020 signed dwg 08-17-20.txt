FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
The SLRWRF and the LSWTP provides municipal wastewater treatment services to a population 
of approximately 180,000 within the boundaries of the City of Oceanside and a portion of the City 
of Vista. Additionally, the SLRWRF serves a population of approximately 10,000 within the 
Rainbow Municipal Water District. The Rainbow Municipal Water District owns 1.5 MGD of the 
Discharger 's treatment capacity and is responsible for its sanitary sewer system up to the point 
where it connects with the Discharger’s sanitary sewer system. To reduce pumping costs, the 
Discharger has an agreement with the City of Vista for the exchange, treatment, and disposal of 
equal volumes of nonindustrial wastewater generated in the respective service areas.  
The MBGPF is a groundwater desalination plant that treats local brackish groundwater extracted 
from the Mission Hydrologic Subarea (HSA) 903.11 to produce up to 6.37 MGD of potable water, 
15 percent of the City of Oceanside’s water supply.  
A. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment and Controls 
1. San Luis Rey Water Reclamation Facility (SLRWRF) 
The SLRWRF is located at 3950 N. River Road in Oceanside, California, approximately 6.5 
miles from the OOO. The headworks of the SLRWRF includes mechanical bar screens and 
aerated grit removal. From the headworks, the flow is diverted into the East Plant (also 
known as Plant 1) and the West Plant (also known as Plant 2). Plant 1 consists of flow 
equalization, primary sedimentation, biological secondary treatment by means of activated 
sludge and secondary sedimentation/clarification. Plant 2 consists of primary sedimentation 
and biological secondary treatment by means of activated sludge and secondary 
sedimentation/clarification. The Discharger may send the Plant 1 effluent to the effluent 
pump station wet well or to its tertiary treatment facility for further treatment and reuse. The 
Discharger can only send the Plant 2 effluent to the effluent pump station wet well. At the 
effluent pump station wet well, the Discharger disinfects the secondary-treated wastewater 
with sodium hypochlorite to reduce bacteriological concentrations and pumps the

Screenings from the headworks and solids from the grit removal are collected onsite and 
trucked to a local landfill. The SLRWRF features gravity belt thickeners for thickening 
secondary sludge, anaerobic digestion of primary and secondary sludge, and dewatering of 
digested sludge using centrifuges. The SLRWRF Class B biosolids are hauled offsite by a 
contractor and used for land application in Arizona. The SLRWRF utilizes Option 1 
(minimum 38 percent reduction of volatile solids) to reduce vector attraction. The SLRWRF 
utilizes Alternative 2 (anaerobic digestion between 15 days at 35-55 °C and 60 days at 
20°C) for pathogen reduction.  
The SLRWRF also features a tertiary treatment facility that produces recycled water. The 
SLRWRF tertiary treatment and recycled water operations are regulated under separate 
WDRs, Order No. 93-07, Waste Discharge Requirements for the San Luis Rey Wastewater 
Treatment Plant City of Oceanside San Diego County. In 2018, the tertiary-treated 
wastewater from the SLRWRF was reused for landscape irrigation (93 million gallons, or an 
average of 0.25 MGD) and to maintain water level in Whelan Lake  (57 million gallons, or 
an average of 0.16 MGD). 
2. La Salina Wastewater Treatment Plant (LSWTP) 
The LSWTP is located at 1330 Tait Street in Oceanside, California, adjacent to the mouth 
of Loma Alta Creek. Secondary treatment process at the LSWTP is accomplished by 
mechanical bar screens, aerated grit removal, flow equalization, primary 
sedimentation/clarification, and biological secondary treatment by means of activated 
sludge and secondary sedimentation/clarification. 
The Discharger’s ROWD stated that the peak monthly design capacity of the LSWTP is 5.5 
MGD. This Order carries over the permitted flow of 5.5 MGD from Order No. R9-2011-
0016. From December 2011 to June 2019, the average influent flow into the LSWTP was 
2.8 MGD and the average effluent flow from the LSWTP to the OOO was 2.6 MGD. 
Screenings from the headworks and solids from the grit removal are collected onsite and 
trucked out of state to Cooper Mountain Landfill in Welton, Arizona. The LSWTP features 
dissolved air floatation thickening for secondary sludge, anaerobic digestion of primary and 
secondary sludge, and belt filter press dewatering of digested sludge. The LSWTP utilizes 
Option 1 (minimum 38 percent reduction of volatile solids) to reduce vector attraction. The 
LSWTP utilizes Alternative 1 (monitoring) for pathogen reduction.

Attachment F – Fact Sheet  F-7 The MBGPF, located northwest of the intersection of Fireside Street and Heritage Street in 
Oceanside, California, treats brackish groundwater for municipal potable water supply. The 
MBGPF extracts groundwater from the Mission (HSA 903.11) Basin Aquifer (part of the 
larger San Luis Rey Valley groundwater basin). 
Treatment at the MBGPF is comprised of chemical pretreatment (hydrochloric acid and 
scale inhibitor), cartridge filtration, reverse osmosis (RO) membrane filtration 
(demineralization), granular activated carbon (GAC) for removal and reduction of volatile 
organics (i.e. 1,2,3-trichloropropane, 1,2-dichloropropane), degasification (for removal of 
hydrogen sulfide and carbon dioxide gases), and post-treatment disinfection by 
chloramination (sodium hypochlorite and ammonium hydroxide). 
The primary RO process is designed as a two-stage system with a 75% overall recovery 
rate. The permitted combined capacity of the MBGPF is 6.37 MGD. There are two RO units 
that can produce up to a maximum of 2.2 MGD permeate each (combined total of 4.4 
MGD). The RO permeate passes through the GAC system and then is blended with 
groundwater that is treated through a manganese greensand filtration system (for iron and 
manganese removal) just upstream of the degasifiers. The manganese greensand filtration 
system can produce up to a maximum of 1.97 MGD. Disinfection of the blended product 
water occurs after the degasifiers. Backwash waste waters produced by the GAC and 
manganese greensand filtration systems are discharged to an underground concrete 
holding basin and subsequently pumped to the SLRWRF via a 42-inch sewer force main. 
The brine waste (reject) from the primary RO process is discharged into a 24-inch ocean 
outline line. At maximum design conditions this results in approximately 1.47 MGD of brine 
(reject) waste. From December 2011 to February 2019, the average effluent flow from the 
MBGPF to the OOO was 0.78 MGD. 
B. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters 
The SLRWRF Effluent Pump Station sends disinfected secondary-treated wastewater from the 
SLRWRF through the San Luis Rey Land Outfall. The San Luis Rey Land Outfall discharges 
the effluent to the 16-inch Ocean Outfall Combined Meter and Sample Location (Combined 
Meter, Monitoring Location EFF-004) at the LSWTP.  
The waste brine from the MBGPF and Genentech, Inc. discharges to the ten-inch-diameter 
land outfall (Brine Line). The Brine Line discharges the waste brine to the San Luis Rey Land 
Outfall at the LSWTP, just before the Combined Meter, commingling with the disinfected 
secondary-treated wastewater from the SLRWRF.  
The LSWTP Pump Station sends secondary-treated wastewater from the LSWTP into the 
Combined Meter, where it commingles with the effluent from the SLRWRF; the MBGPF; and 
Genentech, Inc. From the Combined Meter, the effluent from the SLRWRF; the LSWTP; the 
MBGPF; and Genentech, Inc. flows through the LSWTP Land Outfall. The LSWTP Land 
Outfall discharges to the OOO, just north of the mouth of the Loma Alta Creek and east of 
South Pacific Street.  
The tertiary-treated wastewater from the Fallbrook Public Utility District’s Fallbrook Water 
Reclamation Plant discharges to the 16-inch-diameter land outfall (Fallbrook Land Outfall). The 
Fallbrook Land Outfall discharges into the LSWTP Land Outfall after the Combined Meter but 
before the connection to the OOO.

The San Luis Rey Land Outfall consists of a 34,000-foot-long, 24-inch-/36-inch-diameter 
pipeline that runs from the SLRWRF Effluent Pump Station to the Combined Meter at the 
LSWTP. In the letter dated May 1, 2014, the Discharger reported that, due to the following 
reasons, the average monthly flow rate from the SLRWRF through the San Luis Rey Land 
Outfall (at Monitoring Location EFF-001) cannot exceed 13.5 MGD:  
1. Muck, sediment, and biological growth buildup in the OOO resulting in high friction losses in 
the OOO, increased pressure in the San Luis Rey Land Outfall, and limited pumping 
capacity of the SLRWRF and the LSWTP Effluent Pump Stations; 
2. High head loss at the 15-inch-diameter Combined Meter at the LSWTP resulting in 
increased pressure in the San Luis Rey Land Outfall and limited pumping capacity of the 
SLRWRF and the LSWTP Effluent Pump Stations; 
3. The limited pressure rating (150 pounds per square inch (psi)) and capacity in a portion of 
the San Luis Rey Land Outfall; and  
4. The pumping limitation of the SLRWRF and the LSWTP Effluent Pump Stations. 
The LSWTP Land Outfall consists of a 400-foot-long, 24-inch-diameter pipeline that starts at 
the Combined Meter at the LSWTP and ends at the start of the OOO, just north of the mouth of 
the Loma Alta Creek and east of South Pacific Street. In the letter dated May 1, 2014, the 
Discharger reported that, due to the following reasons, the average monthly flow rate from the 
SLRWRF; the LSWTP; the MBGPF; and Genentech, Inc. through the LSWTP Land Outfall (at 
Monitoring Location EFF-004) cannot exceed 16.6 MGD:  
1. Muck, sediment, and biological growth buildup in the OOO resulting in high friction losses in 
the OOO, increased pressure in the San Luis Rey Land Outfall, and limited pumping 
capacity of the SLRWRF and the LSWTP Effluent Pump Stations;  
2. High head loss at the 15-inch-diameter Combined Meter at the LSWTP resulting in 
increased pressure in the San Luis Rey Land Outfall and limited pumping capacity of the 
SLRWRF and the LSWTP Effluent Pump Stations; and

Planned Changes 
The Discharger listed the following long-term wastewater efforts directed toward achieving 
goals of (1) maximizing recycled water use, and (2) improving wastewater treatment and 
conveyance system reliability:  
1. Replacement of the LSWTP.  In November 2014, the Discharger’s City Council directed 
the Discharger’s Water Utilities staff to move forward with developing a facilities-planning 
approach that would involve decommissioning the LSWTP, replacing the LSWTP with a 
pump station, and conveying wastewater from the LSWTP site to the SLRWRF for

Discharger is developing an implementation plan for supporting planning, environmental 
evaluation, design, permitting, and construction of required facilities.  
2. Expansion of Non-potable Water System.  The Discharger is expanding the non-potable 
water system that conveys tertiary-treated effluent from the SLRWRF to regional recycled 
water customers in accordance with San Diego Water Board Order No. 93-07. The purpose 
of this expansion is to serve the El Corazon soccer fields and the Goat Hill Park Golf 
Course. This effort will include converting five miles of brine pipeline to non-potable use, 
constructing 4400 feet of new non-potable pipeline, and routing waste brine from the 
MBGPF and Genentech, Inc. to the outfall through alternative pipes. 
3. Indirect Potable Reuse (IPR) Planning Study.  Since 2014, the Discharger has been 
engaged in a comprehensive effort to identify opportunities to increase recycled water use 
and minimize ocean discharge through the implementation of IPR, focusing on identifying 
IPR opportunities consistent with recycled water groundwater recharge and extraction 
regulations established by the State Water Resources Control Board (State Water Board), 
Division of Drinking Water. Preliminary IPR planning has identified a preferred Phase 1 
project that involves constructing an Advanced Water Treatment facility at the SLRWRF 
and recharging and recovering 3 MGD of purified water into the Mission Basin. Proposed 
Phase 2 operations would involve expanding groundwater recharge and recovery operation 
to approximately 4.5 MGD. The Discharger has developed an implementation plan for this 
project that identifies planning, pilot studies, environmental review, public outreach, and 
regulatory evaluations required to refine the IPR project concept and verify project 
economics. By email dated September 4, 2018, the Discharger stated that the advanced 
water treatment plant will be online in 2021. The Discharger is working closely with the 
Division of Drinking Water for the approval of the Title 22 Engineering Report and 
permitting on the IPR project. The proposed project contains two to three injection wells at 
3 MGD with an underground retention time of seven months. A public hearing would be 
scheduled after approval of the Title 22 Engineering Report as a part of the permitting 
process. The ROWD will be developed based on any comments on the engineering report.  
4. Coordination with Regional Agencies.  The OOO represents a significant regional asset 
for accommodating brine discharges from future IPR projects or groundwater treatment 
projects. The Discharger is committed to coordinating with adjoining agencies to explore 
interagency opportunities to optimize use and efficiency of regional water, recycled water, 
and wastewater infrastructure. 
5. Fallbrook Land Outfall . By letter dated July 6, 2016, the Discharger informed the San 
Diego Water Board that the Discharger and Fallbrook Public Utility District are engaged in 
negotiations to transfer ownership and operation of the lower portion of the Fallbrook Land 
Outfall from the Fallbrook Public Utility District to the Discharger. As part of the transfer, the 
Discharger will also be supplying recycled water to the California Department of 
Transportation along the lower portion of the Fallbrook Land Outfall.
