FACILITY DESCRIP TION 
A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatm ent 
1. Location and Service Area . The plant is located at 195 Belle Air Road  in the City of South 
San Francisco. It provid es secondary wastewater treatment  of domestic, commercial, and 
industrial wastewater  for the cities of South San Francisco and San Bruno, the Town of 
Colma, and portions of Daly City. The plant serves a population of about 113,500. 
2. Collection System . The City of South San Francisco wastewater collection system and City 
of San Bruno wastew ater collection system are covered by this Order and convey wastewater 
to the p lant. The South San Francisco collection system consists of approximately 148 miles 
of gravity sewers and force mains and seven pump stations. The San Bruno collection system 
consists of approximately 77 miles of gravity sewers and force mains, 20 miles of lower 
laterals, and six pump stations . The Town of Colma and City of  Daly City  operate satellite 
collection systems that discharge transmit wastewater to the South San Francisc o collection 
system . 
3. Wastewater Treatment . The plant receives untreated wastewater from three force main 
pump stations. The influent flows through bar screens and two grit removal systems. During 
normal dry weather operations, it is treated with ferric chloride and polymer for flocculation 
and pumped to a flow splitter for distribution to primary clarifiers, where grease, floating 
solids, and settable solids are removed . 
The primary -treated wastewater typically flows by gravity to five aeration basins as tw o 
separate flow streams. Another four aeration basins remain in stand -by mode. They are  
scheduled for retrofitting and mainly used for wet weather flows. The wastewater is 
combined in the aeration basins with return activated sludge from secondary clarifiers. It then 
flows by gravity through the aeration basins to secondary clarifiers for sedimentation and 
floating solids removal. During wet weather, when influent flow greatly exceeds the peak 
secondary treatment design capacity (currently 30 MGD ), primary effluent may be routed 
around the aeration basins and secondary clarifiers and blended with secondary- treated 
effluent. These bypasses are subject to Discharge Prohibition III.C of this Order and 
Attachment D section I.G.  
The secondary- treated wastewater flows by gravity to two chlorine contact basins where it is  
mixed with sodium hypochlorite for disinfection. After disinfection, it flows by gravity to the  
NBSU effluent pumping stat ion wet well (Discharge Point No. 001), where it is blended with  
chlorinated effluent from the c ities of Burlingame and Millbrae , and the San Francisco 
International Airport. It is then mixed with sodium bisulfite to neutralize the residual 
chlorine. The c ombined effluent is pumped through the NBSU outfall (Discharge Point 
No. 002) and discharged to Lower San Francisco Bay about one mile offshore from Oyster 
Point.

capacity of 13 MGD and a peak wet weather capacity of 62 MGD . From June 2014 through 
October 2018, the plant  treated a daily average of 8.28 MGD, with the highest reported 
average daily flow being 36 MGD. Wet weather diversions , or blending, occurred five  time

4. Sludge and Biosolids Management . Sludge from the primary and secondary clarification 
operations is processed using anaerobic digestion. The process consists of gravity and 
mechanical thickeners or waste activated sludge thickening, mesophilic anaerobic digesters, 
and belt filter presses f or dewatering. The digested and dewatered biosolids are hauled offsite 
to a permitted landfill.  
5. Stormwater Management . All stormwater at the plant  is collected and directed to the 
headworks for treatment ; therefore, no additional stormwater requirements ar e necessary.  
B. Discharge Point and Receiving Waters  
Treated effluent flows through the 60- inch NBSU forcemain into Lower San Francisco Bay at 
Discharge Point No. 002 via a 620- foot diffuser composed of 66 ports (3 -inch diameter) spaced 
7 feet apart and 28  ports (2.8- inch diameter) spaced 6  feet apart. The location of the outfall is 
northeast of Point San Bruno, approximately 5,300 feet offshore at depth 20 feet below mean 
lower low water. The NBSU operates and maintains the outfall , and regular inspections  occur on 
a 10- year schedule. The most recent inspection took place in May 2015. Divers found all diffuser 
ports to be performing in accordance with their  specifications but  noted that the fasteners 
securing the end gate needed to be replaced. Repairs were completed in July 2015. 
When the plant’s effluent flowrate exceeds 35 MGD (engineered contractual limit for the NBSU 
pipeline), fully treated effluent is pumped to a  7 million -gallon effluent storage pond. On occasion, 
when onsite storage basins are full, an emergency discharge through a shallow -water outfall could 
occur. Also, if major plant  systems were to fail or the NBSU force main were to be damaged, 
disinfected effluent could be dechlorinated and discharged through the shallow -water outfall. The 
outfall would discharge to the Colma Creek tidal slough adjacent to the plant . This Order does not 
authorize such discharges , which would be bypasses subject to Discharge Prohibition III.C and 
Attachment D secti on I.G of this Order . The Discharger used the  shallow -water outfall once during 
the previous  order  term (December  2014).
