FACILITY DESCRIPTION 
2.1. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment  
2.1.1.  Service Area and Collection System . The plant provides secondary and 
advanced secondary treatment of domestic and commercial wastewater from the Town of Yountville and California Veterans Home.  Its estimated service 
area population is approximately 2,900 people.

2.1.2.  Wastewater Treatment . The permitted average dry weather design treatment 
capacity of the plant is 0.55 MGD. The Town’s 1996 capacity study indicated 
that the plant’s actual average dry weather capacity is 0.65 MGD ( Wastewater 
Treatment Plant Facility Upgrade Study for the Town of Yountville, March 1996) , however, this Order maintains the original ly authorized  average dry 
weather  design flow . The peak wet weather capacity  is 2.0 MGD; flows in 
excess of this are stored in a 3.8- million -gallon flow equalization pond for later 
treatment. The pond is lined with compacted clay soil from nearby areas.  
The treatment process consists of  aerated grit removal, solids grinding using a 
comminutor, primary clarification, first- stage trickling filtration, intermediate 
clarification, second- stage trickling filtration, aerated solids contact, coagulation 
and flocculation, final sedimentation, compressi ble media filtration, disinfection 
using sodium hypochlorite, and dechlorination with sodium bisulfite. In the 
future, the Dis charger may transition from chlorine disinfection to ozone 
disinfection (see provision 6.3.5.1 of the Order) . Treated wastewater flows to a 
2.7-million -gallon effluent storage pond prior to recycling or discharge to the 
Napa River.  
The design capacity of the compressi ble media filtration system (Fuzzy Filter)  is 
1.3 MGD. When influent flow exceeds the filter capacity, the filter is taken 
offlin e; secondary -treated flow is disinfected,  routed through the effluent storage 
pond,  and dis charged to the Napa River . When influent flow  drops below 
1.3 MGD, the filters are put back online.  Routing treated wastewater in this 
manner is not considered a bypass subject to Discharge Prohibition 3.3 of the 
Order , as explained in Fact Sheet section 4.1.1.4. 
2.1.3. Sludge and Biosolids Management . Biosolids from the various process units 
are returned to the primary clarifier, which operates as an initial sludge 
thickener. The commingled sludge from these processes is then pumped to a Dissolved Air Flotation Thickener (DAFT), processed in primary and secondary sludge digesters, and applied to sludge drying beds. Typically, fewer  than 100 
tons of dried bios olids are hauled annually to the Clover Flat Landfill for 
disposal.  
2.1.4. Stormwater Management . Most of the Facility area is unpaved, allowing 
stormwater to infiltrate to soil. Runoff from paved areas, and any runoff from unpaved areas, drains to Hinman Creek. The Discharger maintains coverage for its stormwater discharges through enrollment in the Phase II Small Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System Permit (Small MS4 Permit ), NPDES General 
Permit CAS000004. As required by the Small MS4 Permit, the Discha rger has 
developed and maintains a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

disinfected secondary -2.2 (as defined in Cal. Code Regs., Division 4, section 
60301.220) water for  reuse under the State Water Board’s Water Reclamation 
Requirements for Recycled Water Use (Order WQ 2016- 0068- DDW ). The 
Town’s reclamation pipeline provides recycled water for golf course, landscape, 
and vineyard irrigation. Additionally, an onsite truck fill station provides recycled 
water for construction site uses, including soil compaction and dust control. 
From 2015 through 2018, the annual percentage of the Discharger’s treated 
effluent recycled for beneficial uses ranged from 56 to 93  percent . 
2.2. Discharge Points and Receiving Waters. When the demand for recycled water 
is low and the river flow -to-effluent flow ratio is at least 41:1, treated effluent is 
discharged to the Napa River via a shallow -water outfall (Discharge Point 001).

2.4. Compliance Summary 
2.4.1. Treatment Plant. The Discharger violated its numeric effluent limitations once 
during the previous order term . In January 2017, the plant ’s total coliform 
five-sample median reached 33 MPN/100 mL, exceeding the limit of 23 
MPN/100  mL. The Discharger attributed the violation to excessive inflow and 
infiltration during a period of sustained heavy rainfall. The plant  was operating 
at or near capacity, shortening the chlorine contact time. The Discharger is  
working on several sewer main rehabilitation projects to reduce inflow and 
infiltration . During prolonged periods of high rainfall, it relies on operations staff 
to adjust disinfection dosages to account for the shorter chlorine contact time.

The Discharger regularly completes asset management tasks to ensure that its collection system is well -maintained.  It distributes quarterly  public outreach 
newsletters on topics such as  reducing fats, oils, and g rease inputs ; proper 
disposal of household hazardous waste (e.g., batteries, fluorescent lights, e-waste) ; and how to report  collection system problems.  The Discharger also 
allocates annual funding for collection system improvements, including pump station equipment replacement, sewer main rehabilitation, and inflow/infiltration reduction. From 2016 to 2019, the Discharger completed “slipline” rehabilitation on several sewer main sections to reduce infiltration and increase system  
longevity.  
2.5. Sea Level Rise.  Sea level ri se does not threaten the Facility , which discharges  
treated wastewater  to the non- tidal portion of the Napa River and is located 
approximately 20 miles upstream of San Pablo Bay . 
2.6.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes. The Discharger plans to complete several projects  within the 
next five years , including control system software upgrades, eff luent holding pond 
improvements, primary trickling filter media  replacement , and construction of a 
centralized control room to monitor plant processes. Additionally, the Discharger may transition from chlorine disinfection to ozone disinfection to reduce t he 
production of disinfection byproducts in treated effluent.   
These changes are mentioned here for informational purposes only  and are not 
requirements of this Order, except to the extent that they pertain to ensuring Facility reliability.
