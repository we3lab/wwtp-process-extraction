FACILITY DESCRIPTION
The Discharger provides sewerage service for the community of 6,268 residents and
serves a population of approximately 3,240 . The Facility has a design capacity for an
average dry weather flow (ADWF) of 0.43 million gallons per day (MGD) and a peak
wet weather flow (PWWF) of 1.32 MGD. The Facility is currently operating at an
ADWF of 0.33 MGD and PWWF of 0.94 MGD.
Facility improvements during the term of the previous Order were completed in
2021. The improvements include:
1.Rehabilitation of the headwork grinder.
2.Installation of a biological selector.
3.New aerators and DO -control system in oxidization ditches.
4.New mixed -liquor recycle pump station.
5.Replacement of clarifier weirs and baffles. Baffles are now density current
baffles.
6.Replacement of all RAS, WAS, and scum pumps, motors, and check valves.
7.Installation of an additional travel bridge filter. The original filter was also
replaced with a rail -mounted backwash system to match that of the new filter.
8.Chlorine contact basin slide gates were replaced with fiberglass -reinforced
plastic gates.
9.Replacement of aerator in northern aerated sludge storage basin (SSB1). The
aerator in the southern aerated sludge storage basin (SSB2) was replaced in
2019.
10. Replacement of sludge transfer pump, motor, and check valve.
11. Miscellaneous improvements to support facilities and control systems.

Solids handling: The two aerated sludge storage basins (SSB) total 4.9 acre -feet
(AF); the basins are asphalt and shotcrete -lined. The northern basin (SSB 1) is
4.3 AF and the southern basin (SSB 2) is 0.63 AF.
For SSB1, the plan view area at top of bank is 37, 430 square feet (sf). The working
sludge depth is 7 feet (maintain 2 -feet of freeboard in basin). The berm elevation is
420 feet msl and the invert elevation is 411 feet. SSB1 was expanded and lined in
2002. The liner consists of 12 -inches of clay with a 3 -inch asphalt floor . Side slopes
consist of 3 -inch shotcrete with an 8 -inch thickened shotcrete section extending 1 -
foot vertically above the asphalt -shotcrete joint. Prior to construction of the clay l iner,
the bottom 8 -inches of soil was scarified and re-compacted. The hydraulic
conductivity is unknown .
For SSB2, the plan view area at top of bank is 1 1,500 sf. The working sludge depth
is 5.5 feet (maintain 2 -feet of freeboard in basin). The berm elevation is 418.5 feet
msl and the invert elevation is 411 feet. SSB2 was lined in 2008. The liner consists
of a 60 -mil High Density Polyethylene ( HDPE ) geomembrane underlain by 16 -oz
geotextile. Prior to construction of the liner, the existing subgrade was rolled with a
smooth drum roller.
The storage of sludge in the SSBs allows solids to be stored over the winter months
and dried in the summer prior to disposal. Only one pond is dewatered each year;
the ponds each receive the equivalent of one year of sludge production prior to
dewatering.
The three sludge drying beds are concrete -lined and contain an underdrain system
that returns drainage from the drying beds to the downstream end of the headworks
via drainage pump station. Biosolids are hauled annually to the Anderson Landfill for
final disposal. The facility produces approximately 100 dry metric tons of dried
biosolids annually. Transportation and disposal/reuse of the biosolids is regulated by
U.S. EPA under 40 C.F.R. part 503.
Stormwater: The only stormwater specific infrastructure at the Facility is curbing
which keeps stormwater from being exposed to wastewater treatment equipment
and processes. In general, stormwater flows across the facility’s impervious surfaces
following the surface grade, which generally flows to the south.
Soils: A Soils Investigation Report (11 June 2002) was completed in 2002 prior to
the expansion of SSB1. Test pits in the area of investigation revealed a surface
layer of silty sandy clays to depths ranging from about 1 foot to approximately 2 feet.
The surface clays generally were found to be underlain by silty sands, gravelly silty
sands, and gravelly sands to the depths explored (approximately 10 feet).
