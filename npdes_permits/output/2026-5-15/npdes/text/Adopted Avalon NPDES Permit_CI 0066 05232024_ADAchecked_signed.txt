===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes
The Avalon WWTF’s chlorine contact basin deteriorated over the years and was in
danger of collapsing, creating a safety hazard to staff working at the Avalon WWTF
when doing routine cleaning and potentially causing water quality issues. For the
duration of the rehabilitation project for the chlorine contact basin (rehabilitation project),
the Discharger moved the sodium hypochlorite injection point from the chlorine contact
basin to the clarifier effluent common channel at the beginning of the bypass line and
collected routine grab samples 0.5 miles downstream of the Avalon WWTF at a street
manhole. The wastewater continued receiving the required chlorine contact time
because the chlorine contact basin provided approximately 25 minutes of contact time,
and the time it took the wastewater to travel to the manhole where grab samples were
collected was approximately 45 minutes. Wastewater was not dechlorinated prior to
discharge. The Discharger also collected composite samples for routine monitoring
along the entry point to the bypass line instead of at the end of the chlorine contact
basin. The bypass line was rated to convey up to 2.3 million gallons per day and the
daily average flow at the Avalon WWTF was approximately 0.5 MGD, so overloading of
the bypass line was not anticipated.
The rehabilitation project began on November 27, 2023, and was completed on January
10, 2024.
