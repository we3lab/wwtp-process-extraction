2.1.2. Following is a description of the treatment processes:
a. Primary Treatment: The primary treatment consists of screening, grit
removal, primary sedimentation and flow equalization. Ferrous chloride is
injected into the primary sedimentation sludge to control hydrogen sulfide
production and to aid in primary settling. Settled solids are removed from
the primary clarifier for biosolids processing.
b. Biological Nutrient Removal (BNR): Biological treatment consists of bio-
augmentation at a biologically activated reaeration (BAR) tank, feeding
into two parallel anoxic tanks and four parallel aeration tanks for full
nitrification and denitrification (NDN). Activated sludge and mixed liquor
recycling is utilized to balance load demands within the system.
c. Secondary and Tertiary Treatment: Mixed liquor is transferred to six
parallel rectangular secondary final sedimentation tanks for secondary
settling. Return activated sludge is collected from the clarifiers and
returned to the BAR process, while waste activated sludge is sent to the

primary settled sludge is sent directly to anaerobic digesters for
thickening. Waste activated sludge is removed from the secondary final
sedimentation tanks and thickened in the dissolved air flotation unit.
Thickened primary and secondary sludge is sent to three anaerobic
digesters operated in parallel for stabilization to achieve Class B biosolids.
Utilizing the methane gas produced by anaerobic digestion, a co-
generation system fuels an engine that keeps the digester heated through
a series of heat exchangers. Anaerobically digested sludge is dewatered
through one of two centrifuges to produce a cake of 18 to 23 percent
solids. Sludge cake is conveyed to tractor trailers and hauled off-site for
disposal. Centrate waste streams flow back to the BAR tank for treatment.
Centrate flows can be equalized by entering the filtrate equalization tank
prior to the BAR tank so that the BAR tank receives constant flow. All the
Class B anaerobically digested sludge is dewatered and sent to an offsite
contracted composting facility.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes
Plant upgrades are planned for the existing wastewater treatment plant in
addition to new facilities planned to protect the estuary ecology, develop
additional water supply sources to meet water demands for planned future
growth, and enhance supply reliability even in drought years.
1. Aeration, Filtration, and Chlorination Process Replacement with Membrane
Bioreactor and Ultraviolet Light Disinfection (MBR/UV)
New BNR tanks, MBR, and UV will replace the secondary, tertiary, and
chlorination systems currently used at the Ventura WRF. The new equipment
will replace aging, structurally deficient equipment; provide improved nutrient
and pathogen removal; and provide a safer disinfection process. Construction
is expected to be completed by 2030.
2. The Discharger is also planning for construction of an AWPF located near the
Ventura WRF on an undeveloped parcel on the corner of Harbor Boulevard
and Olivas Park Drive. Tertiary-treated wastewater from the Ventura WRF will
be diverted to the AWPF for further treatment for indirect potable reuse.
Construction of the AWPF is expected to be completed by the end of 2030.
