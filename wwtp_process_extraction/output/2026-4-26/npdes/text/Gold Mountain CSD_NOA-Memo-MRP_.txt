Gold Mtn. CSD

The Gold Mtn. CSD is responsible for the operation and maintenance of the wastewater treatment
system for the subdivision at Gold Mountain Ranch and the Grizzly Golf Course. The planned community
development was originally designed for 427 residential units, a golf course w/clubhouse and a small
commercial d istrict; with an estimated 100,000 Gallon per day (gpd) discharge. After 16 years of
operation, only 90 homes, a golf course w/ clubhouse, a hotel and recreation center have been
constructed at the facility.

The Discharger currently has an average daily discharge of 9 ,000 gpd, of domestic wastewater to a
septic tank effluent pump (STEP) system for wastewater collection. The community system consists of
individual septic tanks with pump chambers and submersible pumps discharging to a dosing tank and
then to one or both of two common leachfields, with a third leachfield proposed to be brought online in
the near future.

This is an existing facility; therefore enrollment under the General Order is categorically exempt from the
California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) pursuant to California Code of Regulations, title 14, section
15301 which applies to ongoing or existing projects

 I have reviewed the case file for Gold Mountain Community Services District (Gold Mtn.
CSD) and completed a site inspection on 22 August 2017 . The file documents and site
inspection confirms the general condition of the wastewater treatment system and
subsurface disposal fields . The Discharger has kept adequate maintenance documentation
and all treatment and collection infrastructure appears in good order.

 The average daily wastewater flow is less than 9 ,000 gallons per day . The treatment system
has a design capacity which is well above the discharge volume required by the existing
community .

Potential Threats to Water Quality

The wastewater treatment system is located southwe st of the main community of Portola.
The closest distance from the subsurface discharge fields (leachfields) to the nearest
property line is greater than 50 feet. The closest potable water well is greater t han 500 feet
from the leach fields . The closest surface water is greater than 500 feet from the leachfields .
A monitoring well network was installed at the site due to high groundwater, poor soil
conditions and its proximity to Middle Fork Feather River . Completion of the Nitrate Checklist
in Attachment 1 of Order 2014- 0153 -DWQ indicates the following flow and rationale:

A1 Exceed 20,000 gpd? No.
Wastewater flow generally less than 9 ,000 gpd.

Conclusion: No nitrogen removal is required.

Monitoring Requirements

 To protect water quality, General Order monitoring requirements will be sufficient. In
summary, Staff recommends quarterly reporting of the average daily flow rate; quarterly
leach field monitoring, quarterly groundwater monitoring and annual inspections of the septic
tank. Quarterly monitoring will be reported by the first day of the second month after the
quarter ends (e.g. January -March report is due by May 1st). Annual monitoring will be
included with the fourth quarter monitoring.
