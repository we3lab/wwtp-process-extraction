Wastewater Reclamation Plant and Discharge
4. The WRP consists of two mechanically aerated concrete-lined oxidation basins, two unlined
stabilization basins, and six evaporation/percolation ponds. Final disposal of treated
wastewater is by evaporation-percolation in the basins. The WRP has a reported design
treatment capacity of 150,000 gallons per day (gpd).
5. During the period of 1991 through 2011, average annual wastewater flows from the WRP
were 15.426 million gallons (MG). Annual wastewater discharges have declined steadily
from 26.819 MG in 1998 to 7.495 MG in 2011.
6. Disposal ponds are checked annually for sludge accumulation and dredged as necessary.
7. Back-up power is available by portable generators.
8. There are no significant industrial users discharging to the WRP’s collection system.
Hydrogeologic Conditions
9. Annual precipitation averages about three (3) inches. Annual evapotranspiration rate is
approximately 72 inches.

Bombay Beach WRP Waste Discharge Requirements
10. The Salton Sea is approximately one-half mile west of the WRP.
11. There are no domestic or municipal production wells within one mile of the WRP.
12. Water supply to the community has an average total dissolved solids (TDS) concentration
of approximately 150 mg/L. Water supply to the community is from production wells over
fifteen (15) miles away from the Bombay Beach community.
13. Historical depth to groundwater in the vicinity of the WRP has ranged from 17 feet below
ground surface in 1961 to artesian conditions in 1976. More current data is not available.
14. Soils in the area of the WRP consist of gravelly sand derived of gravelly alluvium from
granite.
15. Regional groundwater gradient in the area is from north to south toward the Salton Sea.
16. The WRP site is located in the seismically active Imperial Valley and is considered likely to
be subject to moderate to strong ground motion from earthquakes in the region.
