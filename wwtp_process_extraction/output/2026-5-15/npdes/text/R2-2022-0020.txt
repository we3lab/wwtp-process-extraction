2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment. The plant can provide secondary treatment for an
average daily dry weather design flow of 53.8 million gallons per day (MGD).
From May 2020 through April 2021, the average daily influent flow was
34.2 MGD. The wastewater treatment process consists of screening, grit
removal, primary clarification, secondary biological treatment, secondary
clarification, and ultraviolet (UV) disinfection. Attachment C shows a flow
process diagram for the Facility.
The Facility also has holding basins with a combined capacity of 168 million
gallons. The Discharger uses these holding basins to temporarily store peak
wet weather flows when primary-treated wastewater exceeds the secondary
treatment capacity. When flows subside, the Discharger routes stored
wastewater back to the headworks for full primary and secondary treatment.
2.1.4. Recycled Water. From 2017 through 2020, the Discharger annually diverted,
on average, approximately 5 percent of its total effluent, or 533 million gallons,
to its Recycling Plant. The Recycling Plant further treats the UV-disinfected
effluent with sand/anthracite filtration and sodium hypochlorite disinfection and
stores recycled water in a covered seven-million-gallon reservoir prior to
distribution. The Discharger uses about two-thirds of the recycled water
produced for washdown operations at the wastewater treatment plant and
distributes the remaining recycled water offsite, primarily for landscape
irrigation. Irrigation customers include schools, parks, corporation yards,
landscape medians, and commercial businesses. Light industrial uses include a

2.1.5. Sludge and Biosolids Management. The Discharger thickens secondary
sludge with a dissolved air flotation unit, combines secondary sludge with
primary sludge that it treats with lime, and dewaters the combined sludge with
centrifuges. The Discharger incinerates the final dewatered sludge onsite,
which produces ash. The Discharger hires a contractor to truck the ash to an
offsite recycling facility for use as a soil amendment. If Facility incinerators are
inoperable, the Discharger may haul biosolids to a landfill, an organic materials
recovery facility, or the East Bay Municipal Utility District wastewater treatment
plant.
2.1.6. Stormwater Management. The Discharger is not required to be covered under
the statewide NPDES permit for stormwater discharges associated with
industrial activities, General Permit for Storm Water Discharges Associated with
Industrial Activities, Order 2014-0057-DWQ (NPDES Permit
CAS000001),because all stormwater at the plant is collected and directed to the
plant’s headworks for treatment.
