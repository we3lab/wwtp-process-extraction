The City of Corona, Department of Water & Power (hereinafter Discharger) is the owner
and operator of the Water Reclamation Facility No. 1 (hereinafter Facility), a publicly owned
treatment works (POTW) with tertiary treatment processes. The City of Corona leases the
land from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers at 2205 Railroad Street, Corona, CA 92880 on
which the Facility is located.

Treated wastewater is discharged from Discharge Point (DP) 001 to the Butterfield Drain,
which is within the Prado Basin Management Zone (PBMZ). All surface water flows in the
PBMZ ultimately enter Reach 3 of the Santa Ana River. The Santa Ana River and other
surface streams within the PBMZ are waters of the United States. Secondary treated
wastewater is discharged at DP 002 to nearby percolation ponds located within or adjacent
to the PBMZ and an unlined section of Temescal Creek. Recycled water is delivered
through DP 003 for irrigation to use areas within the PBMZ and overlying the Bedford and
Temescal Groundwater Management Zones. Attachment B provides a map of the area
showing the location of the Facility, as well as the discharge points. Attachment C provides
a flow schematic of the Facility.
Discharges from the Facility are currently regulated by Order No. RS-2007-0005, which was
adopted on March 2, 2007. The Regional Water Board adopted Time Schedule Order No.
RS-2009-0039 on July 20, 2009, requiring the Discharger to upgrade the quality of the
discharge to the percolation ponds from secondary treatment to tertiary treatment.
Order No. RS-2007-0005 expired on March 1, 2012. However, the Discharger filed a report
of waste discharge and submitted an application for renewal of its Waste Discharge
Requirements and National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permit on
August 30, 2011. Since the Discharger filed a timely application for renewal of its permit,
the terms and conditions of Order No. RS-2007-0005 have been automatically continued
and remain in effect until new Waste Discharge Requirements, which also serve as an
NPDES permit, are adopted pursuant to this Order.

A. Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment or Controls
1 . Service Area
Water Reclamation Facility No. 1 treats wastewater flows from the western part of
the City of Corona, which is approximately 11,725 acres. The sewer area tributary to
the Facility consists of low to high density residential, commercial, office professional
and general and light industries. In 2011, the annual average wastewater flow to the
Facility was 10.2 MGD and the maximum daily flow was 15.4 MGD.
The Discharger also operates two additional water reclamation facilities, Water
Reclamation Facilities No. 2 and No. 3. If necessary, flows from Water Reclamation
Facility No. 2 can be diverted to Water Reclamation Facility No. 1. The water
reclamation facilities service a total population of 153,650.

2. Wastewater Design Characteristics and Treatment Capacity
Water Reclamation Facility No. 1 is located at 2205 Railroad Street, Corona. The
current design treatment capacity of the Facility is 11.5 MGD for secondary
treatment and 15.0 MGD for tertiary filtration. Chlorine2 is used for disinfection.
The treatment system includes the headworks, two secondary treatment plants
(Plant 1A, Plant 1 B), tertiary filters, and a chlorine contact basin. The headworks
consist of two channel grinders and a grit removal system that are designed for 14.5
MGD treatment capacity. A flow splitter separates up to 5.5 MGD of the effluent
from the headworks to Plant 1A, while the remainder of the flow is sent to Plant 1B .
The design capacity of Plant 1A is 5.5 MGD. Treatment processes consists of two
rectangular primary sedimentation units; an activated sludge process with nutrient
removal capabilities operated in a serpentine step-feed mode (3 aeration basins),
and secondary clarification provided by 6 rectangular units. Both the primary and
secondary clarifier units are equipped for sludge recovery and conveyance to onsite
biosolids handling facilities.
The design capacity for Plant 1B is 6.0 MGD. Secondary treatment consists of two
parallel oxidation ditches with separate oxic and anoxic zones, and two circular
secondary clarifier units with sludge recovery.
Secondary treated wastewater from Plants 1A and 1 B is discharged to a flow
equalization basin. Water from the equalization basin can be pumped directly to
three percolation ponds, located one mile east of the plant, or to the tertiary filters.
One of the percolation ponds is located along Lincoln Avenue and the other two
ponds are located at the end of Rincon Street near Cota Street.
The tertiary treatment train consists of coagulation, continuous backwash sand
filtration, and chlorination with sodium hypochlorite in two serpentine chlorine contact
tanks followed by dechlorination with sodium bisulfate.
On March 15, 2010, the Discharger requested the California Department of Public
Health (CDPH) to re-rate the tertiary treatment capacity from its original approved
capacity of 9 MGD to its design capacity of 15 MGD. Regional Water Board staff
has evaluated the Dischargers' request and considers the tertiary treatment train re
rating to be rationale. However, the total plant treatment capacity is controlled by
the secondary treatment process of 11.5 MGD.

3. Biosolids Handling Practices
Primary sludge and waste activated sludge from Plant 1A is thickened with a gravity
belt thickener prior to being sent to one of three anaerobic digesters. Digested
sludge is dewatered with belt presses. Waste activated sludge from Plant 1B is
pumped to the two belt presses or the gravity belt thickeners prior to being sent to
the anaerobic digesters. All belt press filtrate is reintroduced to the oxidation ditches
and sludge cake is diverted to a thermal dryer or is transported to an approved
composting facility. Water Reclamation Facility No. 1 currently receives sludge from
Water Reclamation Facility No. 2.
4. Recycled Water Storage Tank
Disinfected tertiary water flows to an on-site four million gallon recycled water
storage tank. Water can be pumped from the storage tank to recycled water users
or to the ponds.
5. On-site Storm Water Runoff
Stormwater runoff from the Facility is contained within the plant property. All
stormwater from the Facility is directed to one of two catch basins and allowed to
percolate/evaporate.
