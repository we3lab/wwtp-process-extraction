6. The original sewer system was constructed out of vitrified clay pipe (VCP). Since the
1980s, the sewer system has been expanded and repaired with polyvinyl chloride
pipe (PVC) with rubber ring joints. The system consists of approximately 100,000
feet of collection sewers and 15,000 feet of main interceptor sewers. The collection
sewers range from 4-10 inches in diameter, and the sewer interceptors range from
12-15 inches in diameter.

Existing Facility and Discharge
7. The Facility consists of headworks, one oxidation ditch, one secondary clarifier, eight
unlined evaporation/percolation ponds, a high-density polyethylene (HDPE) lined
facultative sludge lagoon, and an unlined overflow percolation pond.
8. Wastewater enters the Main Lift Station through a 15-inch interceptor sewer, passes
through a Parshall flume and then a comminutor before it is pumped through an 8-
inch force main, to the treatment plant. The comminutor downstream of the Parshall
flume causes backup and results in inaccurate flow measurements; therefore,
influent flows are currently measured via wet well levels. The Main Lift Station
consists of three pumps. The effective capacity (largest pump out of service) of the
lift station is approximately 1,325 gallons per minute (gpm).
9. At the treatment plant wastewater passes through a hydrosieve static screen and
distribution box. The wastewater flows from the screen by gravity to the oxidation
ditch. The racetrack-shaped oxidation ditch is equipped with one brush aerator and
three floating aerators. Effluent from the oxidation ditch flows by gravity to a 35-foot
diameter secondary clarifier. Clarifier effluent gravity flows to evaporation/percolation
Ponds 1-7 which are typically operated in series. Return activated sludge (RAS) is
pumped back to the oxidation ditch, and clarifier scum is pumped back to the
hydrosieve.
10. Waste Activated Sludge (WAS) is pumped to an HDPE lined facultative sludge
lagoon. The pond was constructed and lined in 1989. Sludge lagoon supernatant is
sent to unlined Pond 8. Pond 8 has seepage at the base of its berm, and therefore a
French drain was installed which routes collected seepage to the unlined overflow
percolation pond. Pond capacities are summarized in the table below.

11. From 2014-2016, due to excess buildup of solids in the sludge lagoon, sludge was
removed each summer and placed in available evaporation/percolation ponds for
drying. The dried solids were hauled to a landfill by October 15 each year.
12. Four sludge drying beds were originally installed to dry WAS from the secondary
clarifier. However, these are no longer utilized due to inadequate drying area and too
short of a drying season.
13. The Facility was originally designed for an average dry weather flow (ADFW) of 0.44
mgd and a peak wet weather flow (PWWF) of 1.02 mgd. The current ADFW is
estimated to at 0.21 mgd based on summer flows (July-September) from 2014 and
2015.

15. There is currently no standby power or emergency storage at the treatment plant.
During prolonged power outages or equipment failures and/or repairs, the treatment
plant is bypassed and untreated or partially treated sewage is sent to the
evaporation/percolation ponds. The discharger plans to include the purchase and
installation of a permanent generator and manual transfer switch at the WWTP as
part of an upcoming capital improvement project (target installation date 2022).
16. In addition to domestic wastewater, WDRs Order 94-017 allows the Discharger to
accept industrial wastewater from Burney Mountain Power Cogeneration Facility,
leachate from Intermountain Landfill and the Burney Transfer Station, and log deck
recycle pond water from Burney Forest Products and Sierra Pacific Industries
Burney (SPI- Burney).
a. In the past the WWTP has received cooling tower blowdown, boiler
blowdown, and plant wash-down water from Burney Mountain Power
Cogeneration Facility through a metered, hard-piped connection into Pond 7.
Burney Mountain Power Cogeneration Facility is no longer in operation
(since 2011), and it is uncertain whether this facility will resume operations in
the future. However, Burney Mountain Power Cogeneration Facility still has a
WDR permit (Order 95-098) that allows them to discharge 0.050 mgd
(ADWF) to Burney Wastewater Treatment Plant.

b. Burney Forest Products, which is regulated by National Pollutant Discharge
Elimination System (NPDES) permit WDR R5-2014-0035, has a history of
capacity issues during wet weather. If the power plant pond or log deck
recycle pond reach capacity, wastewater can be trucked off-site to the
Burney WWTP for disposal into Pond 7. Burney Forest Products has not
discharged to the WWTP since 2008; however, they had an unauthorized
discharge to a creek in December 2014.
c. In the past the WWTP has received log deck recycle water from SPI-Burney.
SPI-Burney has modified their operations and no longer discharges to the
WWTP.
d. Historically the WWTP has received leachate from Intermountain Landfill and
the Burney Transfer Station into the HDPE-lined sludge lagoon. However,
Intermountain Landfill now has a leachate storage tank and hasn’t hauled
leachate to the WWTP since at least before 2000 (according to Geotracker),
and Burney Transfer Station no longer disposes of leachate at the WWTP.
