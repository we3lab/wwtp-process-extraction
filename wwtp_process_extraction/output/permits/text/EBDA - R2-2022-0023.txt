2. FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and Treatment
2.1.1. Locations and Service Areas. EBDA is comprised of five member agencies:
the City of Hayward, Oro Loma Sanitary District, Castro Valley Sanitary District,
City of San Leandro, and Union Sanitary District. The member agencies convey
treated wastewater to the Marina Dechlorination Facility, located at 14150
Monarch Bay Drive in San Leandro. LAVWMA and its member agencies, the
Dublin San Ramon Services District, the City of Livermore, and the City of
Pleasanton, also convey wastewater to the Marina Dechlorination Facility.
Attachment B shows a map of the EBDA and LAVWMA member agency
facilities.
2.1.1.1. City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility. The City of Hayward
Water Pollution Control Facility is located at 3700 Enterprise Avenue in
Hayward. It provides secondary treatment of domestic, commercial, and
industrial wastewater for the City of Hayward, serving a population of about
160,000.
2.1.1.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant. The City of San
Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant is located at 3000 Davis Street in San
Leandro. It provides secondary treatment of domestic, commercial, and
industrial wastewater for the northern portion of the City of San Leandro,
serving a population of about 60,000.

2.1.1.3. Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control
Plant. The Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts jointly own a
wastewater treatment plant located at 2600 Grant Avenue in San Lorenzo. It
provides secondary treatment of domestic, commercial, and industrial

2.1.1.4. Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant. The
Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant is located at
5072 Benson Road. It provides secondary treatment of domestic,
commercial, and industrial wastewater for the cities of Fremont, Newark, and
Union City, serving a population of about 356,800.
2.1.1.5. LAVWMA Export and Storage Facilities. The LAVWMA Export and
Storage Facilities begin at 7176 Johnson Drive in Pleasanton and extend
west to San Leandro. They receive, export, and store secondary treated
domestic, commercial, and industrial wastewater from the cities of Dublin,
Livermore, and Pleasanton, serving a population of about 320,600. LAVWMA
wastewater also contains reverse osmosis reject water from the Zone 7
Water Agency that is mixed with the Dublin San Ramon Services District’s
final effluent. This reject water results from the Zone 7 Water Agency’s
Demineralization and Brine Disposal Project, which removes salts from
groundwater in the Zone 7 Water Agency’s jurisdiction.
2.1.2. Collection Systems
2.1.2.1. City of Hayward. The City of Hayward collection system conveys
wastewater to the City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility. It is a
separate sewer system comprised of approximately 325 miles of
publicly-owned pipes and 9 lift stations.
2.1.2.2. City of San Leandro. The City of San Leandro collection system conveys
wastewater to the City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant. It is a
separate sewer system comprised of approximately 130 miles of
publicly-owned pipes and 13 lift stations.
2.1.2.3. Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts. The Oro Loma Sanitary
District collection system conveys wastewater to the Oro Loma and Castro
Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control Plant. It is a separate sewer
system comprised of approximately 272 miles of publicly-owned pipes and
14 lift stations. The Castro Valley Sanitary District collection system also
conveys wastewater to the Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts
Water Pollution Control Plant. It is a separate sewer system comprised of
approximately 148 miles of publicly-owned pipes and 9 lift stations.

2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment. Attachment C provides treatment schematics for each
treatment plant.
2.1.3.1. City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility. The City of Hayward
Water Pollution Control Facility can provide secondary treatment for an
average daily dry weather design flow of 18.5 MGD. In 2021, the average
daily dry weather influent flow was 10.0 MGD. The treatment process
consists of comminution, grit removal, primary sedimentation, flow
equalization, trickling filtration, activated sludge aeration, secondary
clarification, and chlorine disinfection. The plant also has 240 acres of out-of-
service oxidation ponds that can be used for emergency storage (e.g., during
peak wet weather).
2.1.3.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant. The City of San
Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant can provide secondary treatment for
an average daily dry weather design flow of 7.6 MGD. In 2021, the average
daily dry weather influent flow was 4.3 MGD. The treatment process consists
of screening, grit removal, primary sedimentation, flow equalization, trickling
filtration, activated sludge aeration, secondary clarification, and chlorine
disinfection. The plant also has five acres of out-of-service oxidation ponds
that can be used for emergency storage.
2.1.3.3. Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution Control
Plant. The Oro Loma and Castro Valley Sanitary Districts Water Pollution
Control Plant can provide secondary treatment for an average daily dry
weather design flow of 20.0 MGD. In 2021, the average daily dry weather
influent flow was 11.2 MGD. The treatment process consists of screening,
grit removal, primary sedimentation, modified Ludzack-Ettigner nitrification
and denitrification, secondary clarification, and sodium hypochlorite
disinfection.
2.1.3.4. Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant. The
Raymond A. Boege Alvarado Wastewater Treatment Plant can provide
secondary treatment for an average daily dry weather design flow of
33.0 MGD. In 2021, the average daily dry weather influent flow was
22.0 MGD. The treatment process consists of screening, primary
sedimentation, activated sludge aeration, secondary clarification, and
chlorine disinfection. During this Order term, the Discharger plans to

2.1.4. Sludge and Biosolids Management
2.1.4.1. City of Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility. Sludge at the City of
Hayward Water Pollution Control Facility is anaerobically digested,
dewatered in dewatering beds, and air dried for up to two years. The City
hauls dried biosolids to an authorized disposal site for use as alternative daily
cover.
2.1.4.2. City of San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant. Sludge at the City of
San Leandro Water Pollution Control Plant is anaerobically digested,
dewatered using a belt filter press, and air dried. The City hauls dried
biosolids to an authorized disposal site or applies the biosolids to land as
Class A Exceptional Quality biosolids or Class B biosolids (as defined in
40 C.F.R. part 503) depending on available bed space.
