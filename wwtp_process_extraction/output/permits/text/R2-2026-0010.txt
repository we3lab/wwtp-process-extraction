2. FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Sanitary Sewer System and Treatment
2.1.1. Location and Service Area. The plant is located at 195 Belle Air Road in the
City of South San Francisco. It provides secondary treatment of domestic,
commercial, and industrial wastewater for the cities of South San Francisco and
San Bruno, the Town of Colma, and portions of the City of Daly City. The plants
serve a population of approximately 109,350.
Attachment B provides a map of the area around the treatment plant.
2.1.2. Sanitary Sewer System. The City of South San Francisco sanitary sewer
system and City of San Bruno sanitary sewer system are covered by this order
and convey wastewater to the plant. The South San Francisco sanitary sewer
system consists of approximately 148 miles of gravity sewer pipes and force
mains and 7 pump stations. The San Bruno sanitary sewer system consists of
approximately 85 miles of sewer pipes and force mains and 6 pump stations.
The Town of Colma (6.3 miles of sewer pipes and force main) and City of Daly
City operate satellite collection systems that transmit wastewater to the South
San Francisco collection system.

2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment. The plant receives untreated wastewater from three
force main pump stations. The influent flows through bar screens and two grit
removal systems. During normal dry weather operations, it is treated with ferric
chloride and polymer for flocculation and pumped to a flow splitter for
distribution to primary clarifiers, where grease, floating solids, and settable
solids are removed. Polymer is used during wet weather flows for Chemically
Enhanced Primary Treatment (CEPT). Four stand-by wet weather storage
basins are available for use during increased wet weather flows.
The primary-treated wastewater typically flows by gravity to five aeration basins
as two separate flow streams. Another four aeration basins remain in stand-by
mode. They are scheduled for retrofitting and mainly used for wet weather
flows. The wastewater is combined in the aeration basins with return activated
sludge from secondary clarifiers. It then flows by gravity through the aeration
basins to secondary clarifiers for sedimentation and floating solids removal.
During wet weather, when influent flow greatly exceeds the peak secondary
treatment capacity (40 MGD design capacity), primary effluent may be routed
around the aeration basins and secondary clarifiers and blended with
secondary-treated effluent. These bypasses are subject to Discharge
Prohibition 3.2 of this Order and Attachment D section 1.7.
The secondary-treated wastewater flows by gravity to two chlorine contact
basins where it is mixed with sodium hypochlorite for disinfection. After
disinfection, it flows by gravity to the NBSU effluent pumping station wet well,
where it is blended with chlorinated effluent from the cities of Burlingame and
Millbrae and the San Francisco International Airport. It is then mixed with
sodium bisulfite to neutralize the residual chlorine. The combined effluent is
then pumped through the NBSU common outfall (Discharge Point 002) and
discharged to Lower San Francisco Bay about one mile offshore from Oyster
Point.

2.1.4. Sludge and Biosolids Management. Sludge from the primary and secondary
clarification operations is processed using anaerobic digestion. The process
consists of gravity and mechanical thickeners or waste activated sludge
thickening, mesophilic anaerobic digesters, and belt filter presses for
dewatering. The digested and dewatered biosolids are hauled offsite to a
permitted regional reuse site, where thermal hydrolysis is deployed to produce
biofertilizer.
2.1.5. Stormwater Management. The Discharger captures all stormwater at the plant
and directs it to the plant headworks for treatment. Therefore, no additional
stormwater requirements are necessary.
