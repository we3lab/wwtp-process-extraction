2. FACILITY DESCRIPTION
2.1. Wastewater Collection and Treatment
2.1.1. Location and Service Area. The wastewater treatment plant is located at 1400
Radio Road in Redwood City. It provides advanced secondary treatment of
domestic, commercial, and industrial wastewater for the cities of Belmont, San
Carlos, Redwood City, and Woodside; and for the service area of the West Bay
Sanitary District, which collects wastewater from the cities of Menlo Park,
Atherton, and Portola Valley, and areas of East Palo Alto, Woodside, and
unincorporated San Mateo and Santa Clara counties. The Facility serves a
population of about 220,000 people. Attachment B provides a map and aerial
photo of the area around the treatment plant.
2.1.2 Collection System. The Discharger’s wastewater collection system is part of
the Facility covered by this Order. It consists of approximately nine miles of
force main and four pump stations, which receive wastewater from four satellite
wastewater collection systems (West Bay Sanitary District, City of Belmont, City
of San Carlos, and City of Redwood City). Wastewater travels by gravity to the
four pump stations and then by force main to the treatment plant. During wet
weather, the plant’s booster station and influent lift station may be used to
handle elevated force main flows. An earth and rip-rap equalization basin
owned by the West Bay Sanitary District may be used to reduce force main
flows. After the Discharger completes its upgrades, it will have two pump
stations, Menlo Park and Redwood City; the Belmont and San Carlos pump
stations will no longer operate.
2.1.3. Wastewater Treatment. The wastewater treatment process consists of
preliminary screening with mechanical bar screens, primary sedimentation,
secondary biological treatment with fixed film reactors and aeration basins,
secondary clarification, mono- or dual-media filtration (the Discharger
occasionally routes flows around these filters when influent flows are high),
chlorine disinfection with sodium hypochlorite, and dechlorination using sodium
bisulfite. Coagulant may be added to the dual media filter flow for recycled
water production. A new headworks planned for late 2023. When placed into
operation, the influent will go through new bar screens and a new grit removal
system prior to flowing through to the existing treatment system, including the
existing mechanical bar screens.
The treatment plant has an average daily treatment capacity of up to 29 million
gallons per day (MGD) and a peak wet weather capacity of 71 MGD. From April
2018 through September 2022, the plant discharged 13.6 MGD and a single
day maximum average of 41.2 MGD. Attachment C provides the treatment plant
flow schematic.

drying beds are conveyed back to the treatment plant and combined with
primary influent. Up to about 40 percent of the solids collected downstream
from the fan press units is sent to an onsite biosolids drying process involving
pyrolysis to create biochar used for beneficial reuse.
2.1.5. Stormwater Management. All stormwater flows in contact with equipment or
wastewater at the treatment plant are collected and directed to the plant
headworks for treatment. Therefore, no additional stormwater requirements are
necessary.

===PLANNED CHANGES===

Planned Changes. The Discharger plans a series of plant improvements through
its 980-million dollar Regional Environmental Sewer Conveyance Upgrade
Program. The follow projects are expected to be completed during this permit
term:
· New headworks, including new influent pump station, odor control, screening,
and grit removal;
· Primary sedimentation tank channel coating and hatch replacement;
· Generator feed relocation and panel replacement to provide flexibility and
reliability to emergency generator system;
· Biochar system start-up to allow the Discharger to convert over 40 percent of
its biosolids into biochar;
· Fixed film reactor refurbishment and media replacement; and
· Anaerobic digester refurbishing and replacement of all digester sludge and gas
piping, valves, and all instrumentation.
