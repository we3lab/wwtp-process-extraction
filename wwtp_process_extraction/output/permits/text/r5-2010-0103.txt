II. FACILITY DESCRIPTION
The Discharger provides sewerage service for the community of Lake California, which
serves a population of approximately 1,600. The design average dry weather flow capacity
of the Facility is 0.64 million gallons per day (mgd). The Facility provides advanced
secondary treatment of the wastewater.
A. Description of Wastewater and Biosolids Treatment or Controls
Wastewater enters the Facility through a Palmer Bowlus flume equipped with an
ultrasonic flow meter. The wastewater then flows through a headworks structure
equipped with a grinder and a bar screen. From the headworks, the wastewater flows
to an aeration basin equipped with two aerators. The effluent from the aeration basin
flows to a secondary clarifier and then to a secondary effluent holding pond. Secondary
effluent from the holding pond is pumped to a chlorine contact chamber where the
wastewater is disinfected with sodium hypochlorite and dechlorinated with sodium
bisulfite. The secondary effluent can be filtered, as needed to meet effluent limits,
through multimedia pressure filters. In 2009, the average dry weather flow was 0.12
mgd. Each of the unit processes treats all of the flow that enters the Facility on a given
day.
The Facility processes sludge using four concrete lined drying beds and one asphalt
lined drying bed. Sludge is disposed of offsite at an approved landfill. The Facility has
two on-site standby generators for emergency backup.
